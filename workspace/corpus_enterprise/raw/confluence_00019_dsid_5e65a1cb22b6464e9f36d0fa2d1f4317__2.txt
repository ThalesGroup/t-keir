Postmortem: SEV-2 Dedicated autoscaler stuck at min capacity (2026-02-09)

The Dedicated autoscaler stopped scaling above min due to a metrics query change that returned empty series for a subset of clusters.
Two Dedicated customers in us-west experienced sustained queueing and timeouts. p99 latency exceeded 6s for ~70 minutes; timeout rate peaked at 3.2%.
13:22 UTC: Support escalated customer reports of timeouts on Dedicated pools (us-west).
13:28 UTC: SRE on-call confirmed queue depth rising and no scale-out events.
13:40 UTC: Identified autoscalermetrics query change deployed earlier that day.
14:05 UTC: Hotfix applied to autoscaler query; scale-out resumed.
14:20 UTC: Queue depth normalized; timeouts subsided.
14:47 UTC: Incident resolved.
A refactor in the autoscalerPromQL query switched label matching from cluster_id to cluster. Some clusters emitted cluster_id only, causing the query to return no data and preventing scale decisions.
Primary detection was via customer escalation; internal alerting on scale-out suppression was missing.
Rolled forward a hotfix restoring label compatibility and added a fallback query when no series are returned.
Once identified, the fix was small and low risk. Capacity was available to scale immediately.
Insufficient alerting on autoscaler making no decisions; canary did not include clusters with legacy labels.
Add alert: autoscaler decision loop sees empty metrics for >5 minutes (SRE; due 2026-02-25).
Add integration test covering both cluster and cluster_id labels (Infrastructure; due 2026-03-05).
Update Dedicated release checklist to require canary across at least one legacy-labeled cluster (Infrastructure; due 2026-02-22).
