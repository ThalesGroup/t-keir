Access & Authorization Lifecycle, Vendor Risk Scoring, and Travel Procedures

A single, operational playbook that defines the life cycle for employee and system access, a vendor risk scoring framework tied to procurement thresholds, integrated change management checklist for systems handling regulated data, and travel approval matrices plus reusable templates for requests and reviews.
Overview:

This playbook consolidates access & permission lifecycle steps, vendor risk scoring, procurement/expense thresholds, data-management controls for changes, and travel policy exceptions into a single operational reference. It is intended for requesters, approvers, finance, security, and procurement partners. The goal is consistent decisions, auditable trails, and predictable timing for approvals.

Key Principles:
- Principle of least privilege applied to all identities (human and machine).
- Risk-based vendor decisions: higher risk requires higher review/contract controls.
- Data classification drives change management rigor and approval chain.
- Single source of truth for templates to reduce rework and audit friction.

Sections:
1) Access & Authorization Lifecycle
- Owners: IAM team (process), Requesting team (business justification), Security (policy guardrails), People-ops (identity lifecycle for employees).
- Types of access: Human (SSO + role), Machine-to-machine (service accounts with short-lived keys), External collaborator.
- Request flow (standard):
  1. Requester files Access Request via Internal Portal (template: ACCESS_REQUEST_V1).
  2. Requester provides: business justification, data classification of resources requested, scope (read/write/admin), and expected duration.
  3. Manager approves (within 3 business days). If access touches PII/regulated data, Security review required (additional 5 business days).
  4. IAM implements grant and documents ticket with grant metadata (grantor, expiry, scope).
  5. Review cadence: quarterly access recertification for roles with sensitive-data privileges.
- Temporary access: timeboxed (default 14 days), auto-expire and require extension via portal with manager re-approval.
- Emergency access (break-glass):
  - Trigger: documented incident or urgent business need.
  - Approvers: on-call Security lead + manager.
  - Post-mortem: emergency grants must be logged and reviewed within 5 business days; follow-up remediation required.

2) Change Management (systems & data)
- Scope: All planned changes to production systems, infra, or data schema. Changes that impact classified data must follow the enhanced path.
- Change categories and required approvals:
  - Low-risk (non-prod config, UI text): team owner approval.
  - Medium-risk (infra scaling, dependency updates): tech lead + SRE review.
  - High-risk (schema changes, model updates, access-control changes affecting PII): tech lead + SRE + Security + Product + Compliance if relevant.
- Change window guidance: preferred maintenance windows 02:00–06:00 UTC. Any deviation requires business justification and a rollback plan.
- Required artifacts for change ticket:
  - Summary, owner, rollback plan, test plan, monitoring & SLOs impacted, data classification, post-deploy verification checklist.
- Post-deploy verification: automated smoke tests + manual verification sign-off within 24 hours.

3) Data Management & Classification Mapping
- Classification levels: PUBLIC, INTERNAL, CONFIDENTIAL, RESTRICTED.
- Control mapping (brief):
  - PUBLIC: no special controls; standard backups.
  - INTERNAL: RBAC enforced; logging enabled.
  - CONFIDENTIAL: encryption at rest + in transit; limited access groups; quarterly access recertification.
  - RESTRICTED: highest controls: HSM/KMS integration, mandatory contract clauses for vendors, annual audit.
- Data handling checklist (for owners):
  1. Identify classification and tag data store.
  2. Record retention policy and encryption baselines.
  3. Confirm backups and redaction/PII minimization.
  4. Include classification in change tickets and access requests.

4) Vendor Risk Scoring and Procurement Thresholds
- Purpose: Align procurement approval rigor to vendor risk score.
- Scoring factors (numeric 0–100):
  - Data sensitivity exposure (0–30)
  - Operational criticality (0–25)
  - Regulatory footprint (0–25)
  - Security posture (external scan & attestations) (0–20)
- Risk bands and procurement path:
  - Low (0–29): standard PO, Finance approval only.
  - Moderate (30–59): Legal review (standard T&Cs + DP addendum), Security self-attestation required, Finance + Procurement approval.
  - High (60–84): Legal + Security review, SOC2/ISO evidence or compensating controls, VP-level signoff (Finance & Business).
  - Critical (85–100): Executive procurement committee review, mandatory security integration test, full contract negotiation and annual audit clause.
- Example scoring matrix (text table):
  | Factor | Weight | Example score | Notes |
  |---|---:|---:|---|
  | Data sensitivity | 30 | 24 | Stores CONFIDENTIAL data |
  | Operational criticality | 25 | 20 | Customer-facing inference endpoint |
  | Regulatory footprint | 25 | 15 | GDPR-only impact |
  | Security posture | 20 | 12 | Pen-test w/ findings remediated |
  Total = 71 => High risk => proceed with High path.
- Procurement thresholds (quick reference):
  - <$25k: Finance approval.
  - $25k–$250k: Procurement + Finance.
  - >$250k: Procurement + Finance + Legal (risk band-dependent).

5) Expense & Travel Approval Matrix
- Travel approval tiers:
  - Tier 1 (Domestic, <$1,000): Manager pre-approval via travel portal.
  - Tier 2 (International or $1,000–$4,999): Manager + Finance pre-approval; corporate card recommended if available.
  - Tier 3 (International with sensitive customers, >= $5,000): Manager + Finance + Security sign-off for customer visits; legal review if customer NDA required.
- Allowed expense types and required proof:
  - Flights: itinerary + boarding pass.
  - Lodging: itemized hotel folio.
  - Meals: itemized receipts; no alcohol for client entertainment without VP approval.
  - Ground transport: receipt or mileage log (personal car).
- Travel exception (customer-sensitive visit): Security will perform a short privacy-impact assessment and approve visitor device controls. Expect 5 business days for review.

6) Templates and Reusable Artifacts
- ACCESS_REQUEST_V1 (fields): requester, manager, resource, scope, justification, expiration date, data classification, change ticket id if applicable.
- VENDOR_RISK_CALC_V1: vendor name, scope, data exposure, operational criticality, regulatory footprint, security evidence, computed score, recommended approval path.
- CHANGE_TICKET_TEMPLATE: summary, owner, change category, rollback plan, test plan, monitoring, data classification, verification checklist.
- TRAVEL_REQUEST_V1: traveler, dates, purpose, cost estimate, approvals required, customer-sensitivity flag.

7) Roles & Ownership Summary
- Requester: completes templates and provides business justification.
- Manager: validates business need and approves timeboxed access/expense.
- IAM: executes access grants and maintains logs.
- Security: reviews risks for classified data, emergency grants, and vendor security evidence.
- Procurement & Finance: enforce vendor scoring paths and spend thresholds.
- Legal: contract review for moderate+ risk vendors and sensitive travel NDAs.

8) SLAs and Audit Requirements
- Access requests: standard path 3 business days, security review adds 5 business days.
- Vendor onboarding: low risk 7 business days; high/critical risk escrowed to negotiation timelines.
- Change reviews for high-risk changes: minimum 10 business days for cross-functional review.
- Audit logs retention: 24 months for access grants affecting CONFIDENTIAL/RESTRICTED data.

9) Escalation & Exceptions
- Exceptions must be documented via EXCEPTION_REQUEST_V1 template and have a named approver and expiry. All exceptions touching RESTRICTED data require VP-level authorization.
- Disputes on vendor scores escalate to Procurement Director + Security Director; emergency vendor procurements can be approved by the CFO with retroactive committee review.
