Permissions and Change Orchestration Handbook

Overview

This handbook consolidates company-wide policies and operational processes for permissions & access, change management, data governance, procurement and expenses, vendor management, and travel. It is intended as the canonical reference for managers, approvers, and operational teams responsible for day-to-day controls and exceptions.

Key principles
- Least privilege: default to minimum access necessary for job functions.
- Separation of duties: no single person should have unchecked access to approve, provision, and audit the same critical control.
- Auditability: every change, approval, and procurement must be traceable to a requestor, approver, and ticket.
- Risk-based controls: apply stricter verification for high-impact assets and vendors.
- Automation first: use identity providers (Okta/SCIM), IaC, and procurement tooling to reduce manual error.

Scope
This handbook covers the following topics across the company, including Hosted, Dedicated, and Private product teams, and shared services:
- Access & permissions (cloud, internal apps, data stores, production systems)
- Change management (infra and software, normal/standard/emergency)
- Data management (classification, retention, encryption)
- Procurement & expenses (hardware, cloud spend, third-party SaaS)
- Vendor management (security reviews, contract SLAs)
- Travel policies and expense submission templates

Roles & ownership (high level)
- Requestor: individual who needs access, change, or purchase.
- Approver: manager or owner defined for the resource. Approvers must be explicitly listed in the Approval Matrix.
- Provisioner: SRE/IT/Platform engineer or automated system that performs the work.
- Control owner: designated team owning the asset or process (e.g., Eng-SRE for infra, Finance for procurement).
- Audit owner: Security/Compliance team responsible for quarterly reviews.

Access & Permissions
1) Policy summary
- All system access must be requested via the Access Request portal (Confluence link + Okta ticket).
- Role-based groups are the only supported method for granting long-term access to services. Direct user grants to production systems are temporary and expire after 7 days unless extended with justification.
- MFA is required for all accounts with access to production or billing consoles.

2) Request workflow (standard request)
1. Requestor files an access request ticket in the Access portal with resource, role, justification, and duration.
2. Manager (line manager) approval required.
3. Resource owner approval required for sensitive resources (see classification table below).
4. Automation executes (SCIM group membership change or IAM role grant) and ticket is closed with audit logs.

3) Approval matrix (examples)
- Non-sensitive internal tools: Manager approval.
- Production application logs and dashboards: Manager + Control owner.
- Production host shell / root access: Manager + SRE approval + VP sign-off for >30-day duration.

4) Periodic review
- Teams run access review every 90 days. Security will run an automated cross-check every 30 days to identify stale memberships and flag anomalies.

5) Naming and group hygiene
- Group names: team-service-role (e.g., infra-redis-admin).
- Avoid granting wildcard groups across environments.

6) Automation examples
- SCIM provisioning via Okta: examples/okta-scim-sync.yml (internal repo).
- CLI sample to query memberships:
  - aws iam get-group --group-name infra-redis-admin

Change Management
1) Change types
- Standard/Pre-approved: low-risk changes documented and approved by process owners; require a scheduled window but not CAB for each occurrence.
- Normal: changes that affect production but require CAB review and scheduled deployment.
- Emergency: immediate fixes to restore service; must be followed by a post-implementation review.

2) CAB (Change Advisory Board) guidelines
- Membership: SRE lead, Product owner, Security representative, and an independent Engineering reviewer.
- Cadence: daily triage for high-volume windows, weekly formal CAB for planned rollouts.
- Thresholds for CAB: any change affecting >10% user traffic, PII scope changes, infra/hardware changes to production clusters.

3) Change request template (required fields)
- Change ID, owner, risk level, impact, rollback plan, deployment steps, monitoring checklist, post-change validation criteria, scheduled window, and approvers.

4) Rollout and rollback checklist
- Pre-runbook verification: smoke tests, canary plan, alerts and runbooks attached.
- During rollout: incrementally increase traffic, monitor latency/error budget, pause/back out if key metrics breach thresholds.
- Rollback: execute steps in runbook within approved time window and file an incident if rollback deviates.

5) Emergency change postmortem
- Must be created within 72 hours and include timeline, decision rationale, and preventive actions.

Data Management
1) Classification
- Public: safe for external publication.
- Internal: company-internal non-sensitive.
- Confidential: business-sensitive (contracts, financials).
- Restricted/PII: regulated or personal data requiring encryption at rest and in transit, strict access controls, and retention limits.

2) Handling rules
- Encrypt Restricted and Confidential data at rest (AES-256) and enforce TLS1.2+ in transit.
- Never copy Restricted data to developer laptops without explicit exception and MDM.
- Use anonymized or synthetic data for development and testing whenever possible.

3) Retention & deletion
- Logs (standard observability): 90 days hot, 365 days archived.
- Financial records: 7 years.
- Customer contracts: retention per legal requirements; default 10 years unless otherwise specified.

4) Backups & DR
- Critical production data: RTO 4 hours, RPO 6 hours.
- Backup verification: weekly restore test of a subset and quarterly full restore for critical services.

Procurement & Expenses
1) Approval thresholds (examples)
- <$2,500: Requestor's manager can approve.
- $2,500–$50,000: Department head approval + Finance review.
- >$50,000: VP approval + Legal contract review and Finance sign-off.
- Cloud provider commitment (Reserved/Dedicated): requires Finance + Product leadership approval regardless of amount.

2) Procurement process (step-by-step)
1. Requestor fills Procurement Request form with business justification, estimated cost, budget code.
2. Department approver reviews and approves.
3. Finance reviews budget and payment terms.
4. Legal reviews contracts if vendor or spend >$10,000 or data will be shared.
5. Purchase order (PO) created and vendor on boarded in vendor registry.

3) Expense submission standards
- All travel and expense receipts must be uploaded within 30 days.
- Per diem and mileage chart in Appendix A.
- Cardholder reconciliation monthly; missed reconciliations escalate to manager and People Ops.

Vendor Management
1) Onboarding checklist
- Vendor questionnaire completed (security and privacy).
- Reference and financial checks for new strategic vendors.
- Contract terms: data residency, breach notification, indemnity clauses, and SLA definitions.
- Security controls: SOC2 or equivalent for vendors handling Restricted/PII.

2) SLA & performance monitoring
- Define measurable SLAs: uptime %, mean time to respond (MTTR), escalation contacts.
- Quarterly business reviews for strategic vendors (> $100k ARR).

3) Offboarding
- Revoke all access using the vendor access registry.
- Confirm data deletion or return per contract terms.
- Archive contract and close PO.

Travel Policy (summary)
- Booking: employees should use company travel tool and select economy or business class only per policy thresholds.
- Approval: manager approval required for all trips. International travel requires Security review for devices and People Ops travel checklist.
- Expenses: clear allowable items (meals, lodging, transport) and disallowed items (alcohol beyond defined per-diem unless business-hosted).
- Safety & incident reporting: any travel security incident must be reported to People Ops and Security within 24 hours.

Enforcement, Exceptions & Escalations
- Non-compliance: repeated failure to follow processes may result in access removal, expense reimbursement denial, or HR escalation.
- Exception requests: must be submitted to Security or Finance (depending on control) with explicit compensating controls and an expiration. Exceptions are valid for a maximum of 90 days unless renewed with stronger justification.

Audit & Continuous Improvement
- Quarterly internal audits by Security/Compliance focusing on access reviews, vendor security questionnaires, and procurement approvals.
- Annual table-top exercises to validate change management in high-impact scenarios.
- Roadmap for automation: target to reduce manual approvals by 40% through policy-as-code and tighter SCIM/Okta integration by Q4 2026.
