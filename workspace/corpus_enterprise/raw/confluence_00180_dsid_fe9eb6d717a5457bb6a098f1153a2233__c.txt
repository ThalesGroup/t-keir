Cobalt bridge operator manual and escalation FAQ

Summary:
This page documents the 'Cobalt' bridge variant: a pre-authorized, lightweight incident bridge pattern for short-duration multi-team escalations (SRE + Customer Success + Platform). Cobalt is intended for events where rapid coordination and a single accountable bridge operator are required, but a full executive bridge is not yet warranted.

Goals:
- Reduce time-to-first-action for customer-impacting spikes by 40% compared to ad-hoc DMs.
- Provide a minimal, repeatable operator checklist that works across multi-tenant model-serving and dedicated capacity incidents.
- Standardize customer-facing status templates for low-latency public updates.

When to open a Cobalt bridge (criteria):
1) Any incident where a single bridge operator plus SRE and CS must coordinate live for >15 minutes and <3 hours.
2) Latency SLO breach affecting >=5% of active requests for 10 minutes.
3) Repeated 503 errors on generation endpoints with no clear single-service root cause.
4) Customer-visible errors reported by multiple unrelated tenants.

When NOT to open a Cobalt bridge:
- Regulatory/legal-impacting incidents (open a formal executive bridge).
- Long-running outages expected >3 hours (escalate to full incident command).
- Incidents confined to a single repo-level rollout where the owning team can manage via their team runbook.

Operator playbook (step-by-step):
1) Acknowledge and claim the Cobalt bridge in PagerDuty; set bridge channel to #cobalt-bridge-<region> and create the temporary bridge channel.
2) Triage initial impact: run `cobalt-scan` (internal CLI) to collect top signals: request-rate, 99th/95th latency, CPU/GPU utilization. Example: `cobalt-scan --service=serving-runtime --window=10m`
3) Post initial customer-facing status (see templates below) within 10 minutes of bridge open.
4) Convene SRE + CS + Platform in the bridge channel and assign roles: operator (single), comms, evidence collector, remediation lead.
5) Execute fast mitigations in order (see ordered checklist below). Track each action in the incident timeline.
6) If mitigations fail within 30 minutes and incident severity increases, escalate to a full Incident Command (see Escalation Matrix).
7) When service is stabilized, run the Cobalt closure checklist and schedule a 24–72 hour follow-up: short incident review (not a full postmortem unless required).

Fast mitigation ordered checklist (apply top-to-bottom until impact reduces):
- Step 1: Apply circuit-breaker routing to reduce noisy tenancy: `route-weight set --service=frontdoor --tenant=NOISY_TENANT --weight=0.0`
- Step 2: Temporarily pin affected requests to a lower-cost variant with higher concurrency limits.
- Step 3: Scale up Dedicated pool by +20% using the autoscale override API.
- Step 4: Enable KV-cache fallback for common prefixes (reduce model decode work).
- Step 5: Throttle non-critical background jobs that consume KV/cache bandwidth.

Escalation matrix (severity thresholds and handoffs):
| Severity | Trigger window | Initial responder | Escalation target |
|---|---:|---|---|
| P3 (cobalt) | 10–30m customer impact <10% | Bridge operator + SRE | Keep as Cobalt; optional EM notify |
| P2 (cobalt->ic) | >30m or impact >=10% | Bridge operator + SRE + CS | Incident Commander (IC) -> full bridge |
| P1 (exec) | region-wide outage, high business impact | IC + Exec comms | Exec bridge, on-call leadership, legal as required |

Note: escalation must be enacted automatically if metrics cross thresholds; the bridge operator declares escalation and tags PagerDuty with `escalation:automatic` when threshold is exceeded.

Roles and responsibilities (one-liners):
- Bridge operator: single point of coordination, owns the bridge lifecycle.
- Remediation lead: drives technical fixes and rollbacks.
- Comms lead: crafts public and customer notices using templates below.
- Evidence collector: captures logs, traces, and KV/cache snapshot for post-incident analysis.

Evidence collection checklist (minimum items to attach to incident):
- Timeline (UTC) of actions and observed metrics (10m resolution).
- `serving-runtime` traces for 5 example failed requests (include request_id).
- Hotspot profile: GPU/CPU per-host over the incident window (top 10 hosts).
- KV cache hit-rate and eviction logs.
- Snapshot of routing table and weight overrides.

Status page & external customer templates (use verbatim, fill placeholders):
- Initial public status (within 10m):
"We are experiencing elevated latency on model inference in <region>. Our team is actively investigating. No customer data loss is expected. Updates every 15 minutes."

- Follow-up (when mitigation in progress):
"We have applied targeted routing and capacity measures to reduce impact. Some customers may see improved latency. We will provide another update at <hh:mm UTC>."

- Resolution message:
"The service is restored in <region>. Root cause analysis will be published to affected customers within 72 hours. If you continue to see issues, please open a ticket with request_id=<REQUEST_ID>."

Customer-specific email snippet (short):
"Hi <Customer Name>,
We detected elevated latency affecting your requests originating from <region>. Our on-call team opened a Cobalt bridge and applied routing mitigations; you should see improvements. We will follow up with a root cause summary within 72 hours.
Regards,
Redwood Support"

Game day checklist (30–60 minute exercise to validate Cobalt flows):
- Pre-game: pick a low-risk service and schedule a 60m slot; notify observers.
- Inject: small, targeted latency spike via controlled canary traffic to the chosen service.
- Operator: open Cobalt bridge, run `cobalt-scan`, post initial status.
- Roles: verify SRE/CS/Platform assignment and comms cadence.
- Exercise automation: confirm `route-weight set` and autoscale override runbook succeed.
- Evidence: collect logs and validate evidence collector template.
- Retro: 30m retro using the template at the end; publish findings.

Handoff checklist (end of shift or operator transfer):
1) Update timeline and outstanding actions.
2) Confirm next check-in times and escalate points.
3) Transfer ownership in PagerDuty and Slack bridge with `@handoff` message including summary.
4) If remaining risk exists, schedule a 1-hour watch with a named owner.

Automations and tooling (quick reference):
- `cobalt-scan` — collects telemetry and writes a YAML summary to `/tmp/cobalt-<timestamp>.yml`.
- `route-weight` — routing control utility (see `route-weight --help`).
- `status-publish` — posts to status page using the JSON payload format above.
- `evidence-collector` — wrapper that bundles logs/traces and uploads to secure incident vault.

Runbook retention and post-incident follow-up policy:
- Cobalt incidents that lasted >30 minutes or resulted in customer-visible errors require a short incident review (template linked).
- If the review surfaces a systemic risk (rollout bug, scaling misconfiguration), escalate to a full postmortem and include affected model versions and rollout IDs.
- All Cobalt evidence bundles must be retained for 180 days and classified internal.

FAQ (quick answers):
Q: Can we use Cobalt for a planned maintenance window?
A: No — use maintenance channels; Cobalt is for unplanned rapid coordination.

Q: Who authorizes a Cobalt bridge closure?
A: The bridge operator closes the bridge after remediation lead confirms stable metrics for 10 consecutive minutes and comms lead has posted an external status.

Q: How do we decide between Cobalt and a full Incident Command?
A: Follow the Escalation Matrix thresholds. If impact is trending upward or customer SLA exposure is high, escalate.
