comp-and-bands-review-playbook-and-merit-process-2026

Summary
This playbook describes Redwood Inference’s annual compensation band review and merit cycle, plus the operational steps managers and people-ops must follow for offer approvals, pay adjustments, and pay-equity checks. It combines timeline, approval matrix, calibration guidance, communication templates, and escalation paths. Purpose: ensure fair, defensible, and timely compensation decisions that align with performance, market data, and budget constraints.

Scope
Applies to all full-time and permanent part-time employees in North America and Europe for Redwood-hosted payroll. Contractors and third-party temps follow separate vendor-pay guidelines (see vendor-finance). Covers base salary, spot bonuses, title changes tied to band movement, and merit increases. Does not cover equity refreshes or promotion bonus programs (handled by Total Rewards special programs).

Principles
- Market-driven: use up-to-date benchmark data (Mercer/Levels.fyi/internal comp bands).
- Pay-equity first: ensure adjustments reduce documented gaps by role, gender, and region before discretionary over-target increases.
- Manager accountability: managers own calibration preparation and written justification for out-of-band decisions.
- Transparency: employees receive clear rationale and timing for changes; non-confidential summaries published for org-level trends.

Definitions
- Band: pay band range for a role/level (e.g., ENG-3: $140k–$200k USD).
- Target midpoint: market-aligned midpoint used for benchmarking.
- Merit pool: team-level budget allocated for salary increases (percentage of payroll).
- Out-of-band increase: raise that would move base outside the role band or above the established band cap. Requires escalated approvals.

Annual Merit Cycle Overview (timeline)
1) Planning (Q4 prior fiscal year): People-ops publishes budget guidance and merit pool % targets to Heads of Org. Example: company target merit pool = 6% of base payroll for FY26; teams may be allocated between 3%–9% depending on performance and hiring posture.
2) Calibration & Manager Prep (Jan): Managers prepare draft increases, linking to performance reviews and calibration docs. People-ops schedules calibration windows by department.
3) Executive Calibration (Feb): Cross-org calibration meetings with Director+ to review above-target requests and out-of-band moves.
4) Final Approvals (early Mar): Finance and People-ops finalize budgets and sign off.
5) Payroll Cutover (mid Mar): HR inputs finalized changes to payroll provider; effective date is first full pay period in April.
6) Employee Communications (same week as cutover): Managers deliver 1:1 messages with pay letters and FAQs.

Manager Checklist (before submitting increases)
- Confirm employee's band and current comp vs midpoint and market comp.
- Attach performance summary: top 3 accomplishments, evidence of impact, and rating.
- Show proposed increase, new base, and % of merit pool used.
- For out-of-band requests, include business rationale, retention risk analysis, and suggested alternate mitigations (bonus, promotion timeline).
- Check for potential pay-equity regressions in your team (people-ops will run automated checks but managers should flag anomalies).

Calibration & Decision Rules
- Standard increases: up to 6% for most employees (guided by band position and performance rating).
- High-performer increases: 6%–12% with documented impact and peer calibration support.
- Promotions with role/level change: typically include base increase of 10%–20% depending on band movement; must be accompanied by new role description and promotion packet.
- Out-of-band (above band max): CTO or People Ops VP approval required + Director-level justification.
- If requested increase exceeds available merit pool, managers must prioritize based on retention risk and business criticality and provide rebalancing plan.

Approval Matrix (who signs what)
- Manager (team level): sign-off on proposed increases and file documentation.
- Director: approves standard increases and promotions within team budget.
- People-ops Lead: approves calibration exceptions up to $X total additional spend per month (internal threshold).
- VP People Ops + Finance: signs off on out-of-band moves, comp compression resolutions > 10% gap, and region-level policy changes.

Offer & Hiring Exceptions (fast-track rules)
- Standard offers: within band and sign-on threshold require Manager + Recruiter approval.
- Above-band offers <= 10% above band max: Director + People-ops approval (expedites available for time-sensitive hires).
- Critical hires or counteroffers that exceed 10% above band max: require VP People Ops + Finance and an approved business case.
- Emergency retention increases: documented retention risk, attempted interventions (bonus, non-monetary), and a 6-month follow-up plan.

Pay Transparency Guidance (manager-facing)
- Public information: high-level band ranges and compensation philosophy are published internally.
- Private information: individual salary numbers are confidential and should not be disclosed by managers except to payroll, finance, or people-ops staff with legitimate business need.
- Manager talking points for 1:1s: focus on performance impact, development plan, and timing. If asked directly about peer salaries, defer to People-ops and offer to explain band structure instead.

Template: Manager 1:1 Pay Conversation
1) Affirm contribution and context: "I appreciate your work on X; because of the impact on Y, we've recommended..."
2) Explain change succinctly: "Your new base will be $XXX, effective April 1. This reflects performance/market/promotions."
3) Next steps: "We'll update payroll and you’ll see the increase on your next paycheck; here's the development plan that supports continued growth."
4) If asked about colleagues: use band-level and philosophy language; do not disclose numbers.

Escalations and Appeals
- Employee questions escalate to manager -> People-ops rep -> HRBP -> VP People-ops. People-ops maintains an appeals log.
- Appeals must be submitted within 30 days of communication and should include the employee’s rationale and supporting evidence.
- People-ops will respond with a timeline for review (typical response: 10 business days).

Operational Runbook: Submitting a Request
1) Manager completes the Compensation Worksheet (People-ops -> Templates -> comp-worksheet.xlsx).
2) Attach performance summary and promotion packet (if relevant).
3) Submit via People-ops compensation portal by the calibration deadline.
4) People-ops runs automated equity and compression checks and returns an exception flag report.
5) If exception flagged, manager addresses or escalates with added justification.

Data & Reporting Requirements
- Every increase must be recorded in the compensation ledger with: employee ID, current/base salary, new salary, effective date, approver names, and justification.
- People-ops publishes anonymized quarterly compensation health dashboards (labels: median-to-midpoint ratio, gender pay gap by level, out-of-band %).

Common Scenarios & Guidance
- Compression (junior > senior): file a compression mitigation plan; prioritize critical cases for immediate adjustment.
- Cross-region adjustments: use regional cost-of-labor multiplier table; consult People-ops for exchange-rate locked effective dates.
- Promotion denied after performance rating improved: provide written developmental plan and reassessment timeline (usually next 6 months).

FAQs
Q: When will I see my increase?
A: Effective date aligns with payroll cutover (first full pay period in April).
Q: Can managers give raises outside the merit cycle?
A: Spot increases are rare — limited to retention or market-correction situations and require documented exception.
