Upgrade rehearsal and failover circuit-breaker playbook

Summary
This playbook defines a repeatable, low-risk process for rehearsing major Private deployment upgrades and for exercising the automated failover / circuit-breaker layers that protect customer SLAs. It covers rehearsal cadence, scenario templates, instrumentation requirements, rollback triggers, and a sample circuit-breaker policy with concrete thresholds. The goal is to reduce surprise failures during live upgrades and to ensure evidence for forensic analysis is captured automatically.

Scope
- Applies to Private (VPC and on-prem) deployments across all supported architectures.
- Focus on control-plane upgrades, runtime binary/model swaps, and KV cache/stateful migrations.
- Excludes purely client-side SDK changes. For multi-tenant Hosted/Dedicated upgrades see the separate hosted upgrade playbooks.

Goals and SLOs
- Validate upgrade plan end-to-end in a staging environment that mirrors production network topology and telemetry hooks.
- Ensure automated circuit-breakers engage before customer-visible SLA impact exceeds defined limits.
- Maintain <5% error-rate delta and <25% median latency increase during a canary rehearsal for 10 minutes.
- Preserve forensic evidence for any exercised rollback (minimum: chronological logs, artifact digests, snapshot manifest).

Roles and ownership
- Upgrade Lead (owner): coordinates rehearsals, runbook gating, and customer notifications. Typical owner: release-engineering.
- Operator on-call: executes operator playbook actions during rehearsal and assesses abort/rollforward decisions.
- SRE/Telemetry: ensures traces/metrics/logs are collected and stored in the evidence bucket.
- Security/Compliance: verifies signatures and snapshot attestation where required.

Prerequisites
1) Inventory: list target clusters, tenant mappings, model variants, and control-plane endpoints.
2) Backups & snapshots: confirm last successful snapshot <24h, and that snapshot retention policy is met.
3) Telemetry hooks: traces, request-level telemetry, token-level cost logging, request IDs.
4) Circuit-breaker policies deployed and tested in dry-run mode.
5) Cross-team scheduling: at least 48h lead time and stakeholder ack.

Rehearsal types
- Canary Rehearsal: small percentage (1–5%) of synthetic or shadow traffic routed to upgraded path. Quick validation of correctness and perf.
- Fault-injection Rehearsal: induce controlled resource faults (CPU spike, GPU OOM, network blackhole) to validate degradations and failover.
- Full Swap Dry-run: perform model binary swap on staging with full KV migrations, no customer traffic, verify snapshot rehydrate.
- Multi-tenant Evacuation Rehearsal: simulate tenant migration/evacuation and verify state consistency.

Example rehearsal scenarios (prioritized)
1) Model quantized-swap + prefix cache migration under synthetic 95th percentile token load.
2) Control-plane API schema bump with canary route and automatic fallback to older schema handler.
3) Device preemption: simulate node-level GPU preempt and validate warm-start fallback.
4) Telemetry partition: drop logs to aggregator for 5 minutes and verify on-disk circular buffer retention and evidence export.

Step-by-step rehearsal runbook
1) Preflight (T-72h to T-2h)
   - Publish rehearsal window and owners.
   - Snapshot artifacts: image digests, model checksums, migration plans.
   - Validate staging mirrors: topologies, VM types, kernel versions, and container runtime.
   - Confirm test dataset and traffic generator with deterministic seeds.
2) Dry-run deployment (T-2h)
   - Deploy upgrade to staging control-plane; do not route production traffic.
   - Run the smoke-test suite (end-to-end inference, quota checks, auth paths).
   - Validate observability: dashboards update, synthetic traces appear, log retention policy applied.
3) Canary (T)
   - Shift 1%–5% of traffic or replay synthetic workload to upgraded nodes.
   - Monitor SLOs and circuit-breaker metrics for 10–30 minutes.
   - Owner accepts if acceptance criteria met.
4) Fault injection (optional; parallel)
   - Execute one fault scenario for 5–15 minutes.
   - Confirm circuit-breaker engages and traffic is routed to fallback.
5) Rollback/rollforward decision point
   - Abort and rollback if any of the failure conditions are met (see Rollback triggers).
   - When safe, rollforward with gradual ramp and increased telemetry sampling.
6) Postmortem and evidence preservation
   - Archive artifact manifests, logs, and traces to the evidence bucket.
   - Run the post-rehearsal checklist and produce a 1-page summary for stakeholders.

Rollback triggers (explicit thresholds)
- Error rate: sustained increase >5 percentage points above baseline for 5 minutes.
- Latency: 95th percentile latency > baseline * 1.8 for 5 minutes.
- Throughput: backpressure causing queue growth > 50% of buffer for 3 consecutive intervals (60s windows).
- Data integrity: any inconsistency detected between snapshot manifests and rehydrated state.
- Security: failed signature verification of artifacts or missing attestations.

Circuit-breaker design and example policy
Design principles\- Prefer fast local circuit-breakers at the node/proxy level for immediate mitigation; use global circuit-breakers at the control-plane to coordinate routing decisions.
- Circuit-breakers should act conservatively and favor preserving correctness and data integrity over latency improvements.
- Policies must be parametric with a dry-run mode to validate without impacting traffic.

Operational thresholds (suggested)
+----------------------+-------------------------------+
| Metric               | Trigger                       |
+----------------------+-------------------------------+
| Node error-rate      | > 12% sustained for 60s       |
| Route error-rate     | > 8% sustained for 120s       |
| Node GPU OOM count   | >= 2 OOMs within 10 minutes   |
| KV-consistency fail  | any detected mismatch         |
+----------------------+-------------------------------+

Example circuit-breaker policy (YAML snippet)
---
cb_policy_version: v1
name: regional-fallback-cb
scope: region
triggers:
  - metric: route_error_rate
    window_seconds: 120
    threshold_pct: 8
    action: divert_to_fallback
  - metric: node_gpu_oom_count
    window_seconds: 600
    threshold_count: 2
    action: cordon_node_and_drain
  - metric: kv_reconciliation_errors
    window_seconds: 300
    threshold_count: 1
    action: pause_model_refresh
failback_policy:
  grace_period_seconds: 300
  validation_checks:
    - smoke_tests: true
    - telemetry_lag_seconds: < 30
---

Circuit-breaker playbook steps (when CB fires)
1) Immediate notification to on-call and upgrade lead.
2) Automated action (divert to fallback model or route) executed by routing service.
3) Operator performs quick triage: check recent errors, OOM traces, and last artifact hashes.
4) If condition is transient, increase sampling and monitor for 15 minutes; otherwise progress to rollback/evacuation.

Rollback and forensic evidence requirements
- When rollback is executed, the system must capture and store the following artifacts in a tamper-evident evidence bucket:
  - Artifact digests (images, model weights) and attestations.
  - Last successful snapshots and rehydration manifests.
  - Complete logs (control-plane, node-level, and operator commands) for T-30m before trigger and T+30m after.
  - Traces for affected requests with request IDs.
- Evidence retention: retain rehearsal evidence for 90 days for internal audits; extend to 1 year for regulated customers per contract.

Acceptance criteria for a successful rehearsal
- All rehearsed scenarios completed without hitting rollback triggers, or they hit triggers and circuit-breaker behaved per policy and evidence was captured.
- Runbook acknowledgments: Upgrade Lead and Operator sign-off in the runbook journal.
- Post-rehearsal report: includes metrics table, timeline of events, root-cause hypothesis for any anomalies, and action items.

Templates and example commands
- Snapshot command (example):
  rinfctl snapshot create --cluster tenant-prod-eu --note "pre-upgrade-2026-06-15" --output manifest.json
- Evidence export (example):
  rinfctl evidence export --manifest manifest.json --bucket s3://redwood-evidence/private-rehearsals/2026-06-15
- Dry-run CB validation:
  rinfctl cb validate --policy regional-fallback-cb.yaml --dry-run

Post-rehearsal checklist (minimum)
1) Evidence export successful and checksum-verified.
2) Acceptance criteria documented and stored.
3) Action items created with owners and deadlines.
4) Dashboards returned to baseline sampling and retention.
5) Customer-impact review completed if any degradations occurred.

Appendix A — Example artifacts to attach to postmortem
- manifest.json (artifact digests)
- cb-policy.yaml (the exact policy file used)
- timeline.log (annotated sequence of operator actions)
- perf-metrics.csv (raw telemetry for the rehearsal window)

Appendix B — Quick reference: decision matrix
- If error-rate > rollback threshold AND CB failed to divert => immediate rollback and forensic snapshot.
- If CB diverted successfully and service remained within customer SLA => escalate incident for RCA but continue rollforward when safe.
