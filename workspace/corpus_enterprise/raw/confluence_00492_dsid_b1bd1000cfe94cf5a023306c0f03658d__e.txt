Enterprise Observability Contract Harmonization Playbook

Overview
This playbook defines a repeatable process for harmonizing observability contracts across enterprise customer telemetry sources during onboarding. Harmonization reduces integration friction, ensures consistent SLO/SLA mapping, and shortens time-to-QBR readiness. Use this guide when integrating any new Dedicated or Private customer with custom telemetry, third-party agents, or legacy instrumentation.

Goals
- Provide a minimal required signal set for Redwood feature areas (ingress, request lifecycle, model inference, billing tokens, and error taxonomy).
- Define canonical metric/event names and recommended labels/tags.
- Specify retention, sampling, and privacy controls for regulated customers.
- Prescribe validation checks, ownership, and escalation points.

Scope
Applies to: Dedicated and Private customers onboarding into Redwood's monitoring and customer-facing SLO/Uptime reports. Not intended for ad-hoc product experiments.

Owners and stakeholders
- Customer Success (CS) — primary onboarding coordinator, responsible for acceptance criteria.
- Observability (Platform) — implements collectors, forwarders, and ensures schema conformity.
- SRE/Infra — verifies SLO mappings and alert routing.
- Customer Engineering — integrates customer telemetry; point-of-contact for schema questions.

Required signals (canonical contract)
The table below lists the minimum signals required to support production monitoring and QBR metrics. Provide all signals as either metrics (time-series) or structured events with the specified fields. If a customer cannot emit a field, mark the exception in the onboarding ticket.

| Signal | Type | Required fields / labels | Purpose | Acceptable fallback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ingress.requests_total | metric (counter) | tenant_id, route, region, ingestion_type | Throughput and quota accounting | aggregated by tenant only (if per-route not available)
| request.latency_p50/95/99 | metric (histogram) | tenant_id, route, model_variant, sequence_length_bucket | Latency SLOs and perf trending | p95 only for initial acceptance
| inference.tokens_generated | metric (counter) | tenant_id, model_variant, prompt_hash | Inference economics, cost attribution | per-tenant token counts
| response.error | event | tenant_id, request_id, error_code, error_category | Error triage and classification | aggregated error rate by tenant
| kvcache.hits / kvcache.misses | metric | tenant_id, model_variant, prefix_cache_key | Cache efficiency and cost reduction | sample logs with cache-hit boolean
| billing.request_cost_usd | metric | tenant_id, billing_cycle, cost_type | Chargeback & QBR cost breakdown | estimated cost model from Redwood side
| telemetry.pii_exposure_alert | event | tenant_id, request_id, detection_ruleset_id | Data privacy monitoring | periodic sampling with redaction

Privacy and regulated accounts
For customers with data residency/regulation constraints (HIPAA, GDPR, FINRA):
- Never forward raw request/response text. Use hashed prompt IDs (prompt_hash) and token counts.
- Provide a privacy filter sidecar pattern: upstream collector masks or removes fields labeled pii_sensitive before forwarding.
- Retention: default 30 days for raw structured events, 365 days for aggregated metrics. For regulated customers, support options: 7/30/90-day retention tables.
- Document the KMS/HSM key ids used for redaction and provide evidence of encryption-at-rest and in-transit.

Sampling and cardinality guidance
- Low-cardinality tags: tenant_id, region, model_variant, route. Avoid user_id or free-text prompt content.
- For high-cardinality fields (prompt_hash) use bucketing: keep only first 8 characters of hash and limit distinct values per tenant to 10k/day.
- Sampling strategy: 100% for p95/p99 latency and error counters; 1–5% for raw example traces (for debugging) unless customer permits more.

Onboarding acceptance criteria (checklist)
1. Schema alignment: Customer telemetry emits all required signals with matching field names or mapped via an agreed adapter.
2. Ingestion validation: Observability platform ingests at least 7 days of representative traffic or synthetic replay.
3. SLO mapping: Latency and availability SLOs mapped to tenant dashboards.
4. Privacy gates: PII redaction and retention windows verified.
5. Cost mapping: Token & inference cost metrics available and reconciled with billing estimate.
6. Alerting: Alert rules for latent p95, elevated error_rate (>0.5% sustained 10m), and telemetry pipeline dropouts.
7. QBR readiness: Data populated for the last 30 days for the QBR template (see QBR readiness checklist).

Detailed onboarding procedure
Step 0 — Pre-engagement\- CS collects: customer account team, legal constraints, expected qps, list of models and regions in scope.\- Create an onboarding ticket in JIRA with "observability-contract" template and assign Observability engineer.

Step 1 — Schema discovery (2–3 days)\1. Run a short discovery call with Customer Engineering to list existing telemetry endpoints and formats (Prometheus, OTLP, Datadog, custom logs).\2. Ask for sample payloads (anonymized) and a list of current tag sets.\3. Produce a mapping document within 48 hours with fields either matched or flagged for adapter implementation.

Step 2 — Adapter & transport (1–2 weeks)
- If the customer uses a homegrown format, Observability team builds an adapter (Fluent Bit/Collector pipeline) to translate to canonical names. Document the adapter config as YAML in the ticket.
- Configure TLS and API key exchange. For Private deployments, confirm VPC peering or TLS proxy endpoints.

Step 3 — Validation (3–7 days)
- Run synthetic traffic that covers: high throughput, long sequences (>1024 tokens), and failure modes (timebox exceed, model OOM).
- Verify metrics: counters increase, histograms report expected buckets, events contain required fields.
- Execute the validation queries below and record results in the ticket.

Validation queries (examples)\- p95 latency by tenant: SELECT percentile(latency_ms, 95) FROM request_latencies WHERE tenant_id = '<TENANT>' AND time > now() - 1h\- error rate: SELECT sum(errors) / sum(requests) FROM (SELECT count_if(response.error = true) as errors, count(*) as requests FROM requests WHERE tenant_id = '<TENANT>' AND time > now() - 10m)

Step 4 — SLO mapping, alerts and runbooks (2–5 days)
- SRE maps the onboarded metrics to Redwood SLOs and creates runbook stubs. Example SLOs: request availability >= 99.95% monthly; p95 latency < 300ms for short sequence routes.
- Create alerts: (1) p95 latency increase > 30% over baseline for 10 minutes; (2) sustained error_rate > 0.5% for 10 minutes; (3) telemetry ingestion missing for 2 minutes.

Step 5 — Handback and QBR prep (1–2 days)
- CS verifies that all QBR metrics populate correctly for the prior 30 days.
- Produce an "Observability Acceptance" sign-off artifact with: mapping doc, adapter config, retention policy, SLOs, and runbooks.

Rollback and remediation steps
- If validation fails, revert the adapter config and pause ingestion for the tenant while preserving raw payloads for investigation (no raw text forwarded for regulated accounts).
- Escalation: Observability -> SRE -> CS -> Legal for PII incidents. Use the major-incident template in escalation-playbook.

Common integration patterns and sample configs
1) Prometheus scrape adapter (yaml):
- job_name: "redwood-tenant"
  static_configs:
    - targets: ['collector.local:9100']
  relabel_configs:
    - source_labels: [__meta_kubernetes_namespace]
      target_label: tenant_id

2) OTLP to Redwood-forwarder (cli example):
- otelcol --config /etc/otel/config.yaml --exporter.redwood.endpoint=https://telemetry.redwood.ai:443 --auth.token=<REDACTED>

3) Minimal JSON event example (privacy-preserving):
{
  "event_type": "response.error",
  "tenant_id": "acct-2b7f",
  "request_id": "r-9f1a",
  "error_code": "MODEL_TIMEOUT",
  "error_category": "timeout",
  "timestamp": "2026-02-15T14:03:22Z"
}

QBR readiness checklist (for CS owners)
- 30 days of latency, error, and cost metrics available
- Dashboards pre-populated and linked in the QBR template
- Cost attribution validated against billing estimates 
- Two example incident summaries (if any) with remediation notes
- Privacy and retention policy documented and agreed

Templates and artifacts to attach to the onboarding ticket
- telemetry-mapping.csv (field mappings)
- adapter-config.yaml (collector/exporter config)
- acceptance-signoff.md (one-pager with signatures from CS and Customer Engineering)
- slo-definition.json (SLOs and objectives)
