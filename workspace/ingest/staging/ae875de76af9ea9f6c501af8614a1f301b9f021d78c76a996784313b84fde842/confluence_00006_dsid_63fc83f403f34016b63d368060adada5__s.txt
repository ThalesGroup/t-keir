Overview
========

This dossier is a compact, cross-functional playbook for activating and scaling a Redwood inference pilot with enterprise prospects. It combines concise positioning for each stakeholder (CISO, CTO, Platform/Infra, Product, Finance), a competitive snapshot you can share during discovery, scripts for short elevator and objection handling, sample pricing scenarios with transparent math, and a targeted security FAQ for prospects evaluating embedded or private deployments. Use this as the single-page advocacy brief in workshops, executive previews, and technical pilots.

Executive summary
-----------------

- Goal: Convert a 6–12 week pilot into an enterprise deployment by aligning evidence across security, platform, product, and finance stakeholders.
- Outcome metrics to prove: end-to-end P99 latency under target, cost per 1,000 tokens vs. incumbent, error rate <0.1% for production routes, and successful audit of data residency/KMS controls.
- Suggested pilot scope: 2 production routes (low latency + high throughput), up to 500k tokens/day, SLA-backed dedicated capacity for peak test windows.

Who to engage and why
---------------------

1) CISO / Head of Security
- Primary concerns: data residency, encryption-at-rest/in-transit, key management, audit logs, and supply-chain risk.
- One-line pitch: "Redwood Private gives you a customer-managed control plane with KMS/HSM integrations and full audit trails so security decisions remain in your hands."

2) CTO / VP Engineering
- Primary concerns: operational predictability, deployability, compatibility with infra, rollback safety.
- One-line pitch: "Same API and rollout primitives across hosted, dedicated, and private modes — minimize app changes while gaining predictable inference economics."

3) Platform / SRE
- Primary concerns: autoscaling behavior, observability, SLOs, tracing hooks, and cost controls.
- One-line pitch: "Per-route dashboards, token-level cost attribution, and configurable autoscale/burst policies so platform teams can enforce economics without developer changes."

4) Product / ML Engineers
- Primary concerns: quality regressions, model versioning, latency, and prompt experiments.
- One-line pitch: "Model pinning, canary rollouts, and integrated evaluation hooks guard quality while you iterate on prompts and model variants."

5) Finance / Procurement
- Primary concerns: TCO, commitment flexibility, pilot-to-production migration costs, and auditability of usage.
- One-line pitch: "Transparent per-token economics, optional reserved capacity for predictable unit costs, and migration credits for pilots that convert."

Tailored messaging snippets (short scripts)
------------------------------------------

- 30s CISO elevator: "For customers that cannot have vendor-managed keys or need region isolation, Redwood Private runs with a customer control plane and integrates with your KMS/HSM. We provide an audit log export and a prescriptive SOC2/ISO evidence package for pilots."

- 45s CTO elevator: "You can swap your model endpoint to Redwood with minimal SDK changes and benefit from deterministic batching and fallback policies. During pilot we'll instrument one critical route to measure production SLOs and cost delta."

Objection handling (concise scripts)
-----------------------------------

- Objection: "We can't trust vendor-managed data flows."
  - Script: "Understood — we recommend Redwood Private with VPC-only egress, customer-managed KMS, and an initial SOC2 evidence review. We can provide an engineering runbook and a 2-week on‑site/remote security validation window."

- Objection: "This will be too expensive compared to our internal GPUs."
  - Script: "We’ll run an apples-to-apples throughput and latency benchmark against your baseline (same prompts/seq lengths). Redwood Optimize then simulates batching and quantization profiles; typical pilots see 20–50% per-token cost improvement for production workloads."

Competitive snapshot (one-paragraph)
------------------------------------

Redwood positions between large managed API vendors and self-hosted inference runtimes: we provide the developer velocity and integrations of a hosted API, but with a deterministic, production-grade serving runtime and options for dedicated or private deployments. Notable differentiators for buyers: clear per-route cost attribution, automated fallback policies for reliability, and first-class model lifecycle controls (pinning, canary rollouts). For buyers leaning toward full self-hosting, highlight Redwood’s private deployment patterns and our migration credits for enterprise conversions.

Pilot configuration templates (recommended)
-----------------------------------------

Template A — Latency-focused pilot (recommended for consumer-facing UX)
1) Routes: 1 conversational route with streaming enabled; 1 reranking route for post-processing.
2) Traffic: mirror 5–10% of production traffic or simulate 100 concurrent sessions.
3) Targets: P50 < 150ms token, P95 < 350ms, model quality delta <2% from baseline.
4) Observability: tracing enabled, token-level cost export, and error-rate alert at 0.1%.

Template B — Throughput-focused pilot (recommended for batch/embedding workloads)
1) Routes: batch-embeddings, bulk-generation.
2) Traffic: 500k embeddings/day simulated with mixed vector sizes.
3) Targets: CPU/GPU utilization 60–85% during peak; throughput > target per commit.
4) Observability: queue latency histogram, batch sizing reports, and cache hit metrics.

----------------------------------

Assumptions for worked scenarios:
- Unit costs used in examples are illustrative — adapt to current rate card during quotes.
- Pilot discount: 25% off hosted list for pilot periods under an MSA that converts.

Worked example: If a platform route uses 5M tokens/month and you aim to scale to 50M:
- Hosted list: 5M * $0.0008 = $4,000/month
- Reserved (dedicated) forecast: 50M * $0.00045 = $22,500/month (but unit cost decreases with higher commitment)
- Recommendation: start with hosted for 4–8 weeks to validate SLOs, then move to a modest reserved pool sized for 2–4x peak if conversion probability >40%.

Success criteria and metrics
---------------------------

- Technical success: P95 latency below agreed threshold, error rate <0.1%, model parity within guardrails on evaluation prompt set.
- Financial success: measured per-token cost delta vs. incumbent <= target threshold (e.g., 20% improvement or breakeven after reserved capacity).
- Business success: conversion to a 6–12 month reserved commitment or signed Private license.

Security FAQ for prospect meetings (ready-to-read answers)
---------------------------------------------------------

Q: Where is customer data stored during the pilot?
A: For Redwood Private, all persistent data remains in customer-controlled storage in the selected region. For Dedicated/Hosted pilots we can configure VPC peering and enable strict retention policies; short-lived token caches are stored on encrypted local volumes and rotated per best practice.

Q: Do you support customer-managed keys (KMS/HSM)?
A: Yes. Private deployments and Dedicated customers can use customer-managed KMS or HSM. Keys can be rotated under customer policy and access is limited to approved operator roles.

Q: Can we run a vendor security review / on-site audit?
A: Yes. We have a standardized audit package (SOC2 summary, threat model, patching cadence) and an accelerated review process for pilot conversions. For Private pilots we support scheduled remote validation and a 2‑week engineering validation window.

Q: How do you handle secrets, tokens, and telemetry?
A: Secrets are stored encrypted at rest with strict RBAC controls. Token-level telemetry is anonymized by default for hosted deployments; for Private deployments telemetry can be forwarded to customer observability endpoints without vendor retention.

Rollout & escalation playbook (brief)
------------------------------------

1) Week 0: kickoff — align SLOs, success metrics, and security checklist.
2) Week 1–2: integration — instrument one critical route, enable tracing, and run baseline load tests.
3) Week 3–5: validation — run 2 production-like windows, capture metrics, and run Redwood Optimize suggestions.
4) Week 6–8: decision — present success evidence to stakeholders, propose conversion options (reserved capacity or Private license).
5) Escalation path: bench-level SRE -> Platform eng lead -> CTO/CSO. Include on-call contact from Redwood engagement team.
