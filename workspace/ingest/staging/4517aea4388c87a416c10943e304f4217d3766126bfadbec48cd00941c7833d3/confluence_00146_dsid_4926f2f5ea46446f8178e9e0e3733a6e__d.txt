Distributed team workflows and home office allowance guidelines

Overview

Purpose: This page documents expectations, operational workflows, and the home office allowance program for distributed employees at Redwood Inference. It consolidates guidelines from People Ops, IT provisioning, and Facilities so remote hires and hybrid employees have a single source of truth.

Scope: Applies to all full-time and part-time Redwood employees with a primary work location outside a company-managed office (remote or hybrid). Contractors and vendors follow separate procurement rules described in the Vendor Procurement page.

Owners and contacts:
- Program owner: People Ops (people-ops@redwood.ai)
- IT provisioning: infra-provisioning@redwood.ai
- Ergonomics procurement: facilities@redwood.ai
- Escalation owner: Maya Thompson (people-ops)

1) Principles and expectations
- Autonomy with guardrails: Employees can configure their work setup within the allowance budget, but purchases must meet safety and security minimums.
- Equitable support: The allowance aims to remove barriers to effective remote work and is standardized by role band and country tier.
- Fast provisioning: Standard toolchains and default kits are available for 1-click provisioning to meet onboarding SLOs.

2) Home office allowance policy (high level)
- Eligibility: All full-time and part-time employees after successful onboarding (day 1 eligibility) are eligible. Interns and contractors follow separate stipend guidance.
- Allowance amounts (examples, local-currency equivalents will be published on the People Ops portal):

  Role band | One-time setup allowance | Annual refresh budget
  --------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------
  IC L1-L3 | $750 USD | $200 USD
  IC L4-L5 | $1,200 USD | $300 USD
  Manager & above | $1,500 USD | $400 USD

- What it covers: desks, ergonomic chairs, monitors, keyboard/mice, webcams, headsets, UPS/power protection, basic home networking upgrades (e.g., mesh extenders). Software subscriptions (VPN, password manager enterprise seats) are funded centrally and not part of the stipend.
- Prohibited purchases: personal entertainment equipment (gaming consoles, TVs primarily for leisure), large furniture not suitable for work (e.g., sofas), or purchases violating local tax/benefit rules.

3) Procurement and approval workflow (step-by-step)
1. Employee selects items using the People Ops procurement portal or the recommended vendor catalog.
2. If using catalog/standard kit, automatic approval and IT will schedule delivery within 7 business days.
3. For custom items above $500, submit a short justification via the procurement form; People Ops approves within 3 business days.
4. Reimbursements: Submit receipts through Expense Portal (category: Home Office) within 60 days. Reimbursements are processed on the next payroll cycle following approval.

Quick checklist for ordering:
- Confirm eligibility and remaining allowance balance on the People Ops portal.
- Use recommended models for ergonomic furniture to ensure warranty and return options.
- If equipment requires local installation (e.g., complex monitors), coordinate with IT for remote provisioning windows.

4) Standard remote starter kit (default for new hires)
- Laptop (company-managed) with standard image (see Internal Tools section)
- 27" monitor (or 24" where space constrained)
- Docking station compatible with Redwood image
- Ergonomic office chair (approved list)
- Noise-cancelling headset (USB + Bluetooth)
- External keyboard and mouse
- Power surge protector / small UPS
- One-time stipend to cover desk and small furniture

Onboarding SLOs for provisioning:
- Laptop issuance: Day 0 or first working day (goal: 95% of hires)
- Desk kit delivery: within 7 business days of kit order (goal: 90%)
- Access to core internal tools (email, SSO, Slack, Redwood Console) within 24 hours of start

5) Roles and career alignment for distributed contributors
Guidance for managers and people-ops when mapping role ladders and expectations for remote employees: 
- Performance criteria are identical for remote and on-site employees; however, certain role expectations may include additional travel or in-person checkpoints (documented in job requisitions).
- Promotion calibration: managers must include an explicit note in the promotion packet if remote location materially affects scope (e.g., being remote from the primary product hub may shift interview panel composition).
- Career ladder touchpoints: quarterly career check-ins are recommended for distributed employees to mitigate potential visibility gaps.

6) Collaboration and meeting best practices for distributed teams
- Async-first guidance: use Redwood Docs + Loom + Slack threads for updates; synchronous meetings should have clear agendas and recorded notes.
- Meeting time fairness: Managers must schedule recurring 1:1s within overlapping work windows for direct reports across time zones.
- Travel cadence: Teams should budget an annual in-person offsite or hub visit when role criticality requires face-to-face interaction; People Ops maintains a travel stipend guidance for these events.

7) Internal tools and provisioning (practical steps)
- Standard image and security:
  - Laptops are provisioned with the Redwood standard image (linux/macOS). Security baseline includes disk encryption, company VPN, endpoint agent, and SSO integration.
  - Remote employees must enable disk encryption and MFA on first login.
- Tool access checklist (to be completed by manager and hire within 24 hours):
  1. Email (G Suite)
  2. SSO and Okta group membership
  3. Slack (core channels + team channel)
  4. Redwood Console sandbox access (where relevant)
  5. VPN and developer VPN ACLs (request via infra-provisioning)
  6. HR and payroll portals

Provisioning automation examples (CLI/JSON examples for infra team):
- Example: request vpn ACL change (submit as JSON to infra-provisioning API):

  {
    "user_email": "new.hire@redwood.ai",
    "access_level": "developer",
    "valid_for_days": 30
  }

- If a user is missing access after 24 hours, managers should open an IT ticket with subject prefix: PROVISIONING / <employee-name>.

8) Ergonomics, returns, and safety
- Minimum ergonomic standards: adjustable monitor with VESA mount, lumbar-support office chair, external keyboard/mouse. Approved vendor list maintained by Facilities.
- Returns: If equipment arrives damaged or is unsuitable, employees must request a return through the People Ops portal within 14 days. For chairs, an extended 60-day return window is available because of fit considerations.
- Safety incident reporting: Remote workplace safety incidents (e.g., equipment-caused injury) must be reported to People Ops and Security within 48 hours.

9) Country and tax considerations
- People Ops publishes country-specific guidance for tax treatment of stipends. In some jurisdictions the allowance is taxable; in others it is classified as a non-taxable reimbursement. Employees are responsible for reviewing local guidance.
- For high-value shipments (furniture), Redwood may require a local VAT or import handling process; Facilities will coordinate with the employee.

10) Exceptions, audits, and program health metrics
- Exceptions: Managers can request exceptions for special roles (e.g., lab setups for Research) by filing an Exception Request with People Ops; approvals require budget sign-off.
- Program KPIs tracked monthly:
  - Time-to-provision (median days)
  - Allowance utilization rate
  - Onboarding satisfaction (new-hire survey)
  - Number of ergonomic escalations/returns
- Audit cadence: People Ops will run an annual audit of purchases and reimbursements to ensure compliance with policy.

11) Troubleshooting and escalation matrix
- Issue: Laptop not delivered by day 1 -> Escalate to infra-provisioning and CC People Ops.
- Issue: Missing tool access after 24 hours -> Manager opens PROVISIONING ticket with infra-provisioning.
- Issue: Stipend reimbursement delayed -> Finance (payroll@redwood.ai) with People Ops in CC.
