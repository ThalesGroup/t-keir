## Primary narrative (what we’re really saying)
Teams don’t win by chasing “best tokens/sec” in a lab—they win by meeting user-facing latency targets **at an acceptable unit cost**, consistently, across the regions they serve.

Redwood built and published a reproducible benchmark focused on **production inference economics**:
- **Latency** (p50/p95) and **throughput** under defined concurrency and token mixes
- **Cost efficiency** expressed as **Latency/$** (plus $/1M tokens for transparency)
- **Reliability and consistency** signals (variance gates, repeat runs, exclusions documented)
- **Regional comparability**, so you can plan global performance and capacity

The takeaway: Redwood helps you serve LLMs predictably and economically—whether you start with Hosted API, reserve Dedicated capacity, or deploy Private (VPC/on-prem) for regulated workloads.

## Message house (pillars + proof)
Use this structure in talk tracks, deck narrative, and discovery.

### Pillar 1 — Production Economics: Latency/$ (not vanity metrics)
**Claim (approved):** Redwood publishes and optimizes for **Latency/$** so teams can meet latency targets while controlling per-token cost.

**Proof points to use (pick 2–3):**
- Latency/$ defined as a **normalized efficiency metric** derived from measured latency under load and attributable serving cost (see Methodology page).
- Report includes supporting metrics: **p50/p95 latency, throughput, and $/1M tokens**.
- Benchmarks use a production-oriented prompt mix (prefill/decode balance) and defined concurrency, warmup, and cache/batch policies.

**Customer-impact phrasing:**
- “If you have a p95 target, Latency/$ helps you see what it costs to hit that target, not just how fast a GPU can go on a single request.”


### Pillar 2 — Predictable Performance Under Load (throughput + p95)
**Claim (approved):** Redwood focuses on **tail latency and stability** under defined concurrency so you can reason about real user experience.

**Proof points to use:**
- Runs include concurrency levels and queueing behavior; results report p95 (not only p50).
- Stability gates: repeated runs and variance thresholds trigger reruns; outliers and exclusions are documented.

**Customer-impact phrasing:**
- “Most production issues show up in p95, not in best-case single-request timings.”


### Pillar 3 — Reliability & Operational Guardrails (benchmarking and serving)
**Claim (approved):** The benchmark methodology mirrors Redwood’s production discipline: reproducibility, validation, and documented caveats.

**Proof points to use:**
- Run manifests, configs, and validation checklist are part of the approval workflow.
- Public artifacts are redacted and retained internally to substantiate published claims.

**Customer-impact phrasing:**
- “We’re not asking you to trust a screenshot—we can show the how, the configs, and what we excluded.”


### Pillar 4 — Regional Consistency & Deployment Flexibility (Hosted → Dedicated → Private)
**Claim (approved):** Redwood provides a consistent inference experience across **multiple regions and deployment modes**, with clear levers to balance cost/latency.

**Proof points to use:**
- Report spans NA/EU/APAC where available; differences are explained and caveated.
- Dedicated capacity is positioned for predictability; Private for data residency, isolation, and regulated environments.

**Customer-impact phrasing:**
- “As you expand to new regions or need isolation, you don’t need to rebuild your serving stack—your API and operational model stay consistent.”

## Approved language snippets (copy/paste)
### 15-second opener
“We published a Latency/$ benchmark because teams care about user latency and unit cost together. It measures p50/p95 under defined concurrency and token mixes across models and regions, with a transparent methodology so you can interpret the tradeoffs.”

### When a prospect asks “what does Latency/$ mean?”
“It’s a normalized metric that combines measured latency under a defined workload with attributable serving cost, so you can compare how efficiently different model+region options deliver responsiveness per dollar. We still show p50/p95 and $/1M tokens alongside it.”

### When a prospect asks “is this production-realistic?”
“It’s designed to be closer to production than a single-request microbenchmark: concurrency, warmups, and a prompt mix that reflects prefill/decode patterns. And we publish the methodology and caveats so you can map it to your own workload.”

### When a prospect asks “how should I use this?”
“Use it to shortlist a few model+region candidates, then we’ll validate against your real prompts and latency SLOs. If you need predictable tail latency at scale, we’ll talk Dedicated; if you need residency/isolation, we’ll talk Private.”

## Required caveats (must include when discussing numbers)
Use these especially in competitive contexts.
- “Results depend on workload (context length, concurrency, prefill/decode mix), region, and serving configuration.”
- “Benchmark conditions are defined and documented; numbers should be interpreted within that methodology.”
- “We report distributions (p50/p95) and run stability checks; residual variance can occur due to capacity and network conditions.”
- “Competitor baselines, if referenced, must be described with **source, date, configuration assumptions, and limitations**.”

## Competitive posture (safe framing)
### What we can say
- “Latency/$ helps compare efficiency for real user experience under load.”
- “Raw tokens/sec often misses tail latency and doesn’t reflect the cost to hit a p95 target.”
- “We publish methodology and caveats so teams can evaluate fit and reproduce key parts.”

### What we should do in a live conversation
- Ask for their workload: context length, concurrency, streaming vs non-streaming, p95 requirement, region(s), and traffic shape.
- Offer a “replication plan” in their environment (PoC guidance) rather than arguing over any single chart.

## Disallowed claims / phrasing (do not use)
These are credibility and legal risk areas. If you’re unsure, route to PMM + Legal.

### Absolute superiority claims (no)
- “Fastest / best / #1 across all models and regions”
- “Always cheaper” / “always lowest latency”
- “Guarantees” tied to benchmark outcomes (benchmarks are not SLAs)

### Unqualified comparative claims (no)
- “We beat {competitor} by X%” without specifying exact test conditions, date, and configuration caveats.
- “Competitor results are wrong” (instead: “different methodology/assumptions; here’s ours.”)

### Overstating representativeness (no)
- “This exactly matches every production workload”
- “These numbers will be identical for you”

### Misleading metric language (no)
- “Latency/$ is the only metric that matters”
- “Tokens/sec equals user latency”

## Handling tough questions (redirects)
### “Did you cherry-pick prompts, regions, or models?”
“We published the selection criteria and methodology, and we include caveats and exclusions. If your workload differs, we’ll help run a targeted PoC with your prompts and success criteria.”

### “Were caching/batching enabled?”
“Benchmark configurations are explicitly documented. We’ll clarify the cache/batch policy used for the published numbers and can show how enabling/disabling changes outcomes depending on workload.”

### “Are these numbers an SLA?”
“No—benchmarks are measurements under defined conditions. SLAs and capacity guarantees are handled through Dedicated contracts and deployment architecture.”
