Stakeholder synthesis playbook for long-tail regressions

Summary:

This playbook defines a lightweight but rigorous process for identifying, containing, and synthesizing stakeholder-facing artifacts for long-tail regressions — recurring, low-to-medium impact SLO regressions that surface intermittently across tenants or routes and persist for days to weeks. These regressions are often noisy, hard to reproduce, and frequently involve multi-component interactions (routing, cache, model variants, autoscaling). The goal is to shorten MTTI (mean time to identify), standardize evidence packaging for product and customer stakeholders, and create a repeatable operational cadence to remove ambiguity from customer communications and postmortems.

Sections:
1) Detection & classification
2) Immediate containment runbook (first 30 minutes)
3) Evidence pack: what to collect and how to store it
4) Stakeholder synthesis and messaging templates
5) Longer-term remediation and game-day plan
6) Ownership, rotation tweaks, and success metrics

---

## 1) Detection & classification

Short intro: Long-tail regressions differ from high-severity outages. They often present as subtle SLO drift, tenant-specific p99 spikes, or periodic increases in error rate for a subset of routes. Classify the event quickly to size the response.

Classification matrix (use this to choose containment actions):

| Class | Symptoms | Initial priority | Typical owners | Example threshold |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| L1 — transient noise | single-region p95 latency +10% for <2 hours | low | service owner + oncall | p95 > baseline*1.1 for <2h |
| L2 — long-tail regression | repeated tenant p99 spikes, elevated error % for multiple days | medium | service owner + infra + product | p99 > baseline*1.25 across 6+ occurrences in 48h |
| L3 — emerging systemic pattern | multiple services or regions show drifting SLOs | high | cross-functional page + incident lead | global p95/p99 degradation > 20% or sustained for 4+h |

Notes: baseline = rolling 30-day median for that route/tenant. Use alert aggregation to group correlated signals across tenants and models.

## 2) Immediate containment runbook (first 30 minutes)

Objective: reduce customer-visible impact while preserving evidence for root-cause analysis.

Step-by-step:
1. Acknowledge and classify the signal in the alerting system (PagerDuty). Tag event with "long-tail" and affected tenant(s).
2. Triage owner (oncall) runs quick query to scope: top 10 affected tenant IDs, affected routes, model version, and regions (commands below).
3. If tenant-impacted, engage tenant success/customer success per comms matrix.
4. Apply non-invasive mitigations first: widen autoscaler min capacity by +10%, apply temporary rate-limit backfill, enable prefix cache bypass for suspect model variants. Document each action in the incident chat.
5. If mitigation resolves visible symptoms within 15 minutes, continue monitoring for 2h and begin evidence pack assembly; otherwise escalate to L2 and run the escalation checklist.

Useful queries (examples):
- kubectl logs -l app=redwood-serving --since=30m | grep <tenant-id> | head -n 200
- curl "https://ops-api.redwood/v1/metrics?route=<route>&window=60m" | jq
- SELECT tenant_id, p99_latency FROM metrics.route_aggregates WHERE window='60m' ORDER BY p99_latency DESC LIMIT 20;

Escalation: If >10% of a service's tenants are impacted or the mitigation increases error rate elsewhere, invoke cross-functional page and attach the quick-scope output.

## 3) Evidence pack: what to collect and how to store it

Purpose: Provide a consistent bundle for postmortem analysis and for customer-facing summaries (helps legal, compliance, and CS). Store the evidence pack as an artifact in the incident S3 bucket: s3://incidents/long-tail/YYYYMMDD-<incident-id>/evidence.zip

Minimum evidence checklist:
- Incident metadata file (incident-id, owner, start/end timestamps, classification)
- Top 100 request IDs (with timestamps) and full request/response traces (Jaeger links or trace blobs)
- Request payload examples for each affected tenant/route (sanitized)
- Model version and model routing policy snapshot (models.json)
- KV/prefix cache hit/miss snapshot and eviction logs for the window
- Autoscaler events (scale-up/scale-down) and GPU utilization graphs (CSV)
- Representative logs from: serving runtime, router/proxy, autoscaler, and model loader (last 24h)
- Alert and notification timeline (raw PagerDuty/Slack messages)
- A short reproduction attempt log: commands tried and their outcomes
- Temporary mitigation actions applied and timestamps (who applied them)

Metadata file example (plain text, one line per key):
incident_id: LT-20260301-42
classification: L2
start_ts: 2026-02-26T14:05:00Z
detected_by: anomaly-detector-v2
owner: ashapatel@redwood.ai
affected_tenants: [tenant-671, tenant-254, tenant-194]

Retention: evidence packs for long-tail regressions are retained for 540 days. Mark packs required by legal as restricted in the index.

## 4) Stakeholder synthesis and messaging templates

Purpose: Rapidly produce clear, non-technical and technical messages for customers and internal stakeholders. The synthesis has two parallel outputs: (A) Customer brief (concise), (B) Internal synthesis (technical + evidence pack link). Both must be produced within 48 hours for L2 events.

Customer brief template (use for affected tenants only):
Subject: Brief update — intermittent increase in latency for [service/route]
Hello <CustomerName>,

Summary: Between <start_ts> and ongoing, we observed intermittent increases in latency for <route> which may have affected some requests. We have applied temporary mitigations and continue monitoring. We have not detected data loss.

What we did: temporarily increased capacity and adjusted routing to reduce impact.

What we're doing next: collecting traces and running a targeted game-day to reproduce and fix the root cause. We will share a technical summary within 5 business days and a final postmortem within 21 days.

Who to contact: <CS rep name> (cc) — for urgent concerns reply to this message.

Internal synthesis template (deliver to product, infra, security):
Title: LT-<id> synthesis — short description
Owner: <owner team/email>
Start: <start_ts> — Observed by <detector/alert>
Scope: top affected tenants, routes, model variants, regions (attach evidence pack link)
Immediate mitigations: list of actions + timestamps
Initial hypothesis: succinct bullet list of working theories
Next steps: planned deep-dive, responsible owners, timeline
Impact summary: measurable customer impact (requests failed, latency deltas, affected SLAs)

Always attach: evidence pack S3 link, top 10 traces, and relevant dashboards. For any L3 escalation, use the full incident comm template in the incident-runbook index.

## 5) Longer-term remediation and game-day plan

If a long-tail regression cannot be eliminated within 72 hours, schedule a dedicated remediation sprint with the following cadence:
- Day 0–3: triage and reproduce attempts; expand evidence pack if needed
- Day 4: postmortem skeleton and product sync — 1 hour cross-team meeting
- Day 5–14: remediation sprint (2-week window recommended) with weekly syncs
- Post-sprint: full postmortem and follow-ups tracked as ACTION items in the RCA board

Game-day scenario (run quarterly for long-tail readiness):
Objective: practice diagnosing tenant-scoped latency regressions with noisy autoscaler and cache churn.

Setup checklist:
- Choose two teams: Incident Response (oncall + infra) and Stakeholder Comms (CS + Product).
- Seed synthetic tenants with malformed model routing metadata that triggers a slow fallback path (test cluster only).
- Configure alert nebulizer to send intermittent p99 spikes for the seeded tenants.

Exercise flow (3 hours):
1. Detect — alert triggers for seeded tenants; oncall triages and applies standard containment actions.
2. Evidence pack — participants practice assembling required artifacts within 45 minutes.
3. Stakeholder synthesis — craft customer brief and internal synthesis within 60 minutes.
4. Debrief — 45-minute retro. Record observations as runbook updates and add checklist items to the runbook.

Success criteria: evidence pack completeness >= 90%, customer brief accuracy (validated by CS), and reproduction attempt documented.

## 6) Ownership, rotation tweaks, and success metrics

Who owns this playbook: Eng: Incident Response Working Group (alias: ir-wg@redwood.ai).

Oncall rotation tweak suggestions for long-tail sensitivity:
- Add a 24-h "long-tail shadow" to each weekly rotation: a secondary oncall who reviews longer-running anomalies mid-shift.
- The long-tail shadow rotates weekly and is responsible for ensuring evidence packs are assembled for any L2s detected on their watch.

Success metrics (tracked quarterly):
- Mean time to evidence pack (MTTEP) — goal <= 48 hours for L2 (current baseline: 96h)
- Fraction of L2 events with customer brief delivered within 48h — goal 90%
- Reduction in repeat regressions caused by the same root cause within 90 days — goal 50% reduction
