PagerFlow: responder orchestration guidelines and customer notice templates

Summary:
This page defines the PagerFlow model — a lightweight orchestration layer for responder handoffs, escalation routing, and customer-facing status updates across Redwood Inference. PagerFlow is meant to standardize triage decisions, accelerate containment, and ensure consistent, timely communication to stakeholders and customers.

Scope:
- Applies to all service-impacting events that affect customer-facing inference (hosted, dedicated, private).
- Includes operational steps for triage, escalation, customer notice templates, on-call rotation fairness rules, and a game-day exercise plan for the next 12 months.
- Not intended for routine maintenance notices (see maintenance-notices policy).

PagerFlow overview:
PagerFlow organizes incident response into three phases: Detect -> Route -> Restore. The goal is to automate decision points where safe, and provide clear human-readable steps where automation cannot cover context-sensitive choices.

1) Detect:
- Signals: SLO alerts (latency/availability), rate limit saturation, KV cache thrashing, elevated 5xx ratios, internal health-checks, and external customer reports.
- Evidence capture: always attach the following to a new PagerFlow incident: timestamped alert IDs, first 10 request traces, example failing request id, affected regions, and model(s) implicated.

2) Route:
- Automated routing: PagerFlow uses the signal type and impacted product tier to pick an initial responder (see Escalation matrix). For example, a Host API latency SLO breach triggers the Serving runtime on-call; an auth failure triggers infra/security triage.
- Manual reassignment: bridge operator or lead responder can re-route if evidence points to cross-team ownership. Include reason in the incident timeline.

3) Restore:
- Focus on quick, safe mitigations (prefix cache evict, temporary routing to fallback model, scale-up reserved capacity) before longer-term fixes.
- Use feature flags and canary rollbacks where applicable.

Roles & responsibilities:
- Bridge Operator (first 15 minutes): owns the incident page, status cadence, and escalation to L1/L2.
- L1 On-call (team-specific): executes containment steps, gathers metrics, and proposes a mitigation.
- L2 / Subject Matter Expert: performs deeper diagnostics, code-level rollbacks, or model variant routing.
- Incident Commander (IC): appointed for Sev1/Sev2 and owns stakeholder comms and postmortem initiation.
- Customer Success Liaison: drafts customer notices for anything customer-impacting beyond 30 minutes.

Escalation matrix (compact table):
| Severity | Example symptoms | Initial responder | Escalation after | IC assignment |
| Severity-1 (Sev1) | Cross-region outage, sustained >30% 5xx, total traffic loss | Serving runtime on-call + Bridge | 10 minutes | Platform SRE lead |
| Severity-2 (Sev2) | High error rate for a major model or region, major degradation >20% | L1 Product infra on-call | 30 minutes | Team lead on rotation |
| Severity-3 (Sev3) | Single-region reduced throughput, rate limit hotspots | Service owner on-call | 2 hours | N/A |
| Severity-4 (Sev4) | Degraded internal tooling, minor customer impact | Ticket created, owner triages in working hours | N/A | N/A |

Triage & containment procedure (step-by-step):
1. Acknowledge & capture (Bridge Operator): confirm alert, open incident page, add links to dashboards and traces.
2. Initial classification: pick severity using the matrix above. Document rationale in the incident timeline.
3. Contain (L1): apply non-invasive mitigations first (traffic re-routing, throttling noisy tenants, apply temporary model fallback).
4. If containment fails within escalation window, escalate to L2 and request IC appointment.
5. IC coordinates cross-team work, authorizes any rollbacks, and sets customer notice cadence.
6. Stabilize, then run verification suite for affected paths, and proceed to post-incident review.

Evidence checklist (to attach before postmortem):
- Alert snapshots and rule IDs
- Time series of latency/5xx rates (pre/during/post)
- Representative request IDs / trace/span links
- Resource metrics (GPU utilization, queue length, memory pressure)
- Configuration changes (deploys, infra changes) with commit IDs
- Customer-impact list (tenants, regions, models)

On-call rotation policy (PagerFlow fairness rules):
- Rotation window: 7 days per rotation for primary on-call.
- Shadow shifts: 24-hour handover where the incoming is shadowing to reduce blast risk.
- Equity rules: maximum 48 hours back-to-back shift coverage; extra burn hours accrue compensatory time or on-call stipend adjustment.
- Escalation backup: each primary on-call must list two backups. If backup is unavailable, team lead assigns temporary coverage.

Handover checklist (end of shift):
- Confirm no open Sev1/Sev2 items remain unresolved.
- Export and summarize incidents handled during shift into handover doc: status, actions taken, pending tasks.
- Confirm runbook edits required and assign owners for follow-ups.

Game day plan (quarterly cadence):
- Objectives: exercise KV cache eviction, simulated noisy-tenant throttles, fallback model routing, and customer notice drafting under realistic time pressure.
- Participants: Bridge Operators, L1/L2 on-calls, CS liaison, and platform reliability engineers.
- Scenario examples:
  1) KV cache keyspace explosion causing 95th p99 latency to double.
  2) Model archive corruption that requires automated fallback and manual model re-ingest.
- Success criteria: mean time to first mitigation < 8 minutes, accurate customer notice drafted within 20 minutes, and no unapproved rollbacks.

Postmortem & learning loop (fast path):
- Initiation: For Sev1/Sev2, IC opens a postmortem within 48 hours.
- Template to capture: summary, timeline, contributing factors, root cause hypothesis, mitigations, and action items with owners and ETA.
- Blameless language: use factual descriptions, avoiding attribution to individuals.
- Action item triage: each action must be prioritized as P0/P1/P2 and assigned. P0 actions get 1-week SLA.

Sample customer status templates (copyable) :
1) Short initial notice (within 30 minutes):
Subject: Redwood Inference — Partial service degradation in [region]
Body:
We are investigating elevated error rates affecting text generation in [region]. Our teams are actively triaging. At this time some requests may see increased latency or errors. We will send an update within 30 minutes.
(Do not include speculative root causes or timelines.)

2) Escalated update (after 60 minutes):
Subject: Redwood Inference — Ongoing degradation affecting [feature/model] in [region]
Body:
Status: ongoing
Impact: customers using [model id / endpoint] may experience errors or timeouts.
What we are doing: routing traffic to fallback model, increasing capacity, and investigating root cause.
Next update: in 30 minutes or when we have a change in status.

3) Post-recovery notice:
Subject: Redwood Inference — Service restored for [region]
Body:
Status: restored
Summary: We restored service by [mitigation action]. A post-incident summary and timeline will be published to affected customers within 72 hours.
Customer credits: if applicable, include the entitlement policy link and next steps for credits.

Internal bridge operator checklist (copyable):
- Create incident page and set severity tag
- Notify on-call Slack channel and open PagerFlow alert
- Attach minimum evidence set (alerts, traces, metrics)
- Start 15-minute status cadence: 0/15/30/60 minutes
- Escalate to IC if containment not achieved within window
- Record all mitigation commands and who approved them

Runbook change governance:
- Any runbook edits made during an incident must be proposed as a PR to the runbook repo within 48 hours and reviewed by at least one reviewer from the impacted team.
- Game-day discoveries that change runbooks require a follow-up validation exercise in the next two weeks.

SLOs, guardrails, and alert thresholds (examples):
- Host API availability SLO: 99.95% monthly. Alert when 1-minute error rate > 2% AND sustained for 5 consecutive minutes.
- Token latency p95: alert if increase > 2x baseline for > 5 minutes.
- KV cache miss storm: alert if miss-rate > 60% and eviction count > 10k/min.

Service degradation classification examples (quick reference):
- Degradation type: Latency spike due to cache eviction storm. Action: limit prefix caching, increase batching, route to other region.
- Degradation type: High 5xx after deploy. Action: rollback to previous deploy, isolate commit, run verification.
