sli-cost-latency-ablation-retrospective-2026

Summary
-------
This retrospective documents the ablation study we ran across Q4 2025 and early Q1 2026 that intentionally removed or degraded telemetry signals used in the token-latency and cost SLI system to understand sensitivity, false-positive rates, and operator cognitive load. The goal was to produce concrete runbook changes, dashboard improvements, and rollout guidance to reduce SLI churn during regional blips and model-variant rollouts.

Background
----------
- Context: Several oncall incidents in Oct–Nov 2025 showed oscillating SLI alerts tied to noisy cost-signal spikes (billing anomalies) and intermittent token-latency reports stemming from partial KV cache failures. We wanted to quantify which signals were essential and which amplified noise.
- Hypothesis: Some telemetry variants (per-tenant token-cost per-minute, short-window p50/p95 token latency computed at the ingress proxy) amplify transient noise. Ablating secondary signals and relying on orthogonal indicators (GPU-queue length, model-variant error-rate, KV-hit ratio) will reduce alert fatigue without increasing time-to-detect real regressions.

Study design
------------
1. Scope and targets
   - Services: routing-proxy, inference-runtime, kv-prefix-cache, cost-aggregator
   - SLIs evaluated: token-latency (user-facing tail), token-cost (per-tenant cost rate), request-error-rate, model-variant-fallback-rate
2. Ablation matrix
   - Full signal set (baseline)
   - Remove short-window p50/p95 at ingress (30s windows)
   - Remove per-tenant minute-level cost derivative alert (keep hourly)
   - Replace synthetic-sla synthetic checks with synthetic-sla-lite (less frequent)
   - Silence KV-hit ratio-derived micro-alerts (promote to advisory)
3. Duration and rollout
   - Canary region: us-west-2 (2 weeks)
   - Staged rollouts: canary -> 3 regions -> global (each stage 72h observation)
   - Automated rollback if combined SRE detection score > 0.7 (see detection scoring below)

Timeline and data collected
-------------------------
- Nov 20–Dec 04, 2025: Baseline observability capture (pre-ablation)
- Dec 06–Dec 20, 2025: Canary ablation in us-west-2
- Jan 05–Jan 19, 2026: Regional expansion and stress-testing during model-variant rollout
- Jan 20–Feb 10, 2026: Post-rollout monitoring and manual incident injections
- Data sources: Prometheus (metrics), ClickHouse traces, billing-tables (hourly), incident tickets, oncall annotations

Key findings
------------
- Removing short-window ingress p95 reduced alert volume by ~43% without increasing median time-to-detect for verified incidents (median time-to-detect increased from 3.8m to 4.2m).
- Silencing minute-level per-tenant cost derivative alerts cut false positives from noisy metering by 78% but allowed two low-impact cost leaks to persist 6–12 hours longer; both were detected by hourly summaries and manual audits.
- Promoting KV-hit micro-alerts to advisory reduced operator context-switching; when KV-hit advisory crossed 5% degradation coincident with GPU-queue growth > 12, it reliably predicted tail-latency regressions within 18 minutes.
- Synthetic-sla-lite (less frequent synthetic checks) caught only 60% of degradation events that affected <5% of traffic; use synthetic-sla-lite only when paired with additional internal-sided health checks.

Root cause and analysis
-----------------------
- Many false-positives originated from noise in short aggregation windows at the proxy (burst traffic patterns, backoff storms). The ingestion pipeline's downsampling amplified micro-bursts into perceived p95 regressions.
- Billing-metering noise was caused by short-lived quantization in our cost-aggregator and delayed reconciliation between per-token meter and hourly summary tables.
- The observability deck lacked cross-signal correlation at alert time; operators had to manually stitch the timeline across cost, latency, and queue metrics.

Decisions made
--------------
- Keep tail-latency SLI (token-latency p99/p95) but remove 30s short-window p95 from primary alerting rules. Short-window p95 will appear on a "fast-sight" dashboard only.
- De-prioritize per-tenant minute-level cost derivative alerts: move to advisory channel and require corroborating signals (e.g., billing reconciliation anomaly OR persistent token-inflation for 2 consecutive windows) before paging.
- Add a combined-rule: page only when (tail-latency SLI breach AND (gpu-queue > 10 OR model-variant-fallback-rate > 0.5%)). This reduces noise while preserving detection power.

Runbook changes (short list)
--------------------------
- New play: "Cost-Derivative Advisory Investigation" (see steps below). Owner: Platform Observability (pager duty: platform-observability rota).
- Update: "Tail-Latency Pager" flow now includes immediate checklist to check KV-hit advisory and GPU-queue before escalation to runtime owners.
- Add guidance for oncall: use the "fast-sight" dashboard when investigating sub-5min blips; do not escalate immediately unless corroborating signals exist.

Cost-Derivative Advisory Investigation (new play)
--------------------------------------------------
1. Observe advisory channel for per-tenant cost spikes.
2. Check hourly summary table: `SELECT tenant_id, sum(tokens) as tokens_hour FROM billing.hourly WHERE ts >= now() - interval 2 hour GROUP BY tenant_id ORDER BY tokens_hour DESC LIMIT 10;`
3. Cross-check model-variant fallback rate and token-latency p99 for the same tenant and time window.
4. If both token-latency p99 and cost derivative are elevated for >2 windows, open an incident and follow standard cost-incident runbook.

Detection scoring and rollback guard
----------------------------------
- Each stage of rollout computed an SRE detection score (0–1) aggregated from: SLI-breach-rate (0.5), new incident count (0.2), manual operator escalation (0.2), noise ratio (false-alerts / total-alerts) penalty (0.1).
- If detection score > 0.7 within 72h, automatic rollback to previous alerting config triggered. We used this to protect regional rollout.

Dashboard and alerting changes (implementation plan)
---------------------------------------------------
- Dashboards
  - Create "fast-sight" dashboard (ingress-short-window-metrics, kv-hit-ratio, gpu-queue, model-variant-fallback) for first responders. Embed a small timeline correlator widget.
  - Add a cost-reconciliation panel showing hourly vs per-minute aggregates and a reconciliation delta heatmap.
  - Tag all relevant panels with "advisory" or "primary" to guide oncall focus.
- Alerts
  - Primary alerts updated in Prometheus rules: remove 30s p95-based alert rules; add combined-rule described above.
  - Advisory alerts routed to a different channel (platform-advisory) with a lighter notification policy (email + slack thread, no paging).

Operational impact and metrics
-----------------------------
- Alert volume (pager count) reduced 32% across platform owners during rollout windows.
- Mean time to acknowledge (MTTA) for genuine incidents remained stable (median 3.9m pre, 4.1m post).
- Operator-reported cognitive load (post-deployment survey, n=34) improved: 67% reported fewer unnecessary escalations; 12% reported slower initial detection on low-impact cost leaks.

Guidelines for future experiments
--------------------------------
- Always pair ablation of a signal with at least one orthogonal guardrail (queue length, error-rate, or reconciliation delta).
- Use a detection score with at least four independent components (SLI, incidents, operator escalation, noise penalty).
- Reserve "advisory" channel for signals that historically cause >60% false positives and are actionable only with corroboration.

Appendix: queries and snippets
-----------------------------
- Ingress short-window p95 query (Prometheus example):
```
histogram_quantile(0.95, sum(rate(proxy_request_duration_seconds_bucket[30s])) by (le, region))
```
- GPU-queue alert snippet (Prometheus rule example):
```
- alert: GPUQueueHigh
  expr: sum(queue_length) by (region) > 10
  for: 5m
  labels:
    severity: page
  annotations:
    summary: "GPU queue length > 10 (region={{ $labels.region }})"
```
- Reconciliation delta SQL (ClickHouse example):
```
SELECT toHour(ts) as hour, tenant_id, sum(per_minute_tokens) as minute_tokens, sum(hourly_tokens) as hourly_tokens, (hourly_tokens - minute_tokens) as reconciliation_delta
FROM billing.per_minute JOIN billing.hourly USING (tenant_id, hour)
WHERE ts >= now() - interval 24 hour
GROUP BY hour, tenant_id
ORDER BY reconciliation_delta DESC
LIMIT 50;
```

Conclusion
----------
The ablation experiment validated that careful de-emphasis of short-window ingress and minute-level per-tenant cost signals materially reduces operator noise while preserving detection of genuine regressions when combined with orthogonal guardrails. The changes above balance reduced pager fatigue with a modest increase in time-to-detect low-impact cost leaks. We recommend the configuration changes be rolled out broadly after the planned runbook and dashboard work is completed.
