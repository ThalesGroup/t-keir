Postmortem Writeup Style Guide and Executive Summary Templates

Purpose
This document defines the standard structure, language guidance, and templates for producing postmortem writeups and short executive summaries at Redwood Inference. Our goal is faster, higher-quality postmortems that surface actionable remediation, respect blameless culture, and provide concise executive-ready summaries suitable for customers and leadership.

Scope
- All incidents that meet the postmortem threshold (see "Incident Triage and Postmortem Thresholds" below)
- Game-day exercises where a formal writeup is required
- Customer-impacting outages and incidents requiring external communication or regulatory evidence

Quick reference: When to write a postmortem
1) Severity P1 or P2 incident that impacts >1% of customers or violates an SLO for >10 minutes.
2) Any incident with regulatory evidence requirements (enterprise customers, data residency, security incidents).
3) Any game day or chaos experiment that requires lessons learned.

1. Required structure of a postmortem page (order matters)
- Title: short, specific, include incident date and primary symptom (e.g., "2026-02-18 — Multi-region KV-cache Eviction Causing 5xx Spike")
- Severity and duration: single line (Severity: P1, Start: YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM UTC, End: YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM UTC, Duration: 00h00m)
- Executive summary (one paragraph + 3 bullets) — see template below
- Impact summary: number of customers, affected routes, estimated error budget/percentage, and key metrics (e.g., 95th latency, error rate)
- Timeline: ordered, timestamped events (ISO 8601) and short descriptions; include detection, mitigation, and closure events
- Root cause analysis: concise causal chain (not a laundry list) with evidence links (logs, dashboards, traces)
- Mitigations applied during the incident (with timestamps and owners)
- Long-term corrective actions: 90-day plan with owner and target completion date (format: ACTION — OWNER — ETA)
- Verification plan: how we will validate the fix (synthetic tests, load tests, monitoring checks)
- Lessons learned / Preventative measures: short list, prioritized
- Postmortem checklist and artifacts: list of evidence files retained and retention policy
- Distribution list and stakeholder notifications: who was informed and when
- Closure criteria and sign-off: product, SRE, and legal (if required) approval

2. Executive summary template (target length: 3–6 sentences + up to 3 bullets)
- One-line incident description: what happened and when.
- Top-level impact: number of customers or percentage affected, and key metric delta (e.g., "peak error rate 18% (baseline 0.3%)").
- Root cause (one sentence): the proximate failure in plain language.
- Short statement of immediate mitigation and current state.
- Bulleted quick actions: 2–3 highest-priority next steps with owners.

Example executive summary (copyable)
"On 2026-02-18 at 14:02 UTC a multi-region KV-cache eviction path was triggered during a rolling kernel update, causing sustained 5xx errors on text-generation routes for ~72 minutes. Approximately 12% of production requests experienced errors at peak; SLOs for 99th percentile latency and error budget were violated. The proximate cause was a race condition in the cache eviction handler under parallel evacuation; a mis-sized eviction window was exposed by the higher-than-expected GC pressure. Immediate mitigation reverted the kernel update and scaled replicas to drain hot partitions; customer-facing traffic is back to nominal. Short-term actions: 1) Fix eviction race (owner: Sasha) — ETA 2026-03-15; 2) Add synthetic canary for kernel-update path (owner: Infra Team) — ETA 2026-02-28."

3. Blameless language checklist (must be enforced)
- Use facts, not attributions. Replace: "Engineer X deployed a bad patch" with "A patch containing an eviction-window change was deployed during maintenance".
- Avoid "caused by" that implies intent. Use "contributed to" or "exposed".
- Attribute systemic factors (process, tests, monitoring, documentation), not people.
- Include timeline evidence that supports statements.

4. Incident triage and postmortem thresholds (short table in text)
- P1 (complete service outage or major data loss): postmortem required and must be published within 5 business days.
- P2 (partial degradation impacting many customers): postmortem required within 10 business days.
- P3 & below: postmortem encouraged where cross-team learning is likely; product or incident owner can opt-in.

5. Evidence and artifact retention (minimums)
- Full APM trace export for the incident window (retain 90 days)
- Raw logs for implicated services (retain 30 days unless regulatory hold)
- Correlated metrics, synthetic test outputs, and timeline screenshots (retain 180 days)
- Incident bridge recording and decision artifacts (retain 365 days)
- For enterprise customers or regulatory incidents, follow the Incident Evidence Chain runbook for extended retention and legal hold.

6. Timeline guidance (best practice)
- Use ISO 8601 timestamps (UTC).
- Each event row: TIMESTAMP — ROLE — ACTION/OBSERVATION — LINK TO EVIDENCE.
- Distinguish detection vs mitigation events.
- Annotate who made the decision and why (brief rationale).

Sample timeline fragment (text table)
2026-02-18T14:02:12Z — Monitoring — Spike detected: 5xx rate > 10% on /v1/generate — link: grafana/dashboard/err-rate
2026-02-18T14:05:30Z — Oncall — Opened incident bridge (bridge-id) and paged Platform team
2026-02-18T14:12:10Z — Platform — Scoped to kv-eviction logs; identified eviction storms — link: logs/query?id=evict
2026-02-18T14:25:00Z — SRE — Rolled back kernel update in eu-west-1; began draining replicas — link: deploy/rollback/1234

7. Action item format and tracking (required fields)
- Each action must be entered into the action tracker (JIRA) and referenced in the postmortem using JIRA-ID. Required fields in the tracker: Summary, Owner, Risk/Impact, ETA, Acceptance Criteria, Verification Plan.

8. Customer and status-page messaging templates (short, adjustable by severity)
A) P1 public status-page (initial notice)
- Title: "Service disruption affecting text-generation routes — investigating"
- Body: "We are investigating elevated error rates for text-generation routes (v1). Affected customers may experience 5xx errors. Our engineers have opened a bridge and are actively investigating. We will provide an update by [time]."
- Follow-up updates: factual, timestamped, short; avoid technical blame in public messages.

B) P2 customer email (post-incident summary)
- Subject: "Incident summary: Partial degradation impacting inference latency (YYYY-MM-DD)"
- Body: Short executive summary + impact bullets + what we did to resolve + link to full postmortem page + next steps and contact for support.

C) Enterprise sensitive notice (when legal or customer data exposure is suspected)
- Contact: CSM and Legal must be looped immediately using the enterprise incident escalation matrix. Do not send external text until Legal approves.

9. Postmortem workflow and SLAs
- Owner assignment: The incident commander (IC) assigns the postmortem author within 24 hours of incident closure.
- Draft: Author produces a complete draft within the SLA window (5 or 10 business days depending on severity).
- Peer review: Two reviewers (one SRE, one Product) must provide comments within 48 hours.
- Publication: Postmortem page is published and linked from the incident tracker; exec summary is emailed to leadership and attached to the incident.
- Closure: Postmortem is closed only after all action items have owners and an initial verification test is scheduled.

10. Examples: poor vs. improved phrasing
- Poor: "Alice's deploy broke the cache and caused the outage."
- Improved: "A deploy that modified the cache eviction parameters coincided with increased GC pressure; the change was a proximate factor in exposing an eviction race."

11. Game day specific guidance
- If a game day triggers a postmortem requirement, label the page with the game-day tag and include: exercise hypothesis, injected fault, expected outcome, observed outcome, and recommended changes to the runbook.
- Always include a dedicated section "What behaved as expected" to keep the learning balanced.

12. Publishing checklist (confluence-specific quick steps)
1) Create page under /oncall-and-incident-response/YYYY/MM/ with title format above.
2) Paste executive summary at the top and pin the page to the incident tracker.
3) Add labels: postmortem, incident, severity-P1/P2, owner-[team].
4) Attach artifacts (screenshots, recordings) and add evidence links (do not paste large logs inline).
5) Assign reviewers and request comment turn-around of 48 hours.
6) After reviewers sign off, set status to "published" and notify distribution list.

13. Metrics and dashboards to include in every postmortem (minimum)
- Request volume (qps) and error rate time series for implicated endpoints
- 50/95/99/99.9 latency percentiles for inference routes
- Pod/node-level resource utilization (GPU/CPU/memory) for implicated clusters
- Cache hit ratio and eviction counts (if applicable)
- Autoscaler events and queue length

14. Ownership and escalation for postmortem defects
- If a defect spans multiple teams, the service owner nominates the primary assignee; SRE co-owns verification.
- For regressions that impact customers repeatedly, the head of SRE sponsors an incident review (30-day retro) and may escalate to leadership for additional resourcing.

15. Frequently asked questions (short)
Q: Who writes the postmortem?
A: The IC assigns the primary author; typically an oncall SRE or the service owner.
Q: Can we redact internal names before publishing externally?
A: Yes — redact PII and internal-only notes. Use the public executive summary for customers.
Q: How do I request extended evidence retention?
A: Follow the Incident Evidence Chain runbook and open a legal hold ticket via the runbook link.

Appendix: Quick CLI snippets and pointers
- Export traces for incident window (example: jaeger)
$ jaeger-export --start 2026-02-18T14:00:00Z --end 2026-02-18T15:30:00Z --service inference --output ./traces.json
- Query logs (example using our internal logs service):
$ logs query "service:inference AND status:5xx" --start 2026-02-18T14:00:00Z --end 2026-02-18T15:30:00Z --save evict-logs.ndjson
- Snapshot dashboards: open Grafana panel and click "share -> snapshot"; attach link to postmortem.
