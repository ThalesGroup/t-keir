CISO & Finance Brief: Inference Economics and Security Playbook

Executive summary

This brief is designed for CISO and Finance stakeholders evaluating Redwood Inference for production LLM deployments. It combines a concise economics model with a mapped security and controls playbook to accelerate procurement and risk sign-off. Use this as a one-page narrative for procurement committees and as the operational checklist for pilot-to-production gating.

Key takeaways
- Redwood reduces per-token inference cost through batching, KV/prefix caching, and model quantization profiles while preserving latency SLOs.
- The platform supports hosted, dedicated (reserved GPU pools), and private (VPC/on-prem) models; security controls and evidence differ by deployment mode.
- Finance decisions should balance committed capacity (Dedicated) vs. variable cost (Hosted) based on predictability of token volume and acceptable unit economics.
- CISO review focuses on data flow, key management, audit evidence, and degradation/fallback policies.

1) Decision framework for Finance

Objective: Choose a contracting and deployment model that minimizes expected monthly cost at target latency and uptime while limiting downside risk from traffic spikes or model regressions.

Required inputs (gather before a procurement meeting):
- Monthly average and 95th percentile tokens-per-minute (TPM).
- Required p99 latency target and latency SLO (e.g., p99 < 300 ms).
- Uptime SLO (e.g., 99.9%) and penalty tolerance.
- Data residency and compliance constraints (e.g., customer data must never leave VPC).

Simple pricing calculator (example)
- Hosted: unit = $0.00012 per token (baseline open model), no commit.
- Dedicated: monthly reserved pool = $80,000 / month for 16x A100-equivalent slots -> effective unit cost depends on utilization.
- Private on-prem: one-time license + ops (example: $250k upfront + $35k/year support).

Illustrative scenario (numbers simplified):
- Expected monthly tokens: 1.5 billion.
- Hosted cost: 1.5B * $0.00012 = $180,000/month.
- Dedicated: amortized reserved cost at 70% utilization -> effective unit = $0.00006 -> $90,000/month.
- Recommendation: If expected monthly tokens > 0.8B and predictable (±10%), Dedicated becomes cost-effective; otherwise start Hosted and convert when monthly spend is stable.

Note: Redwood supports step-down during pilot: start Hosted, move to burst-enabled Dedicated commit at 30-day notice. See Pilot checklist.

3) Pilot-to-Production playbook (CFO + CISO checklist)

Phase A — Discovery (week 0–2)
1. Define success metrics: cost per 1M tokens, p99 latency, and false positive/negative rates for safety filters.
2. Provide Redwood with sample traffic profile and data residency requirements.
3. Agree on minimal security evidence pack (SOC2 summary, encryption-at-rest/in-transit proofs).

Phase B — Pilot (week 2–8)
1. Deploy on Hosted in a dedicated tenant with telemetry export to the customer’s observability (optional).
2. Run 2-week load test: measure p50/p95/p99 latency and token throughput.
3. Run quality evaluation against agreed prompt-set; capture regressions.
4. Validate audit log exports, KMS integration, and data retention settings.
5. Finance: track actual spend vs. modeled spend and estimate break-even.

Phase C — Harden and Contract (week 8–12)
1. For Dedicated or Private: draft SOW with capacity, SLA, and support tiers.
2. Security: deliver final evidence pack, run an architecture review with security engineering, and complete pen test timeline (if required).
3. Execute capacity commit or license and schedule rollout.

4) Competitive brief (CISO/Finance lens)

High-level differentiators that matter to CISO & Finance:
- Predictable economics: Dedicated capacity and tooling to forecast unit cost at scale.
- Unified control plane: same operational surface across Hosted/Dedicated/Private reduces integrator burden.
- Observability: token-level cost and latency breakdowns help finance reconcile bills to feature usage.
- Safety hooks and evals: tighter regression detection reduces model-risk remediation costs.

Competitor comparison (summary)
- Provider A (large cloud vendor): strong networking and identity integration but higher per-token unit costs for inference optimized for general CPU/GPU services; procurement and SLA negotiation can take longer.
- Provider B (ML endpoint specialist): lower latency at scale but limited private deployment support and weaker cost-forecasting tools.
- Provider C (open-source runtime): lower license cost but requires substantial ops lift and lacks consolidated audit/sales evidence for enterprise buyers.

How to position to Finance and CISO in a meeting:
- To Finance: show a 12-month cost projection under 3 adoption scenarios (conservative, expected, aggressive). Highlight when Dedicated yields savings and what commit level is required.
- To CISO: present data flow diagram, KMS plan, and how audit/export meets their SIEM ingestion requirements.

5) Pricing guidance and negotiation tactics for AE/SEs

Pricing levers to surface early:
- Committed vs. burst capacity: offer a 6–12 month ramp with tiered commit discounts.
- Multi-year contracts: reduce effective unit price and include price protection for the first 12 months.
- Usage caps and auto-fallback: include contractual run-rate ceilings and automatic fallback to hosted to avoid downtime.
- Observability/Retention add-ons: frequently negotiated (longer retention windows for logs/metrics have incremental cost).

Concession playbook (deal engineering):
1. Trade price for visibility: require the customer to provide monthly usage reports in exchange for a higher commit discount.
2. Offer a pilot credit capped at 3 months usage for qualified proofs.
3. Bundle a security review slot (1-day architecture workshop) as a professional services line item rather than discounting list price.

6) Security FAQ for CISO (concise answers to accelerate RFPs)

Q: Does Redwood retain customer prompts or outputs?
A: By default Hosted keeps ephemeral logs for 30 days for debugging; retention is configurable. Dedicated and Private can be configured to zero retention or to forward logs to customer-managed storage.

Q: How is encryption handled?
A: TLS 1.2+ for in-transit, AES-256 for at-rest. For Private deployments we support HSM-backed keys and customer-managed KMS.

Q: Are third-party model weights stored centrally?
A: Models can be mirrored into private registries under the customer’s control for Private deployments. Hosted uses verified model artifacts stored in Redwood’s secure artifact store with strict access controls.

Q: What compliance evidence is available?
A: SOC2 Type II, ISO 27001 (summary), vendor risk questionnaires, and select penetration-test reports under NDA.

Q: How do you handle incident notifications?
A: Enterprise tiers get a dedicated incident contact, SLA for initial triage (1 hour for Sev 1), and post-incident artifacts.

7) Appendix: SLA and example contractual language

Operational SLOs (example)
- Uptime: 99.9% monthly (Dedicated)
- p99 latency: < 350 ms for <1k token requests (measured under agreed load profile)
- Incident initial response: 1 hour (Sev 1)

Sample contract clause (simple wording for security appendix)
"Redwood will ensure that cryptographic keys used to protect Customer Data are managed according to the Customer's configured KMS. Where Customer elects to use Customer-Managed Keys, Redwood will not have access to plaintext data without Customer authorization. Redwood will provide audit logs and SBOMs upon reasonable request under NDA."
