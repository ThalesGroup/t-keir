Error Budget Recovery: Psychological Safety & Communication Runbook

Summary:

This runbook describes the combined technical and human processes for recovering from an error budget breach or sustained high burn-rate event. It focuses on two often-overlooked elements: (1) structured, rapid technical response to stabilize SLOs and (2) maintaining psychological safety and clear stakeholder communication during and after recovery. The goal is to restore SLOs while preserving team trust and learning.

Scope and audience:
- Scope: public API services and model-serving endpoints within Redwood Inference. Includes hosted, dedicated, and private inference surfaces.
- Audience: SRE oncall, service owners, product managers, incident commanders, support leads.

Key definitions and thresholds (examples used in this runbook):
- SLO baseline: 99.9% successful request rate (p95 latency <= 250ms) per month.
- Error budget: 0.1% allowance per monthly window.
- Warning trigger: error budget burn rate >= 2x sustained for 30 minutes.
- Declare breach: error budget consumed >= 100% for the rolling monthly window.
- Emergency action threshold: burn rate >= 5x OR SLO degradation impacting top customers or SLAs.

Principles:
1) Stabilize first, investigate later. Priority is to reduce user-facing impact before deep root cause analysis.
2) Protect people: prioritize psychological safety and avoid public blame during initial response.
3) Communicate early and often with clear, templated messages.
4) Execute minimal, reversible technical mitigations to regain capacity/failure tolerance.
5) Follow-up with a blameless post-incident review focused on learning and systemic fixes.

Roles and responsibilities (RACI-style):
| Role | Responsibilities |
| Incident Commander (IC) | Declare error budget incident, lead tactical decisions, call for escalation |
| Oncall SRE | Execute runbook steps, implement mitigations, own telemetry updates |
| Service Owner | Decide product tradeoffs (feature gates, throttles), stakeholder comms |
| Product Manager | Customer comms, prioritize mitigation work if feature gating required |
| Support Lead | Triage customer reports, surface high-impact tenants |

Quick-play checklist (first 30 minutes):
1) IC acknowledges incident and posts incident header to #incident-redwood (see templates).
2) Confirm triggers: verify SLO metrics, burn rate, top-affected tenants.
3) Appoint primary SRE and secondary for execution.
4) Run immediate stabilizers (see Stabilization Actions).
5) Enable dedicated telemetry dashboard and increase sampling for traces.
6) Communicate initial internal and customer-facing messages.

Stabilization Actions (technical, prioritized):
- Step A: Activate emergency throttles / priority queueing. Example: set token-priority map to deprioritize background/low-tier traffic. CLI example: curl -XPOST https://api.redwood.internal/v1/traffic/adjust -d '{"policy": "priority-deprioritize: background"}'
- Step B: Enable model-fallback policy to lower-capacity, faster variants (e.g., float32 -> 8-bit quantized) for non-critical routes.
- Step C: Increase autoscale headroom (if capacity available). Kubernetes example: kubectl set resources deployment/model-serving --limits=cpu=3000m --replicas=10
- Step D: Toggle nonessential features off (image generation, heavy reranking) via feature flags.
- Step E: Serve degraded responses with clear client-side headers (X-Redwood-Degraded: true) when graceful degradation is active.

Human and communication playbook (templates and cadence):
- Internal alert (first 10min):
  Subject: [INCIDENT] Error budget breach: <service-name> – IC: <name>
  Body: One-line summary, impact level, immediate mitigations in flight, runbook link, IC and SRE assignees.

- Slack first-line message (post to #incident-redwood and #eng-leads):
  :rotating_light: INCIDENT: <service> SLO breach. Initial mitigations: throttling + fallback active. IC: <name>. Updates every 15m.

- Customer status blurb (public status page):
  We are investigating increased error rates on <service-name>. Some requests may be slower or returned in degraded form. We have activated throttling and fallback paths to minimize disruptions. Estimated next update: 30 minutes.

Psychological safety checklist for ICs and leads:
- Use blameless language: focus on conditions, not individuals.
- Pause non-essential escalations: do not request written explanations from the oncall during active mitigation unless safety/security requires it.
- Reserve post-incident investigative meetings for after stabilization.
- Reassure the team: emphasize that rapid mitigation choices are reversible and prioritized for customer impact reduction.

Decision log (structure to capture during incident):
- Timestamp | Decision | Who | Rationale | Reversible? | Outcome
- Example: 2025-02-10T14:12Z | Enabled priority throttling for free tier | IC Dana | Burn rate 4.2x; top 5 tenants unaffected | Yes | Latency improved by 40%

Longer-term recovery actions (first 24–72 hours):
1) Maintain increased telemetry and error sampling for at least 48 hours.
2) Run capacity replay tests using the captured traffic profile to validate fixes.
3) Schedule a blameless postmortem within 72 hours with invite list: oncall SRE, service owner, PM, one SRE from adjacent team, support lead.
4) Create and track remediation tasks with owners and ETA; tag tasks with label: "slo-recovery-fix".

Post-incident review guidance (structure):
- Objective: determine systemic causes and produce actionable remediation prioritized by customer impact and recurrence risk.
- Agenda: timeline (5–10m), evidence & data (15–20m), decisions recap (10–15m), action items (15m), open questions (10m).
- Required artifacts before the postmortem: raw SLO time series (1s/5s granularity preferred), sample traces for failed requests, policy change logs (feature flags, throttles), capacity allocation charts.

SLO governance for recovery-related changes:
- Temporary SLO relaxation is allowed only with approval from SRE lead and product director and must be timeboxed (max 7 days).
- Permanent SLO changes require: data-driven proposal, risk assessment, updated customer-facing docs, and a planned migration path.

When to escalate to executive comms:
- SLA violation for a paying customer or regulatory breach.
- Multi-region outage impacting >10% of production traffic for >1 hour.
- An incident where legal/compliance or large enterprise customers are affected.

Runbook recovery checklist (compact):
1) IC declared and posted.
2) Primary SRE executing stabilizers.
3) Internal and customer messages posted.
4) Telemetry sampling increased and dashboards standing up.
5) Temporary throttles/feature gates applied and verified.
6) Capacity scaled or model fallbacks activated.
7) On-call handed back with watch period and follow-ups scheduled.

Example retro action matrix (text table):
| Action | Owner | Priority | Due | Status |
| Increase per-tenant rate limits visibility | Service Owner | P1 | 2025-03-05 | Open |
| Implement traffic shadowing to test fallbacks | Eng Platform | P2 | 2025-04-01 | Open |

Telemetry and measurement recommendations:
- Metric set: SLO success rate, burn rate (1h/6h/24h), latency p50/p95/p99, top error categories (5), request volume by tier.
- Set synthetic canaries that run small representative prompts every 30s for model-serving endpoints.
- Retain increased trace sampling for 14 days after incidents.

Templates and example snippets (for quick copy/paste):
- Slack update template: [TIME] Update: mitigation in place (throttling + fallback). Observed: error rate down 30%. Next update in 15m.
- Status page template: We are actively mitigating elevated errors affecting <service>. Degraded responses may be returned. We will provide updates every 30 minutes.

Lessons learned and success criteria: 
- Technical success: SLOs restored to within 1.5x of baseline burn rate within 2 hours.
- Human success: incident participants report feeling supported and heard in the post-incident survey (target: >=80% positive on the "felt supported" question).

Appendix: small CLI & config examples
- Emergency degrade header insertion (nginx):
  proxy_set_header X-Redwood-Degraded "true";

- Example feature-flag toggle (curl):
  curl -XPOST -H 'Authorization: Bearer $ADMIN_TOKEN' https://flags.redwood.internal/v1/toggle -d '{"flag":"rerank-heavy","state":"off"}'

- Simple burn-rate alert query (PromQL):
  (sum(rate(errors[5m])) / sum(rate(requests[5m]))) / 0.001 > 2
