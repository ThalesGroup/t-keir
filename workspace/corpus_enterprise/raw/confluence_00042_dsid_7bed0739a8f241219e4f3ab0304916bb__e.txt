Enterprise Severity Matrix and Customer Communication Playbook

Overview

Purpose
This playbook defines a standardized severity matrix for enterprise customers during onboarding and production, and prescribes customer-facing communication workflows for each severity. The goal is to ensure consistent, transparent, and timely communications while aligning engineering, SRE, and customer success actions with contractual SLAs and business expectations.

Scope
- Applies to Redwood-hosted API, Dedicated, and Private deployments for enterprise customers.
- Covers incidents affecting availability, correctness of outputs, performance regressions, and billing/quotas that materially impact customer business outcomes.
- Does NOT replace product-specific runbooks or legal escalation paths for data breaches (see security-and-compliance incident playbooks).

Definitions and Severity Matrix
Use the following table and criteria to classify customer-impact incidents. Classify on customer-visible impact; escalate by highest impacted customer tier when multiple tenants are affected.

Severity Levels (quick reference)
- P0 (Critical): Customer-facing outage or corrupted responses that prevent core business flows, repeated for >5 minutes, affects 25%+ of requests or one or more high-value tenants.
- P1 (High): Significant degradation in throughput/latency or consistent quality regressions affecting >10% of requests for a specific tenant or features used in production.
- P2 (Medium): Partial feature outage, degraded quality with mitigations available, or single-tenant rate limiting causing customer-visible errors but with fallback.
- P3 (Low): Minor functional issues, intermittent errors, non-urgent quality differences, or documentation gaps.
- P4 (Informational): Non-customer-impacting background issues, upcoming maintenance notices, or low-risk changes.

Severity Scoring Criteria (short form)
1. Impact: % of requests affected or number of customers/tenants impacted.
2. Business Criticality: Revenue at risk, compliance implications, or go-live status.
3. Duration: Time since onset without effective mitigation.
4. Reproducibility: Deterministic vs intermittent.

SLA and Target Responses
- P0: Acknowledge to customer within 5 minutes. Formation of incident bridge with engineering and SRE within 10 minutes. Initial public-facing or customer-facing update within 15 minutes. Target time-to-recover (TTR) depends on root cause; communicate ETA if >30 minutes.
- P1: Acknowledge within 15 minutes. Bridge within 30 minutes. Customer-facing update within 60 minutes and then every 2 hours until resolved.
- P2: Acknowledge within 1 business hour. Customer update within 4 hours and daily status until resolved.
- P3/P4: Acknowledge within 24 business hours. Weekly or as-needed updates.

Owner and Escalation Roster
The following ownership matrix is used when classifying and communicating incidents. Replace names with on-call rotation contact where appropriate.
- First responder: Support Tier 1 (CS Operations)
- Technical owner: SRE / Runtime on-call
- Product owner: Product Manager assigned to the affected feature
- Account lead: Customer Success Manager (CSM) for the tenant
- Executive notify: VP Customer Success (for P0 and repeated P1 on production customers)

Contact escalation paths (order)
1. Support Tier 1 -> 2. SRE on-call -> 3. Runtime engineering -> 4. Product -> 5. VP Customer Success / Head of Eng (as needed)

Operational Checklist by Severity (numbered steps for responders)
P0 (Critical)
1. Classify severity and escalate to SRE immediately.
2. Launch incident bridge (Zoom/Slack/Bridge) and add: Support lead, SRE on-call, runtime eng, product PM, account CSM, and communications.
3. Stabilize traffic: enable fallback model, route to standby region, or activate quota protections. Document actions in the bridge.
4. Craft initial customer-facing update (see templates). Post to customer’s preferred channel (email + support portal ticket + CSM slack).
5. Track metrics and logs: error rate, 95/99th percentile latency, tail drop %, model output error metrics. Attach graphs to updates.
6. Confirm mitigation, run customer verification steps, and coordinate rollback if change-induced.
7. After resolution, run RCA and share timeline within 48 hours and a corrective action plan in the QBR cadence.

P1 (High)
1. Acknowledge and open incident ticket. Notify SRE and product.
2. Attempt quick mitigations (scaling, model fallback, rolling to previous model checkpoint).
3. Provide updates every 2 hours until stable.
4. If unresolved after 6 hours, escalate to executive notify list.

Customer Communication Templates
Use the templates below and customize per tenant (replace tokens in {{braces}}). Always coordinate final messages with the CSM and legal when regulatory language or credits are discussed.

P0 Initial Notification (email/portal)
Subject: URGENT: Incident affecting Redwood Inference API for {{customer_name}} ({{incident_id}})
Body:
Hello {{customer_name}} team,

We are aware of a critical incident affecting {{feature_or_api}} that is impacting production traffic for your account (incident ID: {{incident_id}}). Our on-call engineering and SRE teams have launched a bridge and are actively investigating.

What we know now:
- Time detected: {{iso_timestamp}}
- Observed impact: {{brief-impact-summary}}
- Immediate mitigation actions: {{mitigations}}

Next update: We will provide an update within 15 minutes or sooner. Your Customer Success Manager {{csm_name}} is available at {{csm_contact}} for direct coordination.

We understand the urgency and appreciate your patience.

— Redwood Inference Support

Subject: Notice: partial functionality impacted for {{customer_name}}

Incident Posture and Tone Guidance
- Tone: factual, empathetic, and action-oriented. Avoid speculative technical claims.
- Transparency: If unknowns remain, state what is unknown and planned investigative steps.
- Credits/Compensation: Route any compensation requests to Billing and legal. Do not promise credits in customer message; escalate to finance for approval.

Onboarding-specific Escalations and Go-Live Safeguards
During enterprise onboarding and initial cutover, apply a lower tolerance for regressions and ensure the following:
- Pre-cutover: confirm runbook and contact list, stomping on-call handoff validation.
- Canary phase: require minimum 24-hour monitoring with dedicated telemetry dashboards and synthetic checks for edge-cases.
- Cutover hold gate: no model or config changes that impact the tenant for 72 hours post go-live unless approved by the CSM + SRE.
- Onboarding rapid response team: Onboarding PM, CSM, SRE representative, and runtime eng must be on-call during cutover windows.

Telemetry and Dashboard Recommendations (examples)
- Ingress: request/sec, 50/95/99 latency, HTTP error 5xx/4xx by route.
- Model: token generation rate, kv-cache hit ratio, GPU utilization, OOM events, and per-tenant queue length.
- Quality: perplexity drift, top-1 response divergence from baseline evaluation set, and customer feedback rate.

Example monitoring queries (PromQL-like pseudocode)
- api_errors_total{tenant="{{tenant_id}}"} / api_requests_total{tenant="{{tenant_id}}"} > 0.05
- histogram_quantile(0.95, api_request_duration_seconds_bucket{route="/v1/generate"}) > 1.2

Escalation Communication Channels
- Primary: Customer ticketing portal + dedicated support email.
- Real-time: Slack DM to CSM and customer production channel (if provisioned).
- Bridge: Zoom + pinned Slack incident channel.
- Public status: status.redwood.ai for multi-tenant incidents.

Post-Incident Activities and QBR Integration
1. Within 48 hours: publish timeline, mitigation steps, and next actions to the customer and internal ticket.
2. Within 7 days: share a draft RCA and proposed corrective actions.
3. In next QBR: include incident summary, SLA impact table, and long-term remediations (owners and milestones).
4. For repeated issues: propose permanent routing or quantization changes via Redwood Optimize recommendations and include cost/latency tradeoffs.

Runbook Snippets (copyable checklist for responders)
- Confirm impact and severity.
- Validate customer's on-call and preferred communication channel.
- Collect: logs, trace IDs, example request payloads and model outputs.
- If code/config change suspected: capture deployment IDs and roll back marker.
- For Private deployments: verify network connectivity and customer firewall rules before code rollback.

Templates and Forms
- Incident ticket tags: severity:P0|P1|P2|P3, tenant_id, impacted_feature, psa_required:true|false.
- Customer update subject prefix: [Incident][P{n}] or [Maintenance][Info] for scheduled events.

Appendix: Example short-form verification checklist for customers to run after mitigation
1. Send 5 canonical requests used in production and compare outputs to baseline.
2. Verify 95th percentile latency improvement.
3. Confirm no new 5xx errors for 30 minutes.
4. Confirm billing/quotas unchanged (if relevant).
