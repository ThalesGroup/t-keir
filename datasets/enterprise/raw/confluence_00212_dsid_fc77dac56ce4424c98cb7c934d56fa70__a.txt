ADR: Synthetic-Traffic Privacy and Safe Injection Policy

Summary
-------
This ADR establishes a company-wide policy for generating, routing, and observing synthetic traffic (synthetic requests, test prompts, replayed user traces) injected into Redwood inference stacks. The decision balances the operational need for realistic, continuous testing and monitoring with user privacy, cost containment, and production stability.

Decision
--------
We adopt a conservative, opt-in safe-injection model for synthetic traffic with strong tagging, privacy-preserving data transformations, strict rate & cost budgets, and dedicated isolation lanes where appropriate. Synthetic traffic that includes or is derived from customer data requires explicit approval and additional safeguards (anonymization, differential-privacy transformations, and customer consent).

Motivation and context
----------------------
- Synthetic traffic helps detect regressions, validate rollouts (canaries), exercise fallback paths, and calibrate SLOs and cost models.
- Prior internal incidents: noisy synthetic workloads causing cache pollution and unexpected billing spikes; unclear telemetry attribution between real and synthetic requests; accidental exposure of customer prompt material in debug logs.
- Requirements: preserve ability to run realistic tests; keep production SLAs unaffected; enforce privacy & compliance; make observability and billing clearly attributable.

Scope
-----
Applies to all Redwood runtime modes: hosted API, Dedicated, and Private deployments. Affects teams producing synthetic traffic (QA, SRE, Perf, Product), ingestion/edge proxies, serving runtime, telemetry pipelines, billing, and model evaluation tooling.

Policy details
--------------
1) Tagging and provenance
   - All synthetic requests must include a top-level header/field: X-Redwood-Synthetic: "true" and a synthetic-origin tag with one of the allowed values: {"canary", "perf", "integration-test", "eval-harness", "chaos"}.
   - Telemetry must propagate these tags in traces, metrics (as a cardinality-limited dimension), and logs (redacted).

2) Privacy-preserving transformations
   - If synthetic traffic is derived from customer prompts or logs, it must be transformed by one of the approved methods before injection: token-level anonymization, entity redaction, or differential-privacy noise (per the Privacy Toolkit). Raw customer data is not permitted in synthetic feeds without an approved exception.

3) Isolation and soft lanes
   - Runtime will enforce soft lanes for synthetic traffic: "preferred" (same pools but lower priority) and "isolated" (dedicated worker pools) for heavy perf injection. Routing default: small-volume synthetic -> preferred; large-scale perf/chaos -> isolated.
   - Lanes are implemented via request metadata: synthetic-priority: low|isolated and corresponding queue configurations (see infra changes table).

4) Rate, budget, and cost control
   - Per-team daily budget (tokens and calls) for synthetic traffic. Default soft budget: 1% of team’s peak production tokens or an absolute floor of 50k tokens/day, whichever is smaller.
   - Exceeding budget triggers throttling and an automated escalation to the owning team and SRE. Production customer traffic never yields to synthetic requests when capacity is constrained.

5) Observability and metrics
   - Synthetic-aware SLIs: we add parallel SLIs measuring synthetic success, synthetic-induced error rate, and synthetic latency. Synthetic requests are excluded from customer-facing SLO calculations by default, but we maintain a derived SLO that measures runtime correctness under synthetic load.
   - Tagging keys: synthetic=true, synthetic.origin, synthetic.run_id, synthetic_owner_team.

6) Logging and data retention
   - Logs containing synthetic request content must be redacted of any customer-identifying tokens. Retention for synthetic payloads is limited to 7 days by default and stored in a synthetic-only retention tier.

7) Admission for sensitive synthetic content
   - Any synthetic feed that attempts to include PII, customer prompts, or other sensitive content must gain approval from Security and Privacy (formal ticket), include a transformation proof, and be labeled confidentiality=restricted.

Tradeoffs considered
--------------------
- Pros: preserves ability to run realistic tests in production; improves early detection of regressions; clearer telemetry; controlled cost.
- Cons: added complexity in routing and budget enforcement; risk of synthetic-only blind spots if synthetic lanes diverge from production; small cost overhead for maintaining isolated lanes.

Alternatives evaluated
----------------------
1) No synthetic traffic in production: rejected. Reduced fidelity for rollout and monitor tests deemed unacceptable.
2) Unrestricted synthetic injection: rejected. Real incidents have shown this causes cost spikes and telemetry noise.
3) Total isolation to separate clusters only: rejected for small-scale tests due to increased latency/fidelity loss; accepted for large perf tests.

Implementation plan and rollout steps
-----------------------------------
1) Phase 1 (30 days):
   - Implement tagging enforcement at edge proxies (API gateway) and client SDKs. Add validation: requests missing X-Redwood-Synthetic when flagged by internal tooling are rejected with 400.
   - Add synthetic flags to telemetry pipeline and ensure synthetic dimension cardinality is capped.
   - Owners: Edge (Asha Patel), Telemetry (Liam Chen), SDKs (Priya Nair).

2) Phase 2 (60 days):
   - Implement soft-lane queueing and throttles in runtime. Add budget check in request admission layer with per-team quotas.
   - Owners: Runtime (Maya Rodriguez), SRE (Omar Castillo).

3) Phase 3 (90 days):
   - Add UI in Redwood Console for teams to view synthetic budgets, current consumption, and submit approval requests for sensitive synthetic feeds.
   - Provide SDK helpers for privacy transformations and a CLI utility to generate proof artifacts.
   - Owners: Console (Jin Park), Security/Privacy (Priya Nair).

Infra and config table (high level)
----------------------------------
| Component | Change | Default |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| Edge/API Gateway | Validate X-Redwood-Synthetic header + origin | Reject invalid with 400 |
| Runtime admission | Per-team synthetic token quota + lane hint | 50k tokens/day default |
| Queues | priority queue for synthetic.low and isolated queue | soft-limit 10% of worker capacity |
| Telemetry pipeline | tag propagation + cardinality cap 100 | retention 90 days for metrics |
| Logs | redact synthetic payloads by default | retention 7 days |

API and SDK guidance
--------------------
- SDKs will expose a typed SyntheticRequest builder which sets recommended headers and run_id, and attaches a local annotation proving transformations applied when deriving from customer data.
- Example header: { "X-Redwood-Synthetic": "true", "X-Redwood-Synthetic-Origin": "perf", "X-Redwood-Synthetic-RunId": "perf-20260203-87a2" }
- Clients must not attempt to bypass header validation. Attempts to circumvent will be rate-limited and flagged.

Observability and runbook changes
---------------------------------
- SRE will add synthetic-focused dashboards: synthetic-concurrency, synthetic-error-rate, synthetic-token-consumption-by-team.
- Runbook excerpt: if synthetic traffic causes production error surge, the admission layer will automatically triage and shed synthetic lanes; SRE oncall must: 1) confirm shed; 2) notify affected teams; 3) enable temporary stricter throttles until next business hour.

Testing and evaluation
----------------------
- Unit tests: header validation, budget enforcer.
- Integration tests: end-to-end synthetic run with telemetry propagation.
- Performance tests: validate that small-scale synthetic injection (<0.1% traffic) does not affect p95 latency by more than 1%.

Ownership and governance
------------------------
- Policy owner: Engineering Standards (Maya Rodriguez).
- Runtime & Telemetry implementers: Runtime team and Telemetry team.
- Approval for exceptions: Security & Privacy (Priya Nair) + SRE lead.

Monitoring & SLO impact
----------------------
- Customer-facing SLOs remain computed over non-synthetic traffic.
- New derived SLO: Runtime-availability-under-synthetic (target 99.9% monthly) measured by success rate of canary runs.

Consequences and follow-ups
--------------------------
- Short term: improved detection capabilities and clearer billing attribution.
- Medium term: potential need to tune synthetic generation to stay representative without being costly.
- Follow-ups: add automated differential-privacy transformation library to the SDKs; schedule quarterly review of synthetic budgets.
