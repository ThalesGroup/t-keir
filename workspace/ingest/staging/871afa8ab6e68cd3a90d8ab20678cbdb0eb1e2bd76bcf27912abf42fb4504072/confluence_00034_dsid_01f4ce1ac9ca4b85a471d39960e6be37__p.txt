Post-Incident Action Triage and Continuous Remediation Playbook

Purpose:
This playbook defines a standardized, SLO-driven process for triaging post-incident actions and executing continuous remediation across Redwood Inference services. It focuses on converting incident findings into prioritized, time-boxed work items tied to SLOs, capacity commitments, and automated validation using our operational tooling. The goal is to reduce repeat incidents, lower SLO regression risk, and shorten mean time to fix for systemic causes.

Scope:
- Applies to incidents with customer-facing impact (Pages SEV-2/SEV-1) and any SLO breach event detected by the SLO observability pipeline.
- Covers immediate action triage, remediation planning, rollouts, validation, and runbook/tooling updates.
- Not a replacement for the incident commander or postmortem — this is the actionable remediation workflow that starts at the end of the incident timeline.

Key principles:
- SLO-first prioritization: Actions are prioritized by their expected reduction in SLO burn and customer impact, not by ease of implementation.
- Time-boxed remediation: Every remediation is assigned a remediation class (hot, fast, normal) with target SLAs.
- Evidence-driven verification: Automated, reproducible checks must validate each remediation before and after rollout.
- Continuous improvement: Runbooks and tooling are updated as part of the remediation and are included in the completion criteria.

Roles and responsibilities:
- Incident owner / service owner: Ensures the produced action list is accurate and assigned.
- Remediation owner: Implements the fix, drives rollout and validation.
- SLO steward (SRE): Scores each action against SLO impact and approves prioritization.
- QA / Canary owner: Builds canary tests and validation jobs in the observability pipeline.
- Product stakeholder: Confirms business risk/priority when remediation conflicts with roadmap.

Remediation classification and SLAs:
1) Hot (P0): Immediate engineering change required; target remediation window = 72 hours. Examples: misconfiguration causing sustained SLO breach, runaway process consuming KV cache.
2) Fast (P1): High-reduction action; target window = 30 days. Examples: model quantization profile regression identified; autoscaling rule mis-tuning.
3) Normal (P2): Improvements with clear benefit but lower urgency; target window = 90 days. Examples: telemetry gaps, non-critical refactors.

Triage procedure (step-by-step):
1. Populate the Post-Incident Remediation Board (Jira project: SRE-REM) within 24 hours of incident close. Use the template `SRE-REM - Remediation Template`.
2. For each action, the remediation owner provides: summary, technical cause, estimated effort (T-shirt size), mitigation steps, validation criteria, and rollback plan.
3. SLO steward runs the SLO impact scoring worksheet and assigns an SLO impact score (0-10).
4. Prioritize backlog by: 
   - SLO impact score (desc)
   - Customer exposure (desc)
   - Cross-service blast radius
   - Implementation effort (asc)
5. Assign remediation classification (Hot/Fast/Normal) and set target completion date.

SLO impact scoring quick reference (worksheet excerpt):
- 8-10: Immediate SLO breach or recurring >50% daily burn
- 5-7: Noticeable burn, intermittent customer impact, potential to escalate
- 1-4: Low immediate risk; candidate for batching with other tasks

Action ticket template (required fields):
- Title (short)
- Severity class (Hot/Fast/Normal)
- SLO impact score
- Affected services/components
- Detailed technical summary
- Proposed change (PR/infra change/service config)
- Validation plan (canary + synthetic checks)
- Rollback plan and guardrails
- Estimated effort and owners
- Link to incident postmortem

Validation and automated checks (required before marking remediation complete):
- Canary job in CI/CD that mimics incident traffic profile (minimum 2 repeats).
- Pre/post metric snapshots for SLOs and error budget burn rate. Include: p50/p95/p99 latency, success rate, token-level rejection rates.
- Synthetic test run showing no regression for the relevant prompt sets (if applicable).
- Telemetry ingestion sanity: logs, traces, and sampling for the change must be available for 14 days.

Example validation checklist (table-like):
| Check | Owner | Pass condition | Evidence required |
|--|--|--|--|
| Canary passes under incident-profile load | QA/Canary owner | 0 failures for 1 hour | Canary run ID, logs |
| Latency p99 <= SLO threshold | SRE | p99 <= 1200ms for model-generation svc | Metric snapshot link |
| Error budget burn halts | SRE | Burn rate reduced by >= 60% | Burn-rate graphs |
| No increase in downstream errors | Service owner | Downstream 5xx under baseline | Traces + logs |

Rollout and gating policy:
- All Hot remediations require: canary (10% route), feature-flagged rollout, and automated rollback at 5% error-rate delta or 2x burn-rate.
- Fast remediations require a staged rollout (canary -> 25% -> 50% -> 100%) with 24-hour hold windows and metric checks.
- Normal remediations follow a regular deployment cadence but must include synthetic validation post-deploy.

Runbook and tooling updates (completion criteria):
- Update the affected runbook with a reproducible reproduction section and new remediation steps.
- Add a diagnostic checklist to the runbook that is no longer >3 manual steps without an automated check.
- If the remediation introduced new observability, add dashboards and alerts to the SRE Dashboard catalog.

Capacity and provisioning considerations:
- If remediation touches autoscaling or capacity configuration, include a capacity rehearsal step: simulate expected traffic for 30 minutes at projected peak and validate scaling events.
- Reserve a temporary capacity pool for Hot remediations that require burst testing. Open a capacity request ticket to infra with the tag `sre-capacity-test` and provide expected GPU/CPU and duration.

Incident review linkages and metrics to track post-remediation:
- Time to remediation (TTRem): time between incident close and remediation completion. Target medians: Hot <= 7 days, Fast <= 45 days, Normal <= 120 days.
- Remediation effectiveness: % of remediations that prevent recurrence within 90 days. Target: >= 85%.
- Runbook coverage: % of incidents where runbook was updated as part of remediation. Target: >= 95%.
- Mean validation automation: percent of remediations with automated validation pipelines. Target: >= 90%.

Escalation and arbitration process:
1. If remediation target date will be missed, remediation owner must file an `SRE-REM` update and notify SLO steward within 48 hours with a mitigation plan.
2. If product or legal constraints block a remediation, tag the ticket with `blocked-by-policy` and route to Product or Legal for decision within 5 business days.
3. Arbitration: SRE lead + Product manager + Infra lead decide priority tradeoffs for any Hot remediations that compete for capacity or runway. Decision recorded on the remediation ticket.

Playbook templates and automation hooks (examples):
- Jira template: `SRE-REM - Remediation Template` (fields are enforced via Jira schema).
- CI job: `remediation-canary-test` (parameters: service, profile, run-id).
- Observability job: `slo-impact-snapshot` (outputs a JSON artifact attached to ticket). Example CLI: 
```
$ infra-cli remediation-canary --service model-gen --profile incident-2026-01 --duration 60m --notify SRE
$ observability-cli slo-impact-snapshot --service model-gen --since incident-start --until now --out artifact://sre/12345.json
```

Post-mortem to remediation handoff checklist (immediate tasks after incident close):
- Confirm incident entry in postmortem repo and link (owner).
- Create SRE-REM board entries for all actionable items (owner).
- SLO steward assigns impact scores (within 48 hours).
- Initial prioritization meeting (weekly SRE triage) adds Hot items to the next sprint if not already scheduled.

Reporting and governance cadence:
- Weekly SRE remediation triage meeting: review Hot items, stalled tickets, and capacity conflicts. Attendees: SRE leads, service owners, product liaisons.
- Monthly metrics review: TTRem, effectiveness, and tooling coverage to Engineering leadership.
- Quarterly remediation audit: random sample of remediations audited for evidence, validation artifacts, and runbook updates.

Appendix A — Ticket lifecycle sample (narrative):
- Incident closes on 2026-01-10; postmortem authored and links 4 technical findings. By 2026-01-11, SRE-REM board has 6 tickets.
