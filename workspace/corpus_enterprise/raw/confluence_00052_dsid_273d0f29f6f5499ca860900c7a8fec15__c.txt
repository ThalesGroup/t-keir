Procurement commitment-tiering and revenue-deferment matrix (2026)

Purpose
This playbook defines the standardized commitment-tiering framework used by Procurement, Sales, Legal and Finance to determine (1) procurement approval and purchasing path, and (2) revenue recognition (revrec) deferral and billing treatment for customer-facing commitments (hosted, dedicated, private). It bridges operational procurement thresholds with accounting rules so contract, procurement, and finance teams can act with a single source of truth.

Scope
Applies to all external customer commitments (contracted or purchase-order-backed) and internal purchase obligations created to fulfill those customer commitments. Excludes purely internal non-customer operational purchases (e.g., office supplies). Effective for contracts signed on or after 2026-03-01.

Key definitions
- Commitment: Any customer-facing promise resulting in an expected future cash inflow for Redwood (examples: monthly hosted usage, annual dedicated capacity reservation, private deployment license).
- Tier 1 (Standard): <= $100k expected ARR or <= 12 months term. Low procurement/finance friction.
- Tier 2 (Mid): > $100k and <= $1M expected ARR or multi-year up to 3 years. Requires procurement review & finance prequalification.
- Tier 3 (Major): > $1M expected ARR or multi-year >3 years or includes non-standard data / on-prem controls. Requires executive procurement + legal + finance approval and revised revrec plan.
- Revrec Deferment Window: The period between contract effective date and the date revenue becomes eligible for recognition per accounting policy (e.g., service start, delivery milestone).

Decision matrix (high-level)
| Commitment tier | Procurement approval path | Typical contract clauses | Revenue treatment | Billing behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Procurement auto-approve via self-service catalog | Standard SOW + SLA, standard T&Cs | Recognize upon service delivery; monthly invoicing | Net 30, standard invoices; prepaid allowed for credits |
| Tier 2 | Procurement + Finance checklist sign-off | SOW + limited custom clauses (data residency, minimal indemnity changes) | Defer until capacity reservation is provisioned or service-live milestone | Monthly or quarterly invoicing; offers discounts/credits documented |
| Tier 3 | Procurement exec review + Legal + Finance exec sign-off | Custom commercial terms (on-prem attachments, KMS/HSM, escrow requests) | Complex deferment: revenue recognized by milestone or over performance period; embed amortization schedule in contract metadata | Multi-invoice schedule (initial setup invoice + periodic) or committed-capacity amortized billing; refunds and true-ups require GL-coded adjustments |

Procurement-to-RevRec handoff procedure (owner RACI)
1. Sales captures initial commitment in Salesforce with estimated ARR and expected start date. (Owner: Sales)
2. Sales creates procurement request tagged with sales_opportunity_id and estimated tier. (Owner: Sales; Reviewer: Procurement)
3. Procurement validates tier using the Decision Matrix and assigns approval path. (Owner: Procurement)
4. For Tier 2/3, Procurement routes to Finance for RevRec pre-qualification and to Legal for non-standard terms. (Owner: Procurement; Reviewer: Finance, Legal)
5. Finance returns a RevRec profile with: accounting treatment, expected deferment window, GL accounts, and invoicing cadence. (Owner: Finance)
6. Contract signature triggers automated webhook to Accounting system (internal api: POST /api/v1/contract-hook) carrying contract metadata: {"sales_id": "<id>", "tier": "<1|2|3>", "deferment_days": "<n>", "amortization_schedule_id": "<uuid>"}. (Owner: Platform Engineering; Notifier: Revenue Ops)
7. Finance reconciles first invoice against the RevRec profile and marks initial recognition eligibility. (Owner: Finance)

Note: The webhook payload above is an implementation example. See Appendix B for sample webhook schema and CLI to inspect pending hooks.

Detailed thresholds and finance controls
- Threshold matrix (numeric):
  - Tier 1: estimated ARR <= 100000 USD or contract <= 12 months; Procurement ops auto-approve; RevRec: standard monthly recognition.
  - Tier 2: 100001 USD <= estimated ARR <= 1000000 USD or term 12-36 months; Procurement route to Manager; RevRec: may require deferred recognition until capacity activation; finance must attach amortization schedule.
  - Tier 3: estimated ARR > 1000000 USD or term > 36 months or includes significant non-standard security/data obligations; Procurement route to Director + Legal + VP Finance; RevRec: custom deferral and disclosure; finance creates a contract-specific revenue schedule.

Onboarding checklist for procurement requests (step-by-step)
1. Confirm sales_opportunity_id and attach the signed contract PDF.
2. Populate expected ARR, expected start date, territory, and data residency requirements in procurement form.
3. Tag the request with preliminary tier and upload privacy/security attachments.
4. For Tier 2/3 include capacity plan (eng estimate) and estimated provisioning lead times.
5. For Tier 3, attach risk register and escalation contacts.

Example scenarios (short walk-throughs)
- Scenario A: SMB customer signs a 6-month hosted plan for $12k ARR. Tier 1 path. Procurement auto-approves using standard SOW. Revenue recognized monthly as services delivered. Invoice monthly Net 30.

- Scenario B: Mid-market customer purchases 24-month dedicated pool estimated at $420k ARR with data residency in EU. Tier 2 path. Procurement engages Finance for RevRec pre-qualification; Finance requires deferment until pool is provisioned and issues amortization schedule treating setup fee as one-time recognized upon provisioning.

- Scenario C: Enterprise commits to multi-year (5 year) private deployment with escrow and HSM requirements; total committed value $4.8M. Tier 3 path. Legal negotiates bespoke indemnity and SLAs; Finance and Legal create custom revenue schedule including milestone-based recognition (50% on deployment, 50% ratable over maintenance). Billing will include setup invoice at deployment and quarterly capacity invoices with true-ups.

Escalation and exception handling
- If Procurement disagrees with Sales tiering, Procurement escalates to Procurement Manager within 48 hours.
- If Finance requires a different revrec treatment than Sales expected, Revenue Ops and Finance will document the variance and notify Sales and Customer Success within 3 business days.
- For contract amendments that change the tier or term post-signature, run the Amendment Reclassification flow (Appendix A) and tag the contract with amendment_reason in the contract metadata.

Controls and auditability
- Every procurement request must record the responsible approver and timestamp in WorkdayProcure. Audit trail must include the contract version id and the RevRec profile id.
- Finance runs a weekly reconciliation job that flags mis-matched invoices vs. RevRec profiles (rule: invoice amount variance > 2% or wrong GL mapping). Exception reports are reviewed by Revenue Ops and Closed within 10 business days.

Appendix A — Amendment Reclassification flow (summary)
1. Identify whether the change increases/decreases ARR or term.
2. If change increases the tier (e.g., Tier 1 -> Tier 2), route for re-approval: Procurement + Finance signoff.
3. Capture effective amendment date and create a new amortization schedule if required.
4. Post amendment, Revenue Ops re-evaluates deferred revenue balances and adjusts GL with Journal Entry referencing amendment_id.
