Sourcing-to-Recognition Control Catalog and Procurement Playbook

Overview

Purpose: This playbook defines the end-to-end controls, responsibilities, and step-by-step procedures that govern supplier sourcing, contract execution, procurement approvals, and the mapping of purchased goods/services into Redwood Inference’s revenue recognition and billing workflows (ASC 606 aligned). It is intended for Finance, Legal, Procurement, and Product teams responsible for commercial agreements and billing flows.

Scope: Applies to all external supplier purchases, customer-facing contracts with variable consideration, and implementation agreements that have a direct or indirect impact on revenue recognition or billing (hosted API, Dedicated, Private deployments, add-ons).

Summary of key outcomes:
- Clear approval thresholds for PO/contract sign-off.
- Standard contract clauses to preserve revenue recognition visibility (deliverables, acceptance criteria, termination, credits, change orders).
- Procurement controls to ensure correct GL coding, department chargeback, and budget impact.
- Mapping table from contract taxonomy to revrec accounting treatment and billing triggers.

1) Roles & Ownership

- Finance (Revenue Accounting Manager): final reviewer for revrec treatment; maintains RevRec Catalog.
- Legal (Contracts Counsel): drafts and approves contract language; gatekeeper for non-standard terms.
- Procurement (Head of Procurement): executes POs, vendor on-boarding, and enforces procurement thresholds.
- Product/Eng Owner: certifies scope, acceptance criteria, and milestone readiness for customer/development contracts.
- Billing Ops (Billing Engineer): implements billing cadence, invoice templates, and system integrations.

2) Approval matrix and thresholds (table)

| Action | Approver(s) | Threshold | Additional required doc | SLA to approve |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| Standard PO (stock/services) | Procurement Manager | up to $25,000 | Budget code + vendor W9 | 2 business days |
| Non-standard vendor contract | Legal + Procurement | $25,001 - $250,000 | Legal redline checklist + budget approval | 5 business days |
| High-value strategic contract | VP Finance + GC | > $250,000 | Exec sponsor memo + security review | 10 business days |
| Customer contracts with revenue impact | Revenue Accounting + Legal + Sales | any | Signed SOW + RevRec assessment | 5 business days |

3) Contract language & redline checklist

Required clauses for any agreement that affects billing or revrec:
- Statement of Work (SOW) with explicit deliverables and acceptance criteria.
- Pricing schedule and invoicing cadence (monthly, milestone, usage).
- Credits and refunds: define caps and calculation method.
- Termination and refund mechanics: specify pro rata handling and any reclaim rights.
- Data & security obligations for private deployments (KMS/HSM, audit logs).
- Audit rights and access periods (Finance + Security).
- Change-order procedure and manifestation into SOW + billable schedule.

Redline checklist (must be completed by Legal & Revenue Accounting):
1. Identify performance obligations (explicit list).
2. Determine variable consideration: estimate expected refunds/credits.
3. Confirm billing trigger aligns to recognition trigger (e.g., usage meter, milestone acceptance).
4. Check for material rights or renewal options that create separate performance obligations.
5. Confirm payment terms and recoverability assessment.

4) Procurement workflow (step-by-step)

1. Requisition raised in ERP (fields required: GL account, cost center, project tag, expected delivery date).
2. Budget check automated: requisition blocked if over-budget; Procurement notified.
3. Supplier selection: use catalog supplier if available; otherwise obtain 3 bids for >$50k.
4. Legal review if non-standard terms or if the vendor processes customer data.
5. PO issuance or contract signing. PO must reference the contract and SOW IDs.
6. Goods/services received and formal acceptance recorded (acceptance note required for milestone-based contracts).
7. Invoice routed to AP with PO/contract ID and acceptance evidence.
8. Finance validates GL coding and RevRec Owner confirms recognition mapping if revenue-impacting.

5) RevRec mapping catalog (example excerpt)

| Contract type | Billing trigger | RevRec treatment (ASC 606) | Example GL mapping | Billing owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hosted API subscription (monthly) | Monthly meter report | Recognize over time as satisfied; measure by usage | 4000-HostSub | Billing Ops |
| Dedicated capacity (reserved monthly) | Contract invoice date | Recognize over time ratably across month | 4005-Dedicated | Revenue Accounting |
| Professional services (on-prem install) | Customer acceptance sign-off | Recognize at point in time upon acceptance | 4010-PS | Project Accounting |
| Implementation retainer (non-refundable) | Invoice date with no performance obligation | Recognize when a material right is not conferred; otherwise amortize | 4020-Retainer | Revenue Accounting |

Mapping notes: always attach contract ID and SOW ID to the AR schedule line; revenue accounting must complete a RevRec assessment form (template in attachments) prior to first invoice.

6) Revenue recognition & billing exceptions playbook

Common exceptions and steps: 
- Late acceptance: delay recognition until acceptance recorded. Billing Ops to hold invoice approval; Procurement/Project to provide acceptance evidence within 7 days.
- Variable consideration disputes: estimate using expected value; create contra-receivable bucket and tag adjustment to the month’s close. Notify Sales & Legal for remediation.
- Credits applied post-invoice: create credit memo referencing original invoice and contract clause; Finance posts adjustment and updates RevRec estimate.

7) Accounting journal entry examples (illustrative)

- Monthly subscription recognition (partial month, $10,000 monthly, 10 days delivered):
  - DR Deferred revenue 3,333
  - CR Revenue 3,333

- Professional services recognized on acceptance ($25,000):
  - DR Accounts receivable 25,000
  - CR Revenue 25,000

8) Controls & monitoring

- Monthly RevRec reconciliation: Revenue Accounting to run contract-to-ledger reconciliation and flag variances >1% of monthly revenue.
- Segregation of duties: Procurement issues PO; AP approves invoice; Finance posts recognition.
- Quarterly internal audits: sampling of 20 contracts with revenue impact.
- Dashboard monitors: overdue acceptance items, unbilled receivables >30 days, and change-order aging.

10) Operational runbook: day-of-close checklist (numbered)

1. Run AR aging and collect exceptions.
2. Revenue Accounting runs contract performance schedule and posts period recognition.
3. Billing Ops verifies any usage data mapping for hosted services.
4. Post journal entries and reconcile to subledger.
5. Prepare variance memo for Finance leadership if >1% variance.

Appendix A — Sample contract clause (billing + revrec)

"Billing and Acceptance: Vendor shall invoice Customer monthly in arrears for usage-based services as described in the SOW. Acceptance of deliverables shall occur per SOW criteria; revenue recognition will coincide with the date of acceptance unless otherwise specified. Credits are limited to the lesser of (i) actual unused prepay amounts and (ii) amounts described in the SOW. Refunds initiated for service-level failures shall be applied as credit memos against subsequent invoices."

Appendix B — Frequently asked questions

Q: When does a prepayment convert to deferred revenue versus immediate revenue?
A: If the prepayment is tied to an identified future performance obligation and there are future services to be performed, classify as deferred revenue. If there is no remaining obligation or material right, recognize immediately after evaluating collectability.

Q: Who certifies that an invoice should be held for acceptance?
A: The Product/Project Owner must provide the acceptance note; Procurement flags the invoice and Billing Ops holds payment authorization pending documentation.
