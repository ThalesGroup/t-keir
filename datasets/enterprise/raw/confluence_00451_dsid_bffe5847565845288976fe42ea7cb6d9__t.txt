Flexible Token Bundles & Enterprise Metering: Packaging and Migration PRD

Overview
--------
This PRD describes a new packaging and billing initiative: Flexible Token Bundles (FTB) and Enterprise Metering (EM). The goal is to provide predictable unit economics for mid-market customers while offering usage elasticity for enterprise customers that prefer metered invoicing. This doc covers product requirements, billing model specifics, console and API changes, migration plan for existing customers, rollout milestones, acceptance criteria, and ownership.

Background and motivation
-------------------------
- Feedback from sales and CS: customers want both predictable credits for self-serve teams and a clear metered invoice for enterprise procurement.
- Several pilot customers reported underutilization of large reserved packages and sticker shock for unpredictable burst usage.
- The new offer should reduce billing disputes and accelerate enterprise procurement cycles while preserving PLG motion.

Primary goals
-------------
1) Deliver a Flexible Token Bundles product that:
   - Allows customers to purchase bundles of tokens with optional auto-topups.
   - Supports token expiration controls and enterprise-level rollover rules.
2) Deliver Enterprise Metering that:
   - Produces a detailed per-route/per-model invoice with daily aggregation and line items suitable for enterprise procurement.
   - Supports minimum monthly commitments and credit reconciliation.
3) Minimize migration friction for existing reserved-capacity and credit customers.
4) Ensure clear SLO/usage behavior mapping so customers can predict latency & cost tradeoffs.

Success metrics (first 6 months)
--------------------------------
- 20% of ARR-new bookings via FTB or EM in Q4 post-launch.
- 30% reduction in billing-support tickets related to usage disputes for pilot accounts.
- 10% increase in retention for customers who convert from one-off credit purchases to FTB.

Customer personas
------------------
- Growth-startup engineer (self-serve): wants simple bundles, reasonable rollover, clear console UX.
- Platform/ML team at mid-market: wants predictability, invoiceable commitments, and console telemetry.
- Enterprise procurement: needs per-day metered line items, signed contract SKUs, and flexibility for overage handling.

Product requirements (must-have)
--------------------------------
1) Purchase experience
   - Web console flow: choose bundle size (10M / 100M / 500M tokens), optional auto-topup threshold (10% / 20% / 50%), and rollover policy (none / 90-day / unlimited up to cap).
   - API/CLI endpoint to purchase bundles: POST /v1/billing/bundles {"bundle_tier": "100M","auto_topup": "20%","rollover": "90d"}.
2) Token accounting
   - Tokens consumed are deducted from bundles first, then charged as metered usage if auto-topup disabled.
   - Bundles are consumed per-customer (org-level) with per-route attribution in telemetry.
3) Enterprise Metering invoicing
   - Daily aggregation of usage by: date, model, route, region, effective price tier.
   - Produce invoice-ready CSV and JSON payload that maps to procurement-friendly line items.
4) Contract and minimum-commit support
   - Allow sales to create contract-backed pricing with a minimum monthly commitment and overage multiplier (e.g., 1.2x list price).
5) Console visibility
   - New Billing > Bundles dashboard with: remaining tokens, rollover window, projected burn rate, alerting thresholds, and next auto-topup ETA.
6) Billing & Finance controls
   - Audit trail for bundle purchases and auto-topups (who, when, payment method, invoice id).
   - Support for manual credit adjustments and reconciliations.
7) Security and compliance
   - Preserve existing PII/tokenization of payment methods; support export of billing artifacts with retention aligned to finance policy (7 years).

Non-functional requirements
---------------------------
- Availability: billing API 99.9% monthly uptime.
- Latency: bundle purchase endpoint 95th percentile < 300ms for authenticated requests.
- Data freshness: daily metering export must be available by 06:00 UTC for the previous day.

Design: billing models and examples
----------------------------------
- Flexible Token Bundle (FTB) structure
  - SKU: ftb-10m, ftb-100m, ftb-500m
  - Unit price example: ftb-100m = $650/mo (base example), per-token effective price = $0.0000065
  - Auto-topup: when remaining tokens <= threshold (e.g., 20%), system creates topup purchase for same SKU and posts an invoice.
  - Rollover: unused tokens flow into a 90-day expiry pool, then expire. Customers can upgrade to unlimited-rollover capped bundles for enterprise SKUs.

- Enterprise Metering (EM) structure
  - Daily line items aggregated by model+route.
  - Sample CSV schema (text table):
    date,model,route,region,tokens_consumed,effective_price_per_token,amount_usd,contract_id
    2026-08-01,redwood-v2-base,chat,us-west-2,1,200,0.000007,0.0074,CTR-12345

Pricing examples and overage behavior
------------------------------------
- If an organization has a 100M FTB and consumes 120M tokens in the billing cycle:
  1) If auto-topup enabled: system purchases a 100M topup at list price at the moment of threshold trigger (creates invoice).
  2) If auto-topup disabled: excess 20M charged as metered usage at the per-token overage rate (e.g., 1.0x or 1.2x contract multiplier).

Console and API changes (developer-facing contract)
----------------------------------------------------
- New API endpoints:
  - POST /v1/billing/bundles -> create bundle purchase
  - GET /v1/billing/bundles/{bundle_id} -> bundle status and remaining tokens
  - GET /v1/billing/metering?start=2026-08-01&end=2026-08-07 -> daily aggregation export
- Console additions:
  - Billing > Bundles page: purchase flow, bundle list, auto-topup settings, manual topup button, CSV export.
  - Alerts: webhook and email notifications for 10% remaining, topup success/failure.

Telemetry, analytics and instrumentation
---------------------------------------
- Metrics to emit: bundle_tokens_remaining{org}, bundle_auto_topups_total, bundle_rollover_expired_total, metered_daily_tokens{org,model,route}.
- Events should include invoice_id, purchase_method, and contract_id for EM customers.
- Dashboard updates: per-org burn-rate forecast (7-day), top 5 routes by token consumption, and reconciliation view for finance.

Migration and coexistence strategy
----------------------------------
1) Existing credit customers (one-off credits): offer opt-in to convert credits -> FTB at pro-rated rates.
2) Reserved capacity customers (Dedicated): expose a bridge where dedicated commit remains but can optionally enroll in EM reporting for consolidated billing.
3) Phased migration: pilot with 10 customers for 8 weeks; collect feedback and iterate.
4) Data migration actions: create bundle records that reflect existing credit balances; mark legacy credits as deprecated but readable in console for 90 days.

Rollout plan and milestones
---------------------------
- Phase 0 (Aug 2026): API design finalized, mockups complete, legal & finance alignment.
- Phase 1 (Sep 2026): Implement backend accounting, basic console UX, internal pilot with finance and CS.
- Phase 2 (Oct 2026): Customer pilot (10 customers), automatic daily exports to finance, sample invoices.
- Phase 3 (Nov 2026): Public beta for new signups, limited self-serve purchase.
- Phase 4 (Dec 2026): GA for FTB; EM GA early 2027 after invoicing edge-cases resolved.

Acceptance criteria
--------------------
- Functional: create bundle, consume tokens from bundle, trigger and process auto-topup, export daily EM CSV, and generate invoice-ready files.
- Non-functional: bundle purchase latency < 300ms p95, metering export available by 06:00 UTC daily, billing API 99.9% uptime.
- Operational: pilot accounts report <= 2 billing discrepancies in the 8-week pilot window.

Security, compliance, and finance controls
-----------------------------------------
- All billing artifacts stored in finance S3 with restricted access.
- Audit log entries for any credit adjustments; require two-person approval for adjustments > $5,000.
- Support for SOC2-required retention of billing logs and exportability for audits.

Open questions and tradeoffs
---------------------------
1) Topup granularity: should topups be same-SKU only or allow mixed smaller increments? Tradeoff: UX simplicity vs. flexibility.
2) Rollover policy complexity: unlimited rollover increases liability and accounting complexity.
3) Overage multiplier: fixed vs. tiered by contract size. Finance prefers tiered multipliers.
