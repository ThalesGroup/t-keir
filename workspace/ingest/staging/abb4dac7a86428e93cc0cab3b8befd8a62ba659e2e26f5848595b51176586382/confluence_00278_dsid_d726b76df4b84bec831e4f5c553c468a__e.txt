Entitlement Deprecation and Rollforward: Evidence & Operational Guideline

Overview
This playbook defines the operational and audit-evidence requirements for entitlements (roles, permissions, feature flags) that are scheduled for deprecation and the controlled 'rollforward' process used to reintroduce or replace entitlement sets after a rollback. It consolidates RBAC change controls, audit logging expectations, data residency considerations, and secure-SDLC gates into a single reference for security, IAM, SRE, and compliance teams.

Audience
- IAM engineers
- Security & compliance reviewers
- SRE and platform operators
- Product owners proposing entitlement changes

Goals
1) Ensure every entitlement deprecation has an immutable, auditable evidence package that satisfies SOC-like audits.
2) Provide deterministic, tested steps for rollforward after a rollback or staged canary failure.
3) Minimize blast radius and preserve data residency and retention guarantees during entitlement state changes.

Scope
- Applies to all user-facing and service-level entitlements managed in Redwood's central RBAC service, FeatureFlag service, and platform policy engine (OPA + policy registry).
- Not applicable to ephemeral developer-only toggles that are explicitly labeled "dev-only" and exist for <72h.

Terminology
- Deprecation: planned removal of an entitlement from a policy set or role mapping.
- Rollforward: re-application of a new or adapted entitlement set after an unsuccessful removal or after a rollback window expires.
- Evidence package: collection of artifacts produced and stored to satisfy audit and legal requirements for the change.

Owners & Roles
- Change proposer: Product / Feature owner who requests deprecation (must attach a business justification).
- IAM lead: reviews technical impact and determines mapping/alternatives.
- Security reviewer: evaluates compliance and data-residency impact.
- SRE operator: schedules deployment into policy registry and runs canary logic.
- Compliance custodian (Legal/Compliance): certifies evidence package and retention schedule.

Deprecation Criteria (must be satisfied before a deprecation ticket is accepted)
1) Business justification documented and signed by product manager.
2) Migration path for all current consumers (e.g., role mappings, client SDK versions) with timeline.
3) Backward-compatibility window defined (min 14 days for customer-impacting entitlements).
4) Risk assessment rating: Low/Medium/High with mitigations enumerated.
5) Required approvals: IAM lead + Security + Compliance for High-risk items.

Evidence Package (minimum artifacts)
| Artifact | Purpose | Retention |
|---|---|---|
| Change ticket (JIRA ID) | Source of truth for approval trail | 7 years |
| PR + config diff | Exact policy diff applied to registry | 7 years |
| Signed approval email/attestation | Business & compliance signoff | 7 years |
| Pre-change policy snapshot (hash & export) | Baseline for rollback | 7 years |
| Post-change policy snapshot (hash & export) | Confirmation of final state | 7 years |
| Audit log stream slice (covering 72h pre and 72h post) | Access events and enforcement evidence | 3 years (security logs), 7 years (for regulated customers) |
| Canary runbook & result artifacts | Proof of staged validation | 1 year |
| Rollforward test artifacts (integration and e2e) | Demonstrates re-introduction works as expected | 1 year |
| Data-residency impact memo | Notes if policy change crosses residency boundaries | 7 years |
| Cryptographic checksum (SHA256) of evidence bundle | Tamper-evidence | 7 years |

Evidence Packaging & Submission
- All artifacts must be bundled into an evidence archive using the compliance tooling (cli: redwood-evidence pack --ticket JIRA-1234). The archive must include a manifest.json (flat key:value list) and a SHA256 checksum signed by the change proposer.
- Upload location: secure evidence bucket encrypted with customer HSM or Redwood KMS depending on sensitivity. Access to the evidence bucket is controlled by a timebound temporary token and logged to the audit pipeline.

Audit Logging Expectations
1) Every RBAC policy change must emit: policy_id, actor_id, actor_role, change_diff (compact diff), timestamp, request_id.
2) Enforcement events must include: decision_id, request_id, evaluated_policies, allowed/denied, evaluated_at.
3) Logs must be copy-forwarded to the immutable audit store with a 72-hour cold window and then to long-term retention per table above.
4) For cross-border customers, log copies must obey data residency constraints — see Data Residency section.

Data Residency & Retention Considerations
- If the deprecation affects entitlements whose evaluations store or reference customer data in a specific region, the evidence package must include residency mapping and proof that any exported logs respect export controls.
- Retention tiers: security logs (3 years), compliance-critical artifacts (7 years), ephemeral canary artifacts (1 year). Exceptions must be requested via the risk-and-exceptions workflow.

Secure SDLC Gates (integration with change pipeline)
1) Pre-merge checks: automated policy linter, policy unit tests, and a change-impact simulator must pass.
2) Merge blocking requirements: ticket must have required approvals embedded in PR metadata.
3) Deployment pipeline: staged rollout to policy-registry-canary -> policy-registry-staging -> production with automated rollback hooks.

Rollforward Procedure (high level)
1) Trigger: Can be initiated after a rollback or when a re-introduction is required. Authorization: requires IAM lead + Security approval for High-risk items.
2) Fetch pre-change snapshot and confirm integrity via SHA256.
3) Run policy simulator against current traffic snapshot and target environment.
4) Run canary in a non-customer-impacting namespace for 24 hours (or per canary SLA).
5) Observe enforcement metrics: deny-rate, latency impact on auth calls, error-rate on dependent services.
6) If metrics within acceptance criteria, promote to staged rollout with 10/30/60% traffic buckets.
7) Produce post-rollforward evidence package and upload.

Rollforward Quick Checklist (operators)
1) Confirm ticket and approvals.
2) Verify pre-change snapshot exists and checksum matches.
3) Execute redwood-policy apply --snapshot <id> --dry-run and validate simulator output.
4) Start canary roll with redwood-deploy --policy <id> --canary
5) Monitor dashboards: auth-latency, auth-error-rate, policy-evaluation-duration, enforcement-deny-rate.
6) On success, promote; on failure, reapply previous snapshot and trigger incident workflow.

Acceptance Criteria & KPIs
- Canary window success: 0% customer-facing errors attributable to policy change in canary window.
- Authorization latency: no >20% increase in median auth decision latency in production window after promotion.
- Evidence completeness: all artifacts present and manifest signed before change is marked complete.

Incident Response Links & Escalation
- If rollforward leads to unexpected customer impact or a potential data residency breach, open an incident and tag severity=sev2 or higher. Follow the Incident Evidence Orchestration playbook for artifacts aggregation and legal notification timelines.
- Contacts: IAM lead (pager oncall), Security oncall, Compliance duty officer.

Example CLI Commands
- Export current policy snapshot:
  redwood-policy snapshot export --policy-id=aws-role-access --out=aws-role-access-snap-20270210.json
- Apply snapshot in dry-run simulator:
  redwood-policy apply --snapshot=aws-role-access-snap-20270210.json --dry-run --simulator=production-traffic-20270210
- Pack evidence:
  redwood-evidence pack --ticket=JIRA-1234 --artifacts=./evidence/* --out=JIRA-1234-evidence.tar.gz

Testing & Validation Matrix (short)
| Test type | Owner | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Unit policy tests | IAM engineer | No rule regressions, coverage >= 90% |
| Integration simulator | SRE | No false-deny on known-good request corpus |
| Canary e2e | Product + SRE | No critical errors, metrics within thresholds |

Risk & Exceptions
- If a required artifact cannot be produced (e.g., legacy system does not export a policy snapshot), teams must file a risk exception with a compensating control (extended monitoring, reduced blast radius, written attestation). Exceptions are approved by the Risk Review Board and recorded in the central exceptions registry.
