Overview

This document collects company-wide procedural guardrails and ready-to-use templates for the following operational domains: access & permissions, change management, data management, procurement & expenses, vendor onboarding and offboarding, travel & expense policy, and common internal templates. It is a lightweight canonical reference intended for teams to adopt consistent, auditable procedures while allowing tactical deviations when justified and documented.

Principles

- Least privilege by default: access requests should grant the minimum set of permissions required and explicitly timeboxed where practical.
- Separation of duty: approval chains should separate requester, approver, and deployer roles for sensitive actions.
- Auditability: every approval, change, and procurement must be recorded with rationale and links to artifacts.
- Risk-based exemption: exceptions are permitted but must be logged, short-lived, and reviewed by the relevant steward.
- Reusability: teams should prefer company templates and mechanized workflows to reduce ad-hoc variation.

Scope

Applies to: all Redwood employees, contractors, and vendor personnel interacting with Redwood systems, infrastructure, or buying goods/services on behalf of Redwood.

1) Access & Permissions

Who owns this: Identity & Access working group (primary owner: Security); Operational steward: IT Ops (secondary owner).

Key guardrails:
- Provisioning model: All privileges are assigned through approved roles in the identity provider (IdP). No permanent manual changes in production IAM consoles without a change request.
- Temporary elevated access: Requests for time-limited elevation must use the Access Request form and specify justification, start/end times, and verification steps. Default max duration: 8 hours for standard engineers; 72 hours for approved emergency escalations.
- Quarterly entitlement review: Team managers must attest to role memberships quarterly. Failure to attest triggers automated deprovisioning for accounts with no owner.
- Service accounts: Must be scoped, rotate credentials every 90 days (or use short-lived tokens), and list owner and consumer services.

Access Request process (summary):
1. Requester files the Access Request template (see template A).
2. Team owner verifies business justification and assigns role(s).
3. Security runs policy checks (sensitive-data flag, cross-account access).
4. Access is granted with an expiry and logged.

Access Request template (short):
- Requester name
- Requester email and team
- Resource / role requested
- Justification and scope
- Requested start / end
- Approver name
- Risk assessment checkbox (contains-PII, production-impact)

2) Change Management

Who owns this: Platform & SRE (primary), Change Approvals Committee (CAC) (governance).

Guardrails and approvals:
- Change windows: Routine changes should go through the weekly maintenance window or be coordinated via the change calendar. Emergency changes must follow the emergency change flow.
- Categories:
  - Standard: Pre-approved low-risk changes (config tweaks, library upgrades to allowed ranges). No CAC approval required; must be tracked in the change log.
  - Normal: Requires documented RFC and two approvers (team lead + SRE on-call). Schedule and rollback plan required.
  - Emergency: Implement immediately if needed to restore service. Post-implementation retrospective due within 48 hours.
- Rollback policy: Every normal or emergency change must include an automated rollback plan and a clear success metric.
- Change window SLO: Normal change approval target: 72 hours. CAC quorum response target: 5 business days for non-critical items.

Normal change checklist:
1. RFC created with scope, impact matrix, and rollout plan.
2. Automated tests and canary steps described.
3. Deployment owner and rollback owner specified.
4. Observability hooks (dashboards, alerts) added.
5. Post-deploy verification steps and remediation path documented.

3) Data Management

Who owns this: Data Governance (primary), Security & Legal (secondary).

Guardrails:
- Classification: All datasets must be labeled using the company data classification: Public, Internal, Confidential, Restricted.
- Retention: Default retention for user-facing telemetry: 365 days; embeddings and model artifacts: 730 days unless otherwise approved.
- Encryption: At-rest encryption required for Confidential and Restricted data; in-transit TLS required for all data flows.
- Access approval: Any request to access Confidential or Restricted data requires a documented business case and approval from the Data Steward.
- Anonymization & pseudonymization: For analytics use-cases where PII is not required, teams must apply approved anonymization transformations and provide verification tests.

Data handling checklist for projects:
- Data classification asserted in project brief.
- Data minimization note: list attributes captured and retention rationale.
- Data flow diagram attached (S3 buckets, DBs, access patterns).
- Compliance flags (GDPR, CCPA, sector-specific) annotated.

4) Procurement & Expenses

Who owns this: Finance (primary), Procurement (operational owner).

Thresholds and approvals:
- Low-value (<= $5,000): Team lead approval suffices. Can be charged to team budget via standard PO/expense flow.
- Mid-value ($5,001 - $50,000): Requires Procurement review and Finance sign-off. Vendor risk questionnaire required.
- High-value (> $50,000): Requires legal review, senior finance approval (Director+), and procurement negotiation. Contract template must be used.
- Recurring subscriptions: Subscription renewals > $12,000/yr require Procurement to evaluate alternatives annually.

Procurement process (summary):
1. Requestor completes Procurement Request template with justification and budget code.
2. Procurement runs sourcing, receives 1–3 quotes, and runs vendor risk screen.
3. Finance approves spend and issues PO.
4. Receipt and invoice matched in the accounting system; expense coded.

Expenses policy highlights:
- Travel per diems and caps: Domestic meals cap $75/day; international country-specific caps documented on the Travel page.
- Personal credit card reimbursement requires receipts and project code.
- Approval window: typical expense approvals processed within 10 business days.

5) Vendor Management (Onboarding & Offboarding)

Who owns this: Vendor Management (Procurement), Security for technical onboarding.

Onboarding guardrails:
- Vendor risk classification: Low, Medium, High based on access scope and data sensitivity.
- High-risk vendors (access to Confidential/Restricted data or production systems) require: security questionnaire, SOC2 or equivalent evidence, penetration test summary, and contractual security clauses.
- Minimum required docs: Statement of Work (SOW), Master Services Agreement (MSA) or PIA for sensitive vendors, point of contact, SLAs.

Onboarding checklist (high level):
1. Business sponsor identifies vendor and completes Vendor Intake form.
2. Procurement runs vendor screening and assigns risk level.
3. Security performs technical review and approves access model.
4. Contracts & Legal finalize MSA/SOW.
5. IT provisioning and onboarding with least privilege.

Offboarding steps:
- Revoke access (IdP, cloud IAM, service accounts).
- Collect and sanitize data if required (data return/destruction clause).
- Terminate billing and close PO.
- Run an offboarding verification checklist and file in vendor record.

6) Travel & Expense (T&E) Policy Highlights

- Booking: Employees should use the corporate travel portal for air and hotels where available. Exceptions must be pre-approved.
- Air travel: Economy for trips < 6 hours; premium economy or business requires Director-level approval.
- Hotel standards: Mid-tier business hotels; upgrades only with pre-approval.
- Ground transport: Preferred use of rideshare or rental car per cost comparison; mileage reimbursed at the published rate.
- Expense submission: Submit within 30 calendar days with receipts. Corporate cards auto-reconcile monthly.

8) Exception & Escalation Patterns

- Short-lived exceptions (up to 7 days) may be approved by the team lead + security reviewer with a documented mitigation.
- Medium exceptions (8–90 days) require cross-functional exception log entry and approval by Data Governance (for data exceptions) or Procurement + Legal (for vendor/contract exceptions).
- Long exceptions (> 90 days) must be reviewed quarterly by the relevant governance committee.

Escalation contacts (primary):
- Security triage: security-triage@redwood.example.com
- Procurement desk: procurement@redwood.example.com
- Finance escalations: finance-ops@redwood.example.com

9) Governance & Review Cadence

- Quarterly reviews: Entitlements, vendor risk register, and top-10 procurement commitments.
- Annual review: Data retention defaults, procurement thresholds, travel caps, and templates.
- Continuous improvement: Teams encouraged to propose template or process changes via the Process Change RFC; reviewers: Finance, Security, and People Ops.

Appendix: Quick checklists (copy into ticket or RFC)

- Pre-deploy quick-check for normal change:
  1. Has a rollback been defined? Y/N
  2. Are metrics and dashboards enabled? Y/N
  3. Owner reachable for 2 hours post-deploy? Y/N
  4. Stakeholders informed? Y/N

- Vendor intake red flags:
  - No SOC2 / equivalent for high-risk vendor
  - Refusal to accept data-handling clauses
  - Unknown billing model or recurring auto-charges without transparency
