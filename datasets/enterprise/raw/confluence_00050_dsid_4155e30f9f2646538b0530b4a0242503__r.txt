Responder Sprint: Incident Orchestration, Runbook Hygiene & Rotation Guidelines

Summary:
This document defines the "Stability Sprint" cadence: a recurring, team-scale program combining incident orchestration improvements, runbook hygiene, on-call rotation changes, game-day exercises, and standardized customer communications. The goal is to reduce mean time to mitigate (MTTM), harden our runbook coverage, and keep on-call load sustainable while tightening SLO observability and customer trust.

Scope and intent:
- Scope: company-wide on-call responders, SRE, serving-runtime teams, platform, and customer success communications owners.
- Intent: provide a prescriptive, repeatable sequence to run a two-week Stability Sprint; include triage ladders, runbook maintenance checklist, rotation adjustments, game-day plan, postmortem template, and status page / customer communication templates.

Roles & responsibilities:
- Sprint Lead (rotation): coordinates the sprint logistics, schedules game-day, consolidates feedback. Typical owner: rotation engineer on SRE.
- Runbook Custodians: service owners responsible for updating and validating runbooks in that sprint.
- On-call Captain: manages short-term schedule changes and escalations during the sprint.
- Communications Lead: drafts status page and customer notices when required.
- Observability Owner: ensures SLO dashboards and alert thresholds are available and annotated for the sprint.

Cadence (two-week example):
1) Planning week (Mon–Wed): inventory runbooks, assign custodians, review recent incidents and pain points.
2) Hygiene week (Thu–Fri + next Mon–Tue): runbook edits, test playbook steps in a staging ‘ops-lab’.
3) Game-day (Wed): simulated incident(s) executed end-to-end, followed by a short debrief.
4) Postmortem & Rollout (Thu–Fri): postmortem writeup, remediation tasks created, rotation policy tweaks applied.

Triage decision ladder (quick reference):
1) Detection: alert fires (pager, slack alert).
2) First response (< 5m): acknowledge, capture initial context (service, region, incident id).
3) Primary mitigation (< 15m): apply runbook primary mitigation steps. If not effective, escalate to Secondary Responder.
4) Service fallback (< 30m): activate configured fallbacks (circuit-breakers, degraded mode).
5) Cross-team bridge (> 30m): open incident bridge, involve SRE, platform, and product SMEs.
6) Customer comms & status page (when customer impact > partial degradation for 15m).

Runbook lifecycle & hygiene checklist:
- Minimum contents for each runbook (all items must be present):
  - Runbook title and service owner (name + slack handle).
  - Pager conditions that trigger runbook.
  - Clear, ordered mitigation steps with expected outcome and rollback steps.
  - Telemetry dashboard links and query snippets (PromQL/SQL).
  - Post-execution verification checklist (what signals confirm success).
  - Known failure modes and prior incident links.
  - Safety and customer-communication thresholds.
- Hygiene checklist items:
  - Verify CLI commands run in ephemeral test environment within 48 hours of edit.
  - Validate that SLO thresholds referenced are present on the team dashboard.
  - Confirm secrets/credentials referenced are fetched from KMS (no inline tokens).
  - Add automated smoke tests to CI for critical mitigation steps (when applicable).
  - Tag runbook with last-reviewed date and reviewer initials.

Runbook example (inference-router service):
- Trigger: Pager when 5xx rate > 2% for 5m AND p95 latency > 750ms for 5m.
- Primary mitigation:
  1) Confirm alert: kubectl get pods -l app=inference-router -n prod; note crashlooping pods.
  2) Check replicas: kubectl describe deploy inference-router -n prod | grep Replicas
  3) If pod OOMs observed, scale down worker concurrency: kubectl set env deploy/inference-router WORKER_MAX_CONCURRENCY=8
  4) Validate health: run health-check script: ./scripts/check-inference-healthy.sh --env prod
- If step 3 fails, fallback: revert to canary model routing: POST /internal/route?target=canary (requires runbook token from Vault)

Runbook testing notes:
- Playbook steps must be exercised in the ops-lab staging namespace at least quarterly. Record the run and attach timestamps and logs to the runbook page.

On-call rotation health and sustainable load policy:
- Target on-call load: 1.0 P50 alerts per shift for SRE; escalate if weekly average > 1.8.
- Max consecutive primary on-call shifts: 5 weekdays (exception must be approved by On-call Captain).
- Backup coverage: two-week overlap policy when senior engineer transitions off rotation.
- Timeboxed deep-work windows: block two non-overlapping 3-hour windows per week for responders (protected time).

Game-day design & checklist:
- Frequency: quarterly for platform-wide, monthly for high-risk services.
- Pre-game requirements: runbooks assigned and tested; monitoring queries validated; synthetic traffic baseline captured.
- Scenario creation guidance: use recent near-miss incidents to design realistic simulations. Tag scenarios as either 'degradation' or 'outage'.
- Execution steps:
  1) Declare game-day incident and set SAFE marker on status page (internal).
  2) Execute fault injection (traffic spike, node removal, model rollback).
  3) Observe and follow runbook steps; record timestamps for each step.
  4) Capture RCF (Recovery Completion Factor) = minutes to mitigation / expected mitigation time.
  5) 1-hour post-mortem debrief; create follow-up JIRA tasks for runbook gaps.

Postmortem format (required for P1/P2 or customer-impacting events):
- Title: [Service] — Short summary — YYYY-MM-DD
- Summary: one-paragraph executive summary (what happened, impact, status).
- Timeline: concise, timestamped events and decisions (UTC).
- Root cause analysis: causal chain, not a single sentence.
- Contributing factors: people, process, technology.
- Resolved actions: list of immediate fixes and owners with due dates.
- Long-term improvements: planned, prioritized items with owners and ETA.
- Blameless review notes and learning items.
- Postmortem acceptance: approver and date.

Customer communications & status page templates (examples)
- Short-form status update (for partial degradation):
  Title: Service degradation: inference API latency increased
  Body: We are seeing elevated latency for the inference API in us-west-2 impacting some requests. Engineers are investigating; mitigations are in progress. We will post an update by 15:45 UTC or sooner. Impact: increased latency, some requests may time out.
  Owner: @communications-lead

- Long-form customer notice (outage declared):
  Subject: Incident [INC-1234] — Inference API outage — {start-time} UTC
  Greeting: Hello — we want to share a summary of an ongoing incident affecting inference features.
  Impact: Customers using hosted inference in us-west-2 may experience complete request failures or elevated latencies.
  What we are doing: Our SRE and product teams have opened a bridge, applied routing fallbacks, and are rolling a targeted fix. We are prioritizing full restoration and will provide timeline estimates.
  Next update: {time + 30 min}
  Contact: support@redwood.ai and tenant-status@redwood.ai (for escalations).
  Note: This template should be adapted to include mitigation steps when resolved and a pointer to the formal postmortem.

Operational metrics & SLO tie-ins:
- SLO examples to annotate in runbooks:
  - Availability SLO: 99.95% monthly for API v1.
  - Latency SLO: p95 < 300ms for inference router on standard workloads.
  - Error budget policy: if monthly error budget consumption > 40%, schedule an emergency Stability Sprint and freeze non-essential changes.

Alert tuning guidance (practical rules):
- Alert fatigue limit: keep only actionable alerts. For every alert evaluate: does it require action? If not, convert to dashboard.
- Multi-signal alerts: prefer composite alerts (e.g., high latency + increased 5xx) to reduce false positives.
- Pager escalation: primary (5m), secondary (15m), captain (30m). Escalation steps must be encoded in PagerDuty rules.

Automation & tooling recommendations:
- Use automation to gather context on alert (attach recent deployment id, git commit, node metadata).
- Slack bridge templates: prebuilt snippets for opening bridges with incident id, service, and initial runbook step.
- CI validation: add a runbook-linter step in docs CI to ensure required fields exist and that runbook links are valid.

KPIs for Stability Sprint success:
- 20% reduction in mean time to mitigate (MTTM) over two sprints.
- 100% runbook coverage for top-20 services by traffic post-sprint.
- Reduce noisy pager incidence by 30% in next 90 days.
