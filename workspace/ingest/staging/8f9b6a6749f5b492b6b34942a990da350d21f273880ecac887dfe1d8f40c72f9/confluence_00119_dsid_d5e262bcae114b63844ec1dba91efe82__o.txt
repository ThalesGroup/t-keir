On-call Skill Lattice, Game Lab, and Customer Communication Playbook

Summary
-------
This playbook codifies a skill-lattice approach to on-call staffing, a reusable game-lab for simulation-driven readiness, and standardized customer/status-page communication templates. It is intended for SREs, on-call engineers, incident commanders, and Customer Success (CS) staff who coordinate external messages.

Scope
-----
- Applies to all production-facing services (hosted API, dedicated clusters, private deployments) operated by Redwood Inference.
- Focus areas: incident detection->triage->restoration, targeted runbooks (KV cache, throttling/backpressure, model-quality regressions), on-call rotation competency matrix, game-day lab recipes, and external communication templates.

Objectives
----------
1. Reduce mean time to acknowledgement (MTA) to < 90s for P0s.
2. Reduce mean time to restore (MTTR) for common degradations (cache eviction bursts, throttling) by 30% versus baseline.
3. Ensure uniform external communications with clear escalation thresholds and message templates.
4. Grow on-call proficiency via a structured skill-lattice and quarterly game-labs.

Roles & Responsibilities
------------------------
- Service Owner: accountable for runbook correctness and post-incident action items.
- On-call Primary (Tier 1): first responder; performs acknowledgement, triage, low-risk mitigations.
- On-call Secondary (Tier 2): deeper diagnostic help; owns temporary mitigations and patch rollouts.
- SRE Bridge / Incident Commander (IC): coordinates cross-team activity, customer comms, and war-room.
- Customer Success Liaison: drafts and approves customer-facing notices; coordinates sensitive escalations.
- Observability Owner: verifies signals/alerts and tunes thresholds post-incident.

Incident Lifecycle (standardized)
--------------------------------
1) Detect & Alert
  - Sources: Prometheus/Alertmanager, traces (Jaeger), runtime logs (ELK), synthetic checks, customer-reported tickets.
2) Acknowledge (within 90s for P0)
  - Primary acknowledges pager, posts initial signal in incident Slack channel: "ACK: <signal> — investigating (Primary: @user)."
3) Triage (10m objective)
  - Confirm scope, affected regions, customer impact, SLO breach status.
4) Contain & Mitigate
  - Execute runbook relevant to signal. If unknown, escalate to Secondary.
5) Restore & Validate
  - Restore service to acceptable SLO; run synthetic verification and smoke tests.
6) Communicate
  - Status page update cadence (see templates). Internal incident updates every 30 minutes until stable.
7) Postmortem & Actions
  - Draft within 72 hours. Action items assigned and tracked to completion.

Escalation Matrix (timers)
-------------------------
- P0 (service down or customer-impacting > 50%): Primary -> escalate to Secondary after 10 min -> IC after 20 min -> Exec alert after 60 min.
- P1 (degraded SLOs, partial impact): Primary -> Secondary after 20 min -> IC after 60 min.
- Pager silence: if no ack in 90s, automated escalation to secondary pager and SMS.

Standard Runbooks (selected)
---------------------------
Runbooks below are canonical templates; always follow the step-by-step checklist and attach evidence to the incident. The first three are prioritized for game-lab exercises.

1) KV-cache partial eviction (typical failure in model-serving stacks)
- Detection signals: sustained 99th latency spike on token-generation endpoints, rising L2 cache miss rate, backend GPU utilization drop with bursty CPU usage.
- SLO thresholds: 99th latency > 1.5s (P1), > 3.0s (P0) for > 5 minutes.
- Runbook (steps):
  a) Acknowledge and collect trace IDs for several failing requests.
  b) Check cache hit/miss dashboards: timeframe + host distribution.
  c) Identify noisy neighbors through per-host eviction counters.
  d) If single host: cordon host from load balancer; restart cache process; monitor hit-rate recovery.
  e) If cluster-wide eviction: enable automatic fallback to cold-start model variant or scale cache tier (execute autoscale runbook).
  f) Validate with synthetic load and customer requests.
  g) Post-incident: update cache sizing and eviction policy in config (see Appendix A).

2) API throttled/backpressure cascade
- Detection: 429 rate increases, queue depth alerts, upstream overloaded errors.
- Runbook (summary):
  a) Identify offending route(s) and client tokens via analytics.
  b) Temporarily raise per-customer quotas for critical customers via admin API, or enable burst pool.
  c) Apply traffic shaping rules: route non-critical traffic to best-effort model variants.
  d) If autoscaler failing: fallback to reserve capacity pool (execute Dedicated-fallback SOP).

3) Model-quality regression (serving different weights / bad outputs)
- Detection: increased eval failure rates, automated quality monitors, customer tickets reporting hallucinations.
- Runbook (summary):
  a) Quarantine suspect model version (route traffic to last-known-good).
  b) Disable recent rollout policy and trigger canary rollback.
  c) Collect prompt/response pairs for regression triage.
  d) Notify Product & Research for root cause and corrective training or prompt-set updates.

Runbook Table (quick reference)
------------------------------
| Symptom | Primary signal | Immediate action | Owner | Escalation |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| KV-cache evictions | 99th latency spike, cache miss rate | Cordon host or scale cache | Service Owner | SRE Bridge (10m) |
| Throttling / 429s | 429 rate > 2% | Apply traffic shaping, raise quota for critical | API Platform | SRE Bridge (20m) |
| Model-quality drop | Eval failure rate + CS tickets | Quarantine version, rollback canary | Model Ops | Product + Research |

On-call Rotation & Skill Lattice
--------------------------------
- Rotation duration: standard 2-weeks primary, 4-weeks secondary backup.
- Skill Lattice levels and expectations:
  - L1 (Onboarded): can acknowledge, follow runbook checklists, escalate properly.
  - L2 (Practitioner): can perform deeper diagnostics, issue temporary mitigations, run post-incident validation.
  - L3 (SRE IC / Service Owner): leads cross-team incidents, owns runbook updates and practice labs.
- Progression: engineers complete a checklist of competencies (list of short tasks and game-lab badges) to be promoted between levels.
- Rotation handover: 15-minute shadow session at start of shift; update handover doc with ongoing MTT* items and experiments.

Game-Lab (quarterly structured simulations)
-----------------------------------------
Purpose: provide hands-on practice that mirrors high-probability failure modes; measure MTTA/MTTR and identify weak points in runbooks and comms.

Quarterly schedule (example)
1) Pre-lab (1 week): identify target service, freeze non-critical deploys, notify observers.
2) Lab day (4 hours): run 3 scenarios (KV-cache eviction, cascading throttles, canary-quality regression). Real traffic replay is recommended using anonymized traces.
3) Hotwash (90 minutes): immediate debrief with metrics, comms review, and action item assignment.
4) Retro (within 7 days): iterate on runbooks and update the skill-lattice checklist.

Game-Lab Scenario Example: KV-cache Eviction Simulation
- Objective: validate detection, runbook steps, and status-page cadence under partial host failure.
- Steps:
  1) Inject increased eviction rate into two hosts in the staging cluster (use staging-only tokens).
  2) Observe alert firing, validate that Primary acknowledges within SLO.
  3) Execute runbook: cordon host, restart cache, monitor hit-rate recovery.
  4) Track MTTA and MTTR, collect logs and traces.
  5) Run customer-communication template in a simulated status page update.

Status Page & Customer Communication Templates
---------------------------------------------
Guiding principle: clarity, timeliness, and single-source-of-truth. Use the status page for public updates; use direct customer notifications for high-touch customers. All messages should be approved by SRE Bridge and CS Liaison before posting.

Template: Initial public notice (Status Page)
Title: Intermittent request latency for text-generation APIs in us-west-2
Body:
- Impact: Some customers may see elevated latency for text-generation endpoints in us-west-2.
- Scope: ~10% of requests between 11:03–ongoing local time. %PLACEHOLDER_CUSTOMERS%
- Mitigation: We are investigating and have routed traffic away from affected hosts.
- Next update: in 30 minutes.

Template: Customer-facing email (for impacted enterprise customers)
Subject: Service disruption affecting text-generation in us-west-2 — interim notice
Body:
Hi <Customer Name>,
We are experiencing elevated latency for text-generation workloads in us-west-2 which may affect <product-feature>. Our SRE team has identified a partial KV-cache eviction event and is executing mitigation steps. We will:
1) Route traffic to healthy hosts
2) Temporarily increase quota for critical workloads if needed
3) Provide hourly updates until resolved
Estimated impact: requests may experience 2–6s additional latency. We will follow up with a full post-incident report.
If you would like real-time updates in a bridge, reply to this message and we will invite you.
Regards,
Maya Alvarez (on behalf of Redwood SRE)

Internal cadence for updates
- P0: every 15 minutes until stable, then 30-minute cadence for 2 hours, then hourly.
- P1: every 30 minutes until stable.

Postmortem & Learning Review
---------------------------
- Postmortem template: summary, timeline, root cause, detection/mitigation gaps, action items (owner + due date), and impact to customers.
- Timing: draft within 72 hours; public summary within 7 days for customer-impacting incidents.
- Action item tracking: link to Jira epic (label: incident-<YYYYMMDD>-<service>) and require owners to set due dates no later than 30 days.

Measuring Effectiveness
-----------------------
Key metrics collected per incident and per quarterly game-lab:
- MTTA (mean time to acknowledge)
- MTTR (mean time to restore)
- Number of comms updates and average approval latency
- Runbook execution accuracy (did responders follow required steps?)
- Skill-lattice badge completions after labs

Appendix B: CLI snippets
- Cordon host (LB):
  curl -XPOST https://ops-api.redwood.internal/lb/cordon -d '{"host":"host-23"}' -H 'Authorization: Bearer $OPSTOKEN'
- Trigger cache restart on host via runner:
  redwood-runner exec --host host-23 -- service cache restart

Appendix C: Evidence collection checklist
1. Attach traces for 5 representative failed requests (trace IDs).
2. Save screenshot of 5-minute dashboard view showing alert.
3. Export logs (time window + host list) to incident artifact storage.
4. Add links to synthetic test results used for validation.
