Onboard: SLO-first Packaging Playbook

Summary

This playbook describes the SLO-first packaging model for onboarding new developer and mid-market customers to Redwood Inference. It provides a prescriptive set of packaging defaults, onboarding steps, telemetry and rollout guidance designed to reduce cost uncertainty while guaranteeing customer-facing latency/availability objectives. Use this when negotiating early SLAs, designing onboarding flows, or creating customer-specific packaging addenda.

Goals

- Make service-level objectives the primary axis for packaging and entitlements.
- Reduce cost uncertainty by exposing clear tradeoffs between SLO tiers and cost levers (batching, KV caching, quantization).
- Provide a repeatable onboarding checklist that product, solutions, and customer success can follow for 48–72 hour time-to-first-SLO (customer-visible).
- Ensure safe rollouts with telemetry, rollback triggers, and canary guardrails.

Audience

Product managers, solutions engineers, customer success, account executives negotiating Dedicated/Private contracts, and developer onboarding engineers.

Scope and non-goals

In scope:
- Hosted and Dedicated onboarding paths.
- Default packaging configurations and SLO mappings.
- Onboarding runbook and telemetry requirements.
- Pricing buckets tied to SLOs and predictable capacity commits.

Out of scope:
- Detailed legal contract language (finance/legal will publish templates).
- Deep runtime kernel tuning steps (see eng-serving-runtime docs).

SLO-first packaging model overview

We define three canonical SLO tiers customers can select during onboarding. Each tier maps to a set of default runtime and packaging configurations, and a cost multiplier relative to baseline usage pricing. Teams can override defaults for enterprise customers with Dedicated or Private deployments.

Table: Canonical SLO tiers

| Tier name | Availability SLO | P95 latency target (text gen) | Default runtime profile | Typical customer size | Notes |
|-----------|------------------:|-----------------------------:|------------------------|---------------------|------:|
| Bronze    | 99.5%             | 800 ms                      | shared-hosted, opportunistic batching | small dev teams, PoC | cost-first, longer coldstart windows |
| Silver    | 99.9%             | 350 ms                      | hosted with prefix-KV cache, medium batching | early production apps | balanced cost/latency |
| Gold      | 99.99%            | 120 ms                      | Dedicated warm capacity, reserved KV, strict batching caps | mission-critical, scale | SLA-backed availability and latency |

Defaults and packaging primitives

Each tier exposes configurable primitives that materially impact unit economics. Default values for each tier are documented in the table below. These defaults are opinionated; use engineering review for bespoke allocations.

Table: Default primitives by tier

| Primitive | Bronze | Silver | Gold |
|-----------|--------|--------|------|
| Max batch size | 64 | 32 | 16 |
| Prefix KV cache TTL | 10m | 60m | 6h |
| Quantization profile | fp16 | int8-mixed | int8+llm-quant |
| Warm capacity percent | 0% | 30% | 80% |
| Reserved concurrency | burst | 200 r/s | 2k r/s |

Pricing model and packaging SKUs

High-level pricing approach: map expected steady-state consumption to an SLO SKU. Each SKU consists of: base monthly fee (for dedicated/warm guarantees), per-token variable price, and an SLO credit that limits behavior under overload. Example SKU (Silver):

- base_fee_monthly: $1,200 (stringified in contract)
- per_token_rate: $0.0006 per token
- on_slo_mitigation: traffic reprioritization + adaptive batching

Note: Concrete USD values are negotiation starting points — product/pricing must sign off on any discounting above 15%. Finance owns final published SKUs.

Developer onboarding checklist (48–72 hour target)

1. Initial scoping call (owner: solutions engineering).
   - Confirm target SLO tier, expected peak qps, concurrency pattern, and security requirements.
   - Capture model family and sequence length distribution.
2. Provisioning (owner: infra ops/product).
   - For Hosted: allocate namespace, default runtime profile per chosen tier.
   - For Dedicated: reserve GPU pool and warm capacity; create tenant routing policy; configure KMS if requested.
3. Baseline traffic replay and smoke tests (owner: onboarding eng).
   - Run synthetic traffic generator with representative prompts (use playbook prompts-store).
   - Verify p95 latency and error budget burn over 1–4 hours.
4. Telemetry and tracing hooks (owner: observability/product).
   - Ensure tenant export of token-level telemetry, p95/p99 latency metrics, and request traces to customer's telemetry workspace (if configured).
5. Cost forecast and packaging sign-off (owner: solutions/product).
   - Use the cost simulator (CLI example below) to estimate 30/60/90 day spend given projected traffic.
6. Go-live and canary (owner: SRE/onboarding eng).
   - Canary 5% traffic for 2 hours with active rollback monitors.

Example: cost simulator CLI snippet

# CLI: simulate cost for 10M tokens/month at Silver defaults
redwood-cost-sim --tokens 10000000 --tier silver --sequence-avg 128 --output report.json

Output (example JSON):
{"scenario":"silver-default-10m","monthly_cost_usd":"7200","base_fee":"1200","variable":"6000"}

Telemetry, observability and rollback triggers

Required telemetry exports for onboarding must be configured before removing canary protection. At minimum:

- token_count (per request), request_latency_ms (p50/p95/p99), request_error_count, kv_cache_hit_rate, batch_size_distribution, warm_capacity_utilization.
- SLO burn rate alert: when error budget consumption > 10% per 10-minute window for > 2 windows, trigger automated rollback to previous runtime profile.
- Cost-exposure alert: projected monthly cost increase > 15% vs signed forecast for sustained 60 minutes.

Table: Alert thresholds (recommended)

| Alert | Threshold | Action owner | Automated action |
|-------|----------:|--------------|------------------|
| P95 latency > target + 20% | sustained 5m | SRE | throttle and scale-up signal |
| Error rate > 0.5% | sustained 10m | Oncall | fallback to lower fidelity variant |
| KV hit rate < 10% | 30m window | Runtime eng | suggest cache-warm job |
| Cost drift > 15% projection | 60m | Customer success | send usage hold and flag to AE |

Rollout phases and gating

Phase 0 — Discovery: collect traffic patterns, negotiate tier.
Phase 1 — Provisioned canary: 5% traffic routed for 2–4 hours, full telemetry.
Phase 2 — Gradual ramp: 20% for 12 hours, then 100% if stable.
Phase 3 — Harden + review: 72-hour post-go-live review with performance and cost readouts.

Gates before moving from Phase 1 to Phase 2:
- P95 and error budget within 125% of target for duration of canary.
- No more than 5% cost overshoot vs forecast during canary.

Operational notes and known tradeoffs

- Stronger SLOs (Gold) typically increase unit cost by 2–4x for the same token volume due to warm capacity and reduced batching. Communicate this clearly during pricing conversations.
- For customer workloads dominated by long sequences (avg sequence > 1024 tokens), Gold may require model variant pinning or quantization profile adjustments.
- On Dedicated, reserved KV cache TTLs should be conservatively lowered before any mass migration to avoid cache stampedes.

KPIs and success metrics

- Time to first customer-visible SLO (target <= 72 hours).
- Fraction of onboards that hit forecasted cost within ±15% at 30 days (target >= 80%).
- Post-onboard SLO compliance at 90 days (fraction of customers meeting their tier's SLOs, target >= 95%).

FAQ

Q: Can a customer change tiers mid-cycle?
A: Yes. Tier changes are permitted at the start of the next billing cycle unless otherwise negotiated. Mid-cycle emergency downgrades may be applied with prorated credits.

Q: What happens if SLOs are violated due to third-party model regressions?
A: We route to fallback variants and open an incident. The SLO review will distinguish between platform and model-level causes; customer credits follow the contractual SLA process.
