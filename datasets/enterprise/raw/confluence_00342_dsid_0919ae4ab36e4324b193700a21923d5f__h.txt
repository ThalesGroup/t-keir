Hybrid deployment SLO alignment and capacity contracts

Summary
This page documents the SLO alignment, capacity translation, and operational contract patterns for services that run across Redwood Inference's three deployment modes: Hosted API (shared), Dedicated (reserved), and Private (customer-controlled). It defines how SRE and Product agree SLOs, how error budgets are mapped across modes, and the automated/runbook steps for emergency capacity reallocation, graceful demotion, and post-incident reconciliation.

Goals
- Provide a repeatable, auditable process to translate product-level SLOs to capacity commitments across Hosted, Dedicated, and Private deployments.
- Minimize customer-visible impact during capacity events via automated routing and demotion policies.
- Ensure clear ownership, decision gates, and evidence artifacts for post-incident reconciliation and billing credits.

Scope and applicability
- Applies to all model-serving services and tenant workloads that may run in more than one deployment mode concurrently or move between modes during rollout.
- Not intended for purely internal tooling services (see internal-slo-exceptions).
- Covers: SLO contract templates, capacity translation heuristics, automated mitigation playbook, manual runbook for complex cases, observability and evidence requirements.

Definitions
- Product SLO: The user-facing SLO agreed with product and customer stakeholders (e.g., 99th percentile P95 latency, 99.9% availability).
- Deployment SLO: Low-level objective the SRE team enforces on a specific deployment mode (e.g., cluster-level P99 latency on Dedicated pool).
- Capacity Contract: A signed/recorded commitment describing headroom, burst allowances, and observable guardrails for a tenant in Dedicated or Hosted.
- Demotion: Gradual fallback from a higher-cost deployment variant (Dedicated/Private) to a lower-cost variant (Hosted or cheaper model) to preserve availability.

High-level workflow (summary)
1) Product and SRE define Product SLO for the feature and target tenants.
2) SRE translates Product SLO to deployment SLOs and produces a Capacity Contract per tenant or tenant-class.
3) Contracts include error-budget partitioning, headroom, burst allowances, and fallback/demotion policies.
4) Runtime enforces routing rules and automated demotion under capacity pressure; SRE executes runbook for capacity reallocation if automation cannot resolve.
5) Post-event: reconcile evidence, credit calculations, and run a blameless retrospective.

Capacity Contract template (text)
- tenant_or_feature: <string>
- product_slo: <metric + target + measurement window>
- deployment_modes: [hosted, dedicated, private]
- primary_mode: <one of deployment_modes>
- headroom_commitment_tokens_per_minute: <numeric>
- burst_allowance_tokens: <numeric> (30-minute window)
- failover_sequence: [primary_mode, secondary_mode, tertiary_mode]
- demotion_thresholds: 
  - sustained_utilization_pct: 85%
  - tail_latency_pct_change: +50% over baseline
- owned_by: <team email or alias>
- provisioning_kyc: <yes/no - for Private deployments>

SLO translation heuristics
- Convert product-level latency SLO to per-deployment percentile objectives by measuring historical delta between modes. Typical heuristic (starting point):
  - If Product SLO is P99 < 500ms for interactive feature:
    - Dedicated cluster: enforce P99 < 450ms (5% tighter)
    - Hosted: enforce P99 < 550ms (10% looser) and require cache/warm-prefix guidance
    - Private: match Product SLO but require customer-run observability hooks
- Availability SLOs: map to accepted error-budget partition (see Error-budget partitioning below).
- Embeddings/Batch workloads: translate token throughput SLO to headroom in tokens/sec and set queue-depth goals.

Error-budget partitioning and ledgering
- For each Capacity Contract we record an error-budget allocation in the SLO ledger: product_error_budget -> {hosted_pct, dedicated_pct, private_pct}. Sum must be 100%.
- Default partition: hostable workloads get 60% Hosted, 30% Dedicated, 10% Private (adjust per contract).
- Ledger entries must include a TTL and an owner; automated tooling will debit ledger on breach incidents and tag evidence artifacts for reconciliation.

Automated mitigation and demotion policy (runtime behavior)
- Trigger signals (any of): sustained utilization above headroom threshold, rapid P99 increase, or loss of bookkeeping telemetry for > 120s.
- Automated routing actions (ordered):
  1) Activate warm-pool instances (if available) in hosting region.
  2) Apply prefix-KV cache warmup for affected tenant.
  3) If after 90s the metric remains degraded, execute demotion sequence defined in Capacity Contract: replace model variant with cheaper quantized variant or route subset of requests to Hosted fallback cluster.
  4) Mark tenant as 'degraded' in the SLO ledger and notify oncall via channel + incident ticket.
- Safeguards: automation will not demote Private deployments without an explicit 'allow_demote' flag in the contract.

Manual runbook: when automation escalates
1) Acknowledge the incident, attach SLO ledger id, and capture the triggering telemetry snapshot (30s window) to evidence bucket.
2) Verify capacity signals: cluster_utilization, queue_depth, CPU/GPU RMIs, and KV cache hit rate.
3) If capacity is insufficient and warm-pool scaling fails, perform targeted tenant soft-eviction using priority weights in the capacity-orchestrator: reduce non-critical tenant concurrency by 30% and monitor.
4) If tenant is Dedicated and the contract allows, negotiate temporary headroom grant from Dedicated pool (follow the dedicated headroom grant checklist below).
5) If redistributing tenants, update routing policy and assert a canary of 5% traffic for 10 minutes before broad shift.
6) If manual demotion to Hosted is required, follow the private-hosted-demote checklist and notify Account/CS for any customer-facing changes.

Dedicated headroom grant checklist
- Confirm owner approval (owner listed on Capacity Contract).
- Record the grant in the SLO ledger with duration and tokens.
- Adjust autoscale target for affected pool.
- Notify billing: create a provisional usage note referencing ledger id.
- Post-event: reconcile temporary grant with product and finance; consider credits.

Observability & evidence requirements
- Every contract and every capacity action must generate an evidence bundle with: time range, impacted tenants, SLO ledger id, autoscale events, routing changes, and pre/post metric snapshots.
- Dashboards required per contract:
  - Contract summary: headroom, burst allowance, demotion sequence, owner.
  - Live telemetry: percentile latencies, token throughput, queue depth, cache hit ratio.
  - Incident timeline widget: automated actions and manual steps.
- Alerts (templated):
  - Contract_utilization_warning (80% of headroom for 5m) -> paging to oncall if sustained > 15m.
  - Tail_latency_growth_alert (P99 increases > threshold) -> notifies product and SRE on same channel.

Post-incident reconciliation and crediting policy
- Trigger: Product SLO breach validated by evidence bundle.
- Steps:
  1) SRE certifies evidence bundle and the ledger entry.
  2) If breach attributable to capacity shortfall, compute credit = min(contractual_credit, proportional to time over threshold * tenant_commitment).
  3) Product managers approve credit; Finance issues credit note.
  4) Runbook owner creates retrospective and schedules remediation tasks.

Operational tooling (how to use)
- Create a Capacity Contract via the CLI:
  ```
  redwood capacity create --tenant my-tenant --product-slo "p99<500ms" --primary_mode dedicated --headroom 2000 --burst 50000 --owner "team-inference@s.redwood.com"
  ```
- Inspect ledger entry:
  ```
  redwood capacity ledger get --ledger-id LGR-2026-00017 --format yaml
  ```
- Run runbook steps (soft-eviction helper):
  ```
  redwood capacity orchestrate --evict --tenant my-tenant --concurrency 30
  ```
- Evidence capture helper (automated):
  ```
  redwood evidence capture --since -5m --tags LGR-2026-00017 --output s3://redwood-evidence/2026/ledger-17/
  ```

Governance and decision gates
- New Capacity Contracts require sign-off from: product manager, SRE owner, and finance (if dedicated cost > $X/month).
- Any change that relaxes demotion safeguards for Private deployments must include security signoff.
- Quarterly review: contracts with repeated demotions (>2 in a quarter) must be reviewed and either re-provisioned or re-priced.

Ownership and escalation matrix
- Primary: inference-platform-sre (pager: inference-sre oncall).
- Secondary: infra-ops (for autoscale and GPU pool changes).
- Product escalation: product-inference (for customer comms and credit decisions).
- Finance contact: billing-ops.

Example: mapping a conversational product SLO to contracts (walkthrough)
- Product SLO: “Conversational API P99 < 300ms, 99.5% availability monthly”
- Translation:
  - Dedicated: P99 < 290ms, headroom 3k tokens/min, burst 60k tokens 30m window, failover to Hosted with quantized model variant.
  - Hosted: P99 < 310ms, headroom 8k tokens/min shared, burst 120k tokens.
  - Private: match Product SLO; require trace ingestion and proof-of-change SLA.
- Onboard steps: sign contract, configure routing rules, run synthetic canary, and validate evidence capture.

Appendix: quick-checklists
- New contract quick-check:
  1) Attach product SLO and baseline telemetry (30d).
  2) Validate autoscale and warm-pool presence.
  3) Set demotion flags and private demote consent.
  4) Create ledger entry and dashboard.

- Incident quick-check for oncall:
  1) Capture evidence bundle immediately.
  2) Verify contract headroom and ledger id.
  3) Execute automation steps; if automation fails, follow manual runbook.
  4) Mark tenant degraded and file incident ticket referencing ledger id.
