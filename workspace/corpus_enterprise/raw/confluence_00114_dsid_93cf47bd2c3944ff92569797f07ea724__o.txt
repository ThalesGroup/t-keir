oncall-rotation-onboarding-and-slo-ownership-runbook-2024

Overview:
This runbook describes the standard onboarding flow for engineers joining the SRE on-call rotation and the concrete handoff for SLO ownership and error-budget stewardship. It is written for new rotation members, rotation leads, and managers who need a repeatable checklist to verify readiness to carry SLO responsibilities in production.

Purpose:
- Ensure consistent knowledge transfer for SREs joining or re-joining rotation.
- Reduce time-to-first-independent-incident-handling while protecting customer-facing SLOs.
- Establish a clear sign-off process for SLO ownership and capacity remediation privileges.

Scope:
Applies to: core inference platform services (api-gateway, runtime-router, kv-cache, model-host-pool) and SREs who will be primary or secondary on-call. Does NOT replace service-specific runbooks — those remain authoritative for service-level troubleshooting.

Definitions:
- SLO owner: rotation engineer accountable for monitoring, first-response and initiating mitigation for a target SLO.
- Error budget: rolling 30-day percentage (or per-SLO window) of allowable user-impacting failures.

Before you start (prerequisites):
- GitHub access: read/write to infra/playbooks repo.
- Confluence access to eng-sre space and product SLO matrix.
- PagerDuty and Slack access; add yourself to #sre-oncall and service channels.
- Complete the mandatory SRE training module: "SLO Fundamentals and Incident Triage" in LMS.
- Local dev cluster credentials and sandbox project (for safe drill exercises).

Onboarding checklist (step-by-step):
1) Orientation (Day 0–1)
   - Read the SLO matrix: open the SLO dashboard and export the list for your service(s). Verify thresholds for: p50, p95, p99 latency, availability, and correctness metrics used by product.
   - Join recurring oncall handover: attend next rotation change and shadow the outgoing oncall.

2) Tooling and alerts (Day 1–3)
   - Subscribe to alerts: PagerDuty, OpsGenie (fallback), and Slack alert bridge.
   - Verify alert routing: confirm that critical SLO alerts escalate to your primary channel and that low-severity alerts are rate-limited.
   - Walkthrough the telemetry queries:
     - Example PromQL (tail-latency):
       sum(rate(request_duration_seconds_bucket{job=\"runtime-router\",le=\\\"0.5\\\"}[5m])) / sum(rate(requests_total{job=\"runtime-router\"}[5m]))
     - Example traces: open the trace link pattern: https://jaeger.company.local/trace/{{trace_id}}

3) Read and rehearse runbooks (Day 2–4)
   - Read core runbooks: SLO rollback, capacity hedge, model-hotpatch, and circuit-breaker reset.
   - Run a table-top drill: simulate a p95 latency spike caused by a KV cache miss storm. Walk through mitigation steps (enable short-circuit cache warm, apply tiered throttles). Document decisions.

4) Capacity and provisioning basics (Day 3–6)
   - Review capacity model: headroom policy, burst pools, and preemption rules for spot instances.
   - Review tenant fair-share rules: how requests are prioritized across Dedicated and Hosted customers.
   - Practice issuing a safe scale action: CLI example (dry-run):
     rfn-capacity scale --cluster runtime-prod --pool inference-pool --count 4 --reason "oncall-drill" --dry-run

5) Error budget stewardship (Day 4–7)
   - Understand error budget windows and burn-rate alarms. Verify the dashboard shows the rolling window used by the SLO (30d by default).
   - Confirm the automated responses enabled for the service: throttling, model-fallback, cross-region reroute.
   - Practice executing an emergency fallback:
     - Step: trigger model fallback to smaller quantized variant (canary first)
     - Command (internal API):
       curl -X POST -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" "https://console.internal/api/v1/rollouts/fallback" -d '{"service":"runtime-router","action":"fallback-quantized","target":"model-v2-q4"}'

6) Incident communication and post-incident responsibility (Day 4–8)
   - Run the incident comms template: copy and post to #incident-comm when practicing. Include: impact, scope, mitigation, ETA.
   - Ownership: the SLO owner must create the post-incident ticket under ENG-SRE > incident-review and ensure attachments: timeline, telemetry snapshots, and decision log.

Acceptance criteria (sign-off):
- Completed checklist items 1–6 and provided evidence (screenshots / links) in the onboarding folder.
- Passed a recorded tabletop drill assessed by rotation lead.
- Demonstrated ability to run three recovery actions in the sandbox (scale, fallback, throttle).
- Rotation lead signs off in Confluence comment and updates rotation roster.

Escalation matrix (who to call):
- Immediate (pager): oncall-sre-primary -> oncall-sre-secondary -> eng-sre-manager.
- For capacity quorums (if >2-hour impact): capacity-ops@redwood.com and cloud-accounts oncall.

Runbook snippets and templates:
- Incident start checklist (first 10 minutes):
  1. Acknowledge alert in PagerDuty.
  2. Post initial impact message (template):
     - Subject: [INC][service] brief title
     - Body: one-line impact, estimated affected tenants, user-visible effect, immediate mitigation in progress.
  3. Attach top-3 telemetry graphs: p95 latency, request rate, error-rate (per-tenant).

- Quick mitigation decision matrix (summary):
  - If error-rate spike and p95 increased -> look for traffic shift or model regression.
  - If traffic surge without elevated error-rate -> provision temporary capacity or enable burst pool.
  - If sustained burn-rate > 2x baseline for 60m -> initiate throttling and notify product.

Ownership and responsibilities table:
| Role | Responsibility | OK to action without manager approval |
|-----|---------------|---------------------------------------|
| SLO owner (oncall) | Triage, execute mitigations, create incident ticket | Yes (scale, fallback, short-term throttles) |
| Rotation lead | Sign-off postmortem, progressive escalations | No (policy changes are escalated) |
| Capacity oncall | Provisioning, permanent capacity changes | No (requires capacity committee approval) |

SLO-specific quick references (examples):
- API-Gateway availability SLO: 99.95% (30d rolling), error budget 0.05%.
- Runtime-router latency SLO: p95 < 200ms for inference path, p99 < 600ms (window: 7d).
- Embeddings service correctness SLO: 98% similarity threshold pass rate (30d).

Training plan and milestones (first 90 days):
- Week 1–2: Complete checklist above and shadow two full rotations.
- Week 3–4: Run two live drills and participate in a capacity rehearsal.
- Month 1–2: Co-own one minor incident under supervision and produce post-incident notes.
- Month 3: Independently manage an escalation and be eligible for rotation lead consideration.

Appendix B — Post-oncall checklist for outgoing engineer (handover):
1. Document unresolved actions with owners.
2. Export and link current top telemetry graphs.
3. Confirm any temporary mitigations remain or are part of a long-term plan.
4. Hand off any running audits or tickets to next rotation.

Notes and rationale:
- We require a formal sign-off to reduce silent SLO ownership drift. This runbook emphasizes evidence-based progression (drills, recorded actions) rather than ad-hoc approval.
