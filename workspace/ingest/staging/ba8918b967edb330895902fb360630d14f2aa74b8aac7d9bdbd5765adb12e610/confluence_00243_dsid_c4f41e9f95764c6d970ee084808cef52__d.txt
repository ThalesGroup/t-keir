Developer consumption sizing and packaging playbook

Overview
--------
This playbook describes recommended approaches to size consumption estimates and package offerings for developer-focused customers using Redwood Inference. It translates observed developer behavior, common integration patterns, and cost/SLO tradeoffs into actionable pricing and packaging guidance for product managers and pricing analysts.

Goals
- Reduce ambiguity for self-serve customers when selecting a package.
- Align packaging units with developer mental models (requests, tokens, embeddings, features).
- Provide reproducible hand-off artifacts for finance and sales engineering.

Intended audience
- Product managers (pricing/packaging)
- Growth and go-to-market engineers
- Solutions engineering and customer success
- Finance and billing engineers

When to use this playbook
- New feature launches (e.g., streaming, fidelity tiers) where consumption patterns are unknown.
- Designing a self-serve bundle or a trial credit allocation.
- Responding to recurring support requests about how to estimate bills.

Key concepts and units
- Token unit: per 1,000 output tokens normalized to a baseline model (e.g., rdx-base-v1).
- Request unit: a single API call for generation/chat, regardless of streaming vs non-streaming; large responses are token-weighted.
- Embedding unit: per 1,024 vector-ops (rounded up).
- Feature flags: premium features (function calling, structured outputs, high-fidelity variants) are metered separately.

High-level sizing flow (quick):
1) Select representative workload (sample prompts, average length, concurrency).
2) Measure per-request token and latency distribution with the developer sandbox.
3) Map to candidate package units (token buckets, request bursts, embedding quotas).
4) Simulate monthly cost under three scenarios: conservative, expected, aggressive.
5) Pick price points and guardrails (rate limits, overage rates, feature throttles).

Detailed steps and templates
----------------------------
1) Choose workload archetype
- Reference archetypes: conversational-agent, document-search, summarization-batch, realtime-autocomplete.
- Template to capture workload:
  - Example payloads (3–5 representative prompts)
  - Desired P95 latency target and required fidelity (e.g., hallucination tolerance)
  - Expected concurrency (sessions per minute)

2) Local measurement (developer sandbox)
- Use Redwood local sandbox or hosted free tier to run the workload for 10k synthetic requests.
- Capture: tokens_in, tokens_out, total_tokens, API_time_ms, streaming_fraction.
- CLI example:
  rdx-bench --model rdx-base-v1 --prompt-file ./sample-prompts.json --requests 10000 --concurrency 16 --out telemetry.json
- Required telemetry columns: request_id, start_ts, end_ts, prompt_tokens, completion_tokens, status_code, variant_used

3) Normalize to monthly consumption
- Convert per-request metrics to monthly:
  monthly_tokens = average_total_tokens * projected_monthly_requests
  monthly_requests = projected_monthly_requests
- Include 95th-percentile burst multiplier (default 2x) to provision burst allowance.

4) Pricing candidate generation
- Packaging options to consider (choose 1–2 primary):
  A. Token-bucket subscription + overage per 1k tokens.
  B. Request-bundle (N requests/month) + token soft-cap with throttles.
  C. Feature add-ons (embeddings, streaming, high-fidelity variants) priced per-unit.

5) Simulate cost scenarios (table)
- Create a three-scenario table (samples provided below).

| Scenario | Monthly requests | Avg total tokens/request | Monthly tokens | Recommended package | Expected bill (USD) | Notes |
|---------:|-----------------:|------------------------:|---------------:|---------------------|-------------------:|-------|
| Conservative | 10,000 | 120 | 1,200,000 | Starter: 1.5M tokens @ $5 per 1k = +embeddings 200K @ $0.20 | $6,400 | good for small teams |
| Expected | 100,000 | 240 | 24,000,000 | Growth: 25M tokens + 50k embeddings + streaming flag | $96,000 | requires burst allowance |
| Aggressive | 500,000 | 300 | 150,000,000 | Enterprise: custom reserved quota, dedicated latency contract | $480,000 | recommend Dedicated evaluation |
Note: the dollar figures above are illustrative. Replace with current list prices and reserved discount schedules from finance table (link below).

Guardrails and feature boundaries
- Rate limits: set per-tenant RPS and per-route token rate. Typical defaults: 10 RPS, 5k tokens/s peak for Growth bundle.
- Overage behavior: auto-throttle at soft-cap + email warnings at 80% consumption; overage charging at 1.5x list per-1k token rate.
- Feature gating: streaming and function-calling are off in Starter; available as add-ons for Growth; included in Enterprise.

Billing & metering artifacts (required deliverables)
- Billing spec: CSV row format submitted to billing pipeline with fields: customer_id, billing_period_start, billing_period_end, unit_type, units_consumed, list_price_per_unit, discount_code, net_amount.
- Metering window: UTC monthly (first-of-month to last-of-month).
- Audit log: keep per-request metering for 12 months for disputes.

Packaging playbook decisions (decision matrix)
- Use this matrix to pick packaging approach quickly:
  - Developer-first UX: prefer token-buckets with small starter buckets and clear overage rates.
  - High latency-sensitive workloads: emphasize request-bundle or reserved throughput (Dedicated).
  - Embedding-heavy customers: quantify vector-ops, offer embedding top-ups.

Operational runbook for new package launch
1) Phase 0 — Experiment (2 weeks)
   - Create lab tenant with telemetry capture and synthetic traffic.
   - Publish internal pricing teaser and update sandbox UI.
2) Phase 1 — Soft-launch to selected orgs (4 weeks)
   - Invite 10–15 beta orgs. Monitor consumption, errors, and refunds.
   - Weekly sync with CS and Sales Engineering.
3) Phase 2 — Public launch
   - Enable packaging in Console, update pricing pages, and send email campaigns.
   - 24/7 on-call for first 72 hours for consumption disputes.

Migration path recommendations
- From free-tier -> Starter: auto-suggest when 7-day consumption > 100k tokens; offer trial credit equal to 20% of projected first month.
- From Starter -> Growth: recommend when sustained 30-day average > 60% of bucket for 10 days.
- From Growth -> Enterprise: engagement trigger when expected monthly bill > $50k or latency SLO violations > 2 events/month.

Telemetry & KPIs to track post-launch
- Conversion funnel: sandbox -> paid trial -> paid customer.
- ARPU by archetype (conversational vs batch).
- Churn by overage frequency.
- Disputes per 10k billing events.
- SLO compliance: percentage of requests meeting P95 latency objective per-plan.
