commercialization-playbook-optimize-conductor-fidelity-addons-2029-02-09

Overview:

Purpose:
This playbook describes the commercialization strategy, product packaging, pricing mechanics, and launch operations for Redwood Optimize 'Conductor' and its associated Fidelity Add‑Ons. It is intended for product managers, sales engineering, pricing, and GTM teams coordinating the cross-functional launch and early commercial expansion.

Scope:
- Redwood Optimize Conductor: orchestration and policy engine that executes model selection, batching, and cache coordination across multi-variant deployments.
- Fidelity Add‑Ons: per-tenant optional bundles that trade unit-cost for output fidelity and latency guarantees (e.g., Fidelity Shield, Deterministic Routing, Hot-KV Retention).

Background:
Redwood Optimize has matured from an internal optimizer into a modular commercial product. Conductor is the central orchestration component customers adopt to achieve cross-model cost targets and fidelity SLOs. Early adopters requested licensed fidelity controls (to lock outputs, pin KV caches, or preemptively allocate reserved micro-burst capacity). This playbook turns those capabilities into GTM-ready SKUs and outlines rollout controls to balance revenue with operational risk.

Objectives:
1. Launch Conductor with a clear baseline SKU and three additive Fidelity Add‑Ons that are easy to sell and instrument.
2. Provide deterministic billing primitives so finance and customer success can reconcile cost savings vs charges.
3. Define rollout guardrails (canary segments, quota limits, automatic rollback criteria).
4. Establish success metrics and playbooks for onboarding, escalations, and migrations.

Target customer profiles:
- AI-native startups that require predictable per-route fidelity for PLG growth.
- Mid-market teams optimizing inference spend across dozens of model variants.
- Enterprises with contractual fidelity and latency guarantees for regulated workflows.

Value propositions (by persona):
- Engineering: single API to express fidelity policies with runtime enforcement, reduces fragmentation of in-house schedulers.
- Platform/Infra: lowers average cost per token via cross-model KV reuse and safe batching while enabling tiered SLOs.
- Finance/GTM: productized add‑ons that capture value from customers who trade cost for reliability.

Pricing models and examples:
- Conductor baseline: charged as fixed monthly tenant product fee plus a small per-route decision token charge (0.1 cents per policy decision).
- Fidelity Shield: charged as usage multiplier (e.g., +15% to tokens billed under shielded routes) with a monthly cap. Optionally sold as a firm per-route SLA price for enterprise customers.
- Deterministic Routing: sold as micro‑SKU per-request overlay (e.g., $0.002 per routed request), measured on routed count.
- Hot-KV: charged as tiered GB-month (0–50GB = $X/GB, 50–250GB = $Y/GB) plus pre-warm operations fee for bursts.

Packaging guidance and recommended bundles:
- Starter Optimize: Conductor included with limited policy rate (5k decisions/day), no add‑ons. Ideal for trials and PLG users.
- Scale Bundle: Conductor + Fidelity Shield (discounted when purchased together), includes 100k policy decisions/day and 5GB Hot-KV.
- Enterprise Fidelity: Custom pricing with deterministic routing SLAs, reserved KV capacity, and a seat for SRE integration. Includes implementation support and 6-month usage refund SLA for failing SLOs.

Contract & legal notes (clauses to include in the exhibit):
1) Fidelity SLA definitions: define measurement method, sampling windows (30m), and remediation credits (pro-rated product credit).
2) Data residency on Hot-KV: specify whether KV storage is tenant-isolated and encryption at-rest policy.
3) Capacity overrun: automatic throttling vs paid pre-warm option.
4) Termination and migration assistance: outline export format for cached KV prefixes and policy definitions.

Operational rollout and guardrails:
1. Staged rollout phases:
   a. Alpha (internal + 2 customers): manual enablement, close SRE monitoring, 24/7 on-call for first 2 weeks.
   b. Beta (selected customers, <20 tenants): feature-flagged in console, billing in sandbox only, success rep onboarding.
   c. GA (public purchase): backfill documentation, default quotas applied, support SLAs live.
2. Canary segmentation rules:
   - Start with non-critical routes that account for <5% of tenant token volume.
   - Disable add-ons automatically if error rate delta > 0.5% or average latency increase > 60ms sustained over 10 minutes.
3. Automated rollback triggers (runtime):
   - Policy decision runtime exceptions > 0.1% across requests for 5 consecutive minutes.
   - Memory pressure on KV nodes > 75% for > 5 minutes.
   - Billing meterfeed anomalies (negative credits or >200% spike) trips a hold on add-on billing pipeline.

Onboarding playbook (step-by-step):
1. Pre-sales: run the Conductor readiness assessment (see Appendix A). Capture route shapes, expected qps, model variants.
2. Sales engineering: configure a sandbox tenant, pre-seed Hot-KV prefixes for sample workflows, and run the fidelity ramp test.
3. Customer success: run a 3-week migration plan:
   - Week 0: training and policy authoring workshop
   - Week 1: alpha traffic on non-critical routes
   - Week 2: scale to 25% route volume and validate metrics
   - Week 3: full cutover and closeout checklist
4. Finance: verify billing metering events in staging and confirm invoice mapping (SKU IDs, units).
5. Post-launch: 30/60/90 day QBR cadence to confirm ROI and potential upsell to Enterprise Fidelity.

Monitoring, observability, and telemetry contracts:
- Minimum telemetry emitted per policy decision: decision_id, timestamp, route_id, policy_version, decision_latency_ms, model_selected, kv_hit (boolean), tenant_id.
- Cost dashboards: tiles for cost delta vs baseline, per-route fidelity charge accumulation, Hot-KV usage by GB.
- Alerting thresholds (suggested):
  - fidelity-policy-latency > 200ms (P3)
  - kv-evictions-per-minute > 5 (P2)
  - deterministic-routing-drop-rate > 0.1% (P1)

Metrics of success (KPIs):
- Adoption: percent of Optimize customers purchasing ≥1 Fidelity Add‑On in first 90 days. Target: 18%.
- ARR uplift: incremental ARR from Conductor + Add‑Ons within 12 months. Target: $4M.
- Cost delta: average net cost savings customers realize vs baseline serving. Target: >=15% for Scale Bundle customers.
- Operational stability: <2 rollbacks per quarter across all customers.

GTM sequencing and collateral:
- Phase 1 collateral (Beta): technical whitepaper, decision sample code, SRE runbook, and feature video.
- Phase 2 collateral (GA): pricing sheet, 1-pager for sales, prepared contract exhibits, training for AE/SE teams.
- Enablement: self-serve quickstart (console wizard to enable Conductor and 14-day Fidelity trial), sales playbook for enterprise negotiation play.

Escalation and incident playbooks (high level):
- For fidelity regressions (customer-reported quality drop): immediate ingress to 'fidelity-incident' Slack channel. Triage steps: gather decision samples, roll back to previous policy_version, open a priority incident with SRE.
- For billing anomalies: freeze add-on billing for affected tenant, notify Finance and CS, run detailed meter reconciliation.

Rollback and migration considerations:
- All policy changes are versioned. Customers can request a rollback to the previous policy_version; retention of decision logs for 90 days is mandatory for forensic analysis.
- KV export format: newline-delimited JSON of prefix -> serialized-value, with a provided CLI (redwood-kv export --tenant <id> --prefix <p>). Include rate limits on exports (max 50MB/min).

Frequently asked questions (FAQ):
Q: Can customers trial Fidelity Add‑Ons without immediate billing?
A: Yes — we offer a 14-day trial for Fidelity Shield and Deterministic Routing for up to 5k routed requests. Finance will not invoice trials and usage is metered only for internal validation.

Q: How do we measure fidelity for SLA disputes?
A: We use a deterministic rerun sampling methodology (sample size 1k reruns over a 30m window) comparing normalized output similarity. Exhibit defines threshold and sampling frequency.

Q: What are typical migration risks?
A: Hot-KV scaling, unexpected policy decision latency due to large policy trees, and billing mismatches. Follow the onboarding playbook and staged rollouts to mitigate.
